![A living hippopotamus stands at the shallow edge of a pale concrete zoo pool, head lowered toward the wet ledge, viewed through a window with dark mullions, steel handrails curving down to the water and a chain-link fence with green foliage behind, under flat overcast daylight.](/images/stories/fiona-the-hippo-dies-cincinnati-zoo.webp)

Fiona, the nine-year-old Nile hippopotamus whose premature birth at the Cincinnati Zoo in 2017 drew a global following, died Thursday after veterinarians failed to remove a knitted hat she had swallowed, the zoo announced.

A child at the zoo had thrown the cap into the air; it struck a ceiling fan and fell through an opening into her enclosure, the zoo said, as the Associated Press reported. Fiona ate it and showed no symptoms at first. Days later her appetite failed, and keepers fed her extra in the hope the hat would pass through her system. She spent about eleven days under round-the-clock supervision and medical care.

The treatment grew more inventive as it went on. Veterinarians sedated her twice and passed a scope nearly nine feet long through her rectum to view her intestines; an ultrasound on Tuesday showed the hat had not moved. The team borrowed a vibration plate from a horse farm and had her stand on it several times a day, hoping the shaking would push the object along her digestive tract. She received enemas and medication for pain and nausea. Keepers offered her the smell of coffee, to which she responded with grunts and twitching ears.

Surgery was ruled out because of her size. Fiona weighed in the region of 2,500 pounds, and the zoo said operations on full-grown hippos carried prohibitive risk. "It is with extreme sadness that we share with you the devastating news that Fiona did not make it after multiple attempts from our amazing vets and keeper team to help her through what ended up being a medical emergency," the zoo said in a statement. "Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome."

Fiona was born six weeks premature in January 2017, weighing 29 pounds, then the lowest recorded birth weight for her species. She was the first Nile hippopotamus born at the zoo in seventy-five years. Too weak to stand or nurse, she was raised with staff hand-milking her mother, Bibi, while daily photographs and videos won her an international audience, a children's book series, and a name borrowed from the hippopotamus character in "Shrek." Her brother Fritz was born in 2022.

Visitors left flowers and cards at the statue of Fiona outside the zoo within an hour of the announcement. Mayor Aftab Pureval called her "a symbol of perseverance and joy" in Cincinnati, and Governor Mike DeWine of Ohio called her "a true icon." Usha Vance, whose family keeps a home in Cincinnati, posted a photograph of one of her children face to face with Fiona through the glass of her enclosure.
