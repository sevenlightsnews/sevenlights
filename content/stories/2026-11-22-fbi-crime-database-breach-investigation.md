The Federal Bureau of Investigation is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center, two people briefed on the matter said Friday. The NCIC connects federal, state and local authorities so that criminal records, warrants and other law enforcement information are available nationwide. The bureau declined to comment. CNN reported the matter earlier in the evening.

The bureau has not said who may have breached the system or what data was compromised, and investigators are examining the entry point. The report marks the second intrusion affecting FBI systems in recent months. In September the agency announced an investigation into a notorious hacking group that claimed it had stolen data on agents through the bureau's job portal.

The NCIC report follows an apparent cyberattack at the US Department of Agriculture. Employees were informed Thursday that one of the department's systems had been targeted. USDA officials with knowledge of the matter said a system the agency uses to pay farmers was hit, and that no other systems appeared to be affected as of Friday. Officials raised alarms that hackers could find a way to reach more sensitive information through the compromised system.

The breaches come during a week of technology failures across the United States. Outages affecting private and public sectors over the past several days, including a nationwide aviation ground stop last week caused by a failure in Federal Aviation Administration communications systems, have raised concerns about the nation's cybersecurity and the condition of aging infrastructure.

The sensitivity of the NCIC gives the report particular weight for policing. Officers rely on the system during traffic stops and arrests to check identities, warrants and criminal histories. Retired law enforcement officials warned that a breach could endanger officers in the field if the records returned cannot be trusted or are altered.

The FBI had no further comment as of Friday night, and it was not established whether the NCIC episode and the September job-portal intrusion were connected. The report lands amid heightened scrutiny of how federal systems are defended, after a week of outages across the public and private sectors.
