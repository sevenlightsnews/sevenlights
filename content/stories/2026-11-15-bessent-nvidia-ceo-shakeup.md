Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called on Sunday for Nvidia's board to hire an outsider as chief executive and to carry out what he described as a "dramatic shakeup" of the chipmaker's leadership, as the company begins the search for a successor to Jensen Huang.

Huang stepped down this month after the board opened an investigation into his conduct, following reports of extramarital affairs including a relationship with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Bessent said the company will be "on the spot" when it reports earnings Tuesday, and demanded answers about Nvidia's record on export controls. He questioned whether the company pursued potentially illicit chip sales to boost earnings and suggested other executives beyond Huang should face scrutiny.

Some backers of Huang have begun campaigning for his return. The Treasury secretary's remarks deepened a public feud between Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whose department is examining suspected diversion of American chips to China. Both men are expected to accompany President Trump to Asia this week.

The pressure on the board comes as rival chipmakers weigh whether Huang's departure opens ground they had conceded for years. Analysts at research firms have described the succession question as the largest in the industry, given that Huang founded the company and ran it with dozens of direct reports and no announced succession plan.

Nvidia shares slipped this month as investors digested the ouster, and the stock drifted lower again in recent trading as the search widened. Washington has linked the episode to a broader reckoning over chip smuggling: export-control officials have pressed the company for records tied to the Megaspeed distribution case, and congressional committees have sought Huang's testimony.

The board has begun interviews for a permanent chief executive. The earnings report Tuesday will be the first under interim leadership and the first market test of whether the company can hold its pricing and customer commitments without its founder in the chair.

Update, November 16, 2026: Huang is sounding allies about a possible return as chief executive, the Financial Times reported. Two large shareholders — Brad Gerstner of Altimeter Capital and Gavin Baker of Atreides Management — urged the board in an open letter Friday to "pursue a slow, measured, and deliberate process" on future leadership while leaving open the "potential return" of Jensen Huang. Altimeter held nearly $1.9 billion of Nvidia shares at the end of June and Atreides nearly $250 million.
