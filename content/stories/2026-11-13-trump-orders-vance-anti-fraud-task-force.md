President Trump wrote on Truth Social on Thursday that "we don't need a deal with Iran" and ordered Vice President JD Vance to give his "complete and total attention" to "corruption and crime" in Democratic-run cities and states, naming Minnesota, California and Illinois.

The directive pulled the vice president away from the file he had personally managed. Vance had been working through intermediaries in Iran and Qatar toward a ceasefire and a resumption of nuclear talks after the war that began in February with a joint United States-Israeli strike on Iran.

The vice president chairs the task force to eliminate fraud, created by executive order, and the President's post framed that mandate as his new center of gravity. Trump wrote that he had asked Vance to "focus his complete and total attention" on "corruption and crime" in the three states he named, claiming billions of dollars were being stolen monthly from government programs there. The announcement deepened questions about what the president's plan is to end the war. It was not immediately clear whether the Iran portfolio would pass to another official or lapse.

The war had strained the administration and the economy. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz after the February strikes, and fuel prices surged. A United States blockade of Iranian ports remains in effect, though the strait has largely reopened. Trump said last week that the war was "effectively over," and at a White House appearance alongside the prime minister of Qatar he said the United States was "locked and loaded" but that a resumption of hostilities was unnecessary. Gulf states had lobbied Washington against restarting the fighting.

Oil prices jumped and stocks fell after Thursday's post, as traders read it as a signal that a negotiated settlement had moved further away.

Vance's office and the White House did not respond to requests for comment on what the order means for the negotiations. The vice president had argued privately that ending the war was the fastest route to lowering fuel prices and repairing the administration's standing with its own voters.
