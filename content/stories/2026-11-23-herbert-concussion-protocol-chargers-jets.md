Chargers quarterback Justin Herbert entered the National Football League's concussion protocol after playing the entire 27-24 loss to the New York Jets on Sunday.

Herbert did not miss a snap in the game, Pro Football Talk and The Athletic reported. He was sacked six times and hit repeatedly by the Jets' pass rush, and the team placed him under evaluation after the final whistle rather than declaring him uninjured.

Under the protocol, a player suspected of a concussion is removed from play for examination and must pass a sequence of steps before returning. The steps run from a symptomatic observation on the sideline and an off-field screening examination to a graded exercise challenge that raises heart rate, followed by a stepwise return to training and participation. Team physicians and an independent neurotrauma consultant oversee the process, and clearance requires that symptoms be absent at rest and under stress.

The protocol was adopted in 2011 after a season of prominent head injuries and has been revised repeatedly. Independent spotters in the booth can remove a player from the game for examination, and no coach or trainer may return a suspected case to play without medical clearance.

The loss dropped the Chargers to 1-9. The team gave no immediate timetable for Herbert's return. His availability for next week's fixture depends on clearing each step of the protocol before game day.

Herbert, drafted eighth overall in 2020 out of Oregon, has been the franchise's centerpiece since his rookie season. Los Angeles has built its roster around the quarterback, and his absence from any portion of the remaining schedule would force the first quarterback change of the season.

The final margin left Los Angeles within three points of the Jets at the whistle. The next examination determines whether Herbert faces another hit's consequence or a cleared path back to the lineup.
