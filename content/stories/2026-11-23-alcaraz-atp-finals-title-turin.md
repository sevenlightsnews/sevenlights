Carlos Alcaraz defeated Alexander Zverev 6-2, 7-6 (7) on Sunday to win the ATP Finals in Turin, the year-end title he had never captured.

The victory gave Alcaraz, 23, the two most consequential lines of a disrupted season. He began 2026 as the youngest man in history to complete a career Grand Slam, and he finished it as the fourth man to hold all four Grand Slam titles and the ATP Finals. He ends the year on a 14-match winning streak built on two of the tour's fastest indoor courts, Paris and Turin.

Zverev, who will finish the season as world number one after winning his first two Grand Slam titles this year, entered the final unbeaten at the event, as did Alcaraz. The head-to-head had favored Alcaraz 7-6, with four wins in the past five meetings, including a five-set French Open final in 2024 and a fifth-set comeback from a break down at this year's Australian Open.

Much of Alcaraz's season was defined by a wrist injury that kept him out for four and a half months in the spring and summer. He missed only two of the four Grand Slams and two of the nine Masters events. Faster indoor conditions, long viewed as unfavorable to his game, produced his strongest results at the season's end.

For Zverev, the final left the ranking ahead of the achievement. He has questioned publicly whether the number one position reflects his results, and a win over Alcaraz would have strengthened the claim. It did not come.

The result closes the strangest of men's tennis seasons, in which the titles of world number one and the world's best player rested with different men. Alcaraz lifted the trophy he had never held; Zverev returns to pre-season with the ranking and the first major final results of his career to defend.

Play resumes with the Australian Open in January.
