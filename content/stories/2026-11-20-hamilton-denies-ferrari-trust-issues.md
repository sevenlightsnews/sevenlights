![A wide dusk photograph of a wet racetrack pit lane whose glossy surface reflects a long red garage wall topped by a glowing amber light strip, set beneath a deep blue sky with pink horizon clouds and distant low hills](/images/stories/hamilton-denies-ferrari-trust-issues.webp)

Lewis Hamilton denied there is a lack of trust between himself and Ferrari, despite overruling the team's pit instructions in two of the last four races of the Formula One season.

At the Singapore Grand Prix in October, Mr. Hamilton ignored an instruction to pit under a virtual safety car at the same time as teammate Charles Leclerc, believing he could make a one-stop strategy work. Worn tyres lost grip late in the race and he fell from third to sixth. He later accepted responsibility, apologised to the team and called the result "a painful lesson." At the season's previous round in Brazil, he again declined a late call to pit for intermediate tyres after being told conditions would not change; the rain intensified, he spun while attempting to pass Liam Lawson, and finished ninth. He said the team had not told him the rain was heavier in the first sector of the lap.

"No, it's definitely not trust," Mr. Hamilton said on the eve of the Las Vegas Grand Prix. "We're constantly working on our communication. I think it's been a lot better this year." He said his race engineer, Carlo Santi, "has done an amazing job," and added: "Those particular two times, particularly last time, I didn't have the information I needed, and I got the call last minute. We're super united, everyone's working in the right direction."

Friction with Mr. Leclerc is separate and, Mr. Hamilton indicated, settled. The Monegasque attempted to pass Mr. Hamilton in Mexico in an incident that cost both drivers, prompting public criticism from Mr. Hamilton. Team principal Fred Vasseur said the exchange showed a lack of respect for the team, and the drivers held clear-the-air talks in Brazil. Mr. Hamilton said he expects the pair to race cleanly against each other from here.

In Las Vegas, Mr. Hamilton was sixth fastest in a rain-affected second practice on Thursday, having handed his car to Ferrari reserve driver Dino Beganovic for the washed-out first session. He said the team made "a couple of good findings" as the track dried, but that with dry conditions expected for qualifying, Ferrari must "react quickly" in a compressed final practice.

The season's final rounds begin with qualifying on Friday night, local time. Ferrari's form over one dry lap will show whether the car, not the radio war of words, is its problem.
