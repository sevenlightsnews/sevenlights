![Aerial view at dusk of a vast data center campus: long windowless white server halls in neat rows, cooling equipment between them, high-voltage lines crossing surrounding farmland, orange evening sky, documentary aerial photojournalism, no readable text, 3:2](/images/stories/trump-tax-threat-overseas-data-centers.webp)

President Trump said Friday at a White House press conference that his administration would bar American companies from building data centers in other countries, and impose a "tremendous tax" on any that did.

"We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country," Mr. Trump said. "If they want to continue building the centers outside the U.S., they can, but we're going to put a tremendous tax on them. They're tremendous assets." He said the United States was "not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them."

The president gave no size for the tax, no list of covered companies and no effective date, and offered no explanation of the legal mechanism he would use. Congress holds constitutional authority over federal taxation, and Mr. Trump did not say whether he would seek legislation or claim existing executive powers. CNBC reported the policy appeared to come as news to his own staff.

The threat reached a wide field. Amazon, Microsoft, Google and Oracle operate extensive international cloud networks, and the operators Equinix and Digital Realty maintain facilities abroad. Companies build data centers outside their home markets to cut processing delays, keep service reliable near customers and satisfy laws that require certain data to stay within national borders. A penalty on overseas construction could therefore reach well beyond investments tied to China.

Mr. Trump spoke a day after returning from Shenzhen, where he met Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in a trilateral meeting, and a day after a Commerce Department regulation took effect imposing licensing requirements on certain advanced semiconductor exports bound for overseas data centers that give substantial computing access to companies headquartered in China. The president said he and Mr. Xi had discussed artificial intelligence and agreed to work together on guardrails.

The proposal landed amid growing resistance to data center construction at home. A CNBC survey found 64 per cent of registered voters opposed a facility within 10 miles of their home, against 13 per cent in support. Construction costs rose 13 per cent in 2026 on higher equipment prices, labor pressures and limits on electricity. Mr. Trump dismissed the domestic opposition and urged communities to negotiate financial benefits from builders: "You have to take advantage of the data centers."

Analysts and trade groups awaited details on scope and authority. Until a mechanism exists, the tax remains a threat companies can price only as risk.
