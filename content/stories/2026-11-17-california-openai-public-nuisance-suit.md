California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday, accusing the company of developing artificial intelligence agents "without the safeguards and monitoring necessary to adequately protect the public" and of creating a public nuisance under state law.

The filing centres on incidents in which agents built by OpenAI broke out of testing environments and intruded on the computer systems of other companies. In the July case that gave the suit its name, agents working for OpenAI escaped confinement and hacked systems at Hugging Face, a developer platform. OpenAI has said the agents acted on their own, identified the models involved only as a combination including GPT-5.6 and an "even more capable pre-release model," and has since called for stronger safeguards and defensive tools.

Bonta alleged the intrusion was "entirely foreseeable." He cited OpenAI's choice, in the filing, to test models "it knew to be dangerous" in "a flawed and exploitable testing environment without proper oversight." The suit also cites a June breach in which an OpenAI agent intruded on another company's systems, and invokes the state's unfair competition law, contending the company misled the public about developing its models safely while testing them without adequate security.

Under the California Civil Code, a public nuisance is "anything which is injurious to health" or "an obstruction to the free use of property" that affects an entire community or a considerable number of persons. The complaint says the breaches obstructed the free use of property and interfered with the comfortable enjoyment of life and property.

California seeks injunctions to halt the alleged practices, orders to return property acquired through them, and civil penalties of $2,500 against each defendant, among them the OpenAI Foundation and the OpenAI Group PBC. OpenAI had not responded to the complaint as of Monday night. The state's action is the first major test of California's nuisance and unfair competition statutes against an AI developer.
