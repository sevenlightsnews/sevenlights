![Police cars with blue lights outside a suburban concert venue at night on a wet street, officers in high-visibility jackets behind a cordon, low winter light, documentary photojournalism, no readable text, 3:2](/images/stories/denmark-expels-russian-defence-attache-kyivstoner-plot.webp)

Denmark announced Friday that it would expel Russia's defence attaché in Copenhagen after its intelligence service said it suspected Russian intelligence services of plotting an assassination of a Ukrainian rapper at a concert in a Copenhagen suburb.

Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said the Russian ambassador had been summoned to the foreign ministry and told of the decision to remove the attaché within seven days. Rasmussen said Denmark was also weighing suspension of Schengen short-stay visas for Russian nationals.

The PET intelligence service said it suspected the Russian intelligence services had planned the hit with help from a Danish criminal network. Police placed two people under formal investigation Friday on charges of attempted murder and of assisting a foreign intelligence service's operations in Denmark. Both are linked to Loyal to Familia, a street gang outlawed in 2021, Danish broadcasters reported.

"The case shows, in our assessment, that the Russian intelligence services attempted to carry out an execution in Denmark," PET chief Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen said at a news conference. "A Russian assassination attempt is not something we've seen in Denmark before, and that gives me an impression of an increasingly aggressive and risk-taking Russia."

Denmark's public broadcaster DR identified the target as Kyivstoner, a rapper who holds both Ukrainian and American citizenship.

Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen called the case "a clear illustration of the serious security situation in which Denmark and Europe find themselves." She said Denmark faced "a cornered Russia, acting more aggressively and with a greater propensity to take risks."

The Russian embassy in Copenhagen denied the allegations, saying the authorities had "not bothered to present any evidence whatsoever of Russia's involvement in this mysterious crime." Foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova, quoted by the TASS agency, said "Moscow will give an appropriate response."

Denmark has been a steadfast supporter of Ukraine since Russia's invasion nearly five years ago. Rasmussen said the strongest response Denmark could give "is not to waver and to support Ukraine."

The two suspects remain under investigation, and the attaché must leave the country within seven days. Danish officials said the visa question would be decided in the coming weeks.

Update, November 23, 2026: Danish media identified the target as Albert Vasiliev. The PET service said three individuals were stopped near the venue where he was to perform on September 25, and the two charged have pleaded not guilty. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned the plot Saturday as "an attack on EU soil." Polish authorities said Russia had tried to assassinate the rapper twice in Poland, Al Jazeera reported.
