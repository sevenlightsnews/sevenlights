House Democrats on Wednesday nominated Representative Hakeem Jeffries of New York to be speaker of the House when the new Congress is sworn in in January, a vote that would make him the first Black lawmaker to lead the chamber.

The nomination came at a caucus meeting held behind closed doors, by secret ballot, with no other Democrat seeking the post. A source familiar with the tally said Jeffries won 251 of 262 ballots cast. Jeffries posted on X afterward: "Thank you House Democrats. Together, we will deliver bold change."

The result followed a change in tactics reported earlier this week. Jeffries and his allies had hoped to nominate him by acclamation; forcing a tallied vote instead tested his support before the House floor vote in January. A loss of more than 40 votes would have signalled trouble; he lost 11.

Democrats retaken both the House and the Senate in the November midterms with significant majorities, and Jeffries, minority leader since 2023, is set to be one of President Donald Trump's most prominent opponents during the final two years of his presidency. The party will hold its largest House majority in nearly two decades. Nancy Pelosi, the last Democratic speaker, retires from Congress at the end of this year.

Some progressives had urged lawmakers to look elsewhere, arguing Jeffries represents a politically risky status quo. Representative Jamie Raskin of Maryland, often floated as an alternative, told Axios he was not running. Representative Ayanna Pressley of Massachusetts, the focus of a draft movement, has told allies she is not interested. Asked at a town hall Monday, she said: "I do not need to be the speaker of the House to speak for the people."

The caucus also chose the rest of its leadership for the 120th Congress — majority leader, whip, assistant speaker and caucus officers — with most posts filled unopposed by voice vote.

Jeffries, 56, will be formally elected speaker when the House convenes on January 3. Party officials said the floor vote should follow without incident, though the 11 dissenting ballots showed the unease within the caucus is real.

Update — November 20, 2026: Seven of the 11 Democrats who voted against Mr. Jeffries, all of them freshmen, issued a joint statement explaining their opposition: "Whether on Medicare for All, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics, or ending the genocide in Gaza, our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate on these issues." The signatories were representatives-elect Darializa Avila Chevalier of New York, Adam Hamawy of New Jersey, Frederick Haynes of Texas, Melat Kiros of Colorado, Donavan McKinney of Michigan, Chris Rabb of Pennsylvania and Claire Valdez of New York. Mr. Jeffries told reporters the recorded vote, which he had requested over the acclamation sought by his allies, was meant to let members speak plainly: "The results of today's election speak for themselves. Our job is to fight MAGA extremism, not each other."
