Texas executed Reinaldo Dennes on Thursday at the state penitentiary in Huntsville in a procedure that ran past an hour after prison staff made repeated unsuccessful attempts to find a suitable vein. The execution was completed at 7.07 p.m., 63 minutes after Dennes was strapped to the gurney, and he was pronounced dead a minute later following examination by a physician.

Two intravenous needles ultimately carried the lethal dose of the sedative pentobarbital, one inserted in Dennes' left foot and one in the left side of his neck. Spokeswoman Amanda Hernandez acknowledged a number of unsuccessful attempts before the lines were placed. She said the sites used were in accordance with the state's procedures, and that all procedures were followed appropriately.

Dennes moaned repeatedly about pain and groaned several times once the procedure began. Hernandez suggested he may have exaggerated or feigned it, saying he began complaining before the drugs began to flow and cited pain in places where no intravenous line had been inserted. "There was nothing abnormal on our part," she said. "This isn't the first time we had to insert IVs into other locations. It's also not the first time we've had an inmate make allegations of pain immediately."

After the drugs took effect, witnesses heard sounds like snoring that grew quieter, followed by about a dozen quiet breaths that faded. Dennes gave a brief statement before the procedure began.

He was convicted of capital murder in the death of Janos Szucs, a diamond dealer found shot multiple times in his Houston office in January 1996. A security guard, David Copeland, was shot during the robbery; he survived his wounds and died five years ago. His widow, Janice Copeland, was a witness to the execution.

Dennes was the second person put to death in the United States that day. Florida had earlier carried out a record 20th execution of the year, that of Thomas Mitchell Overton.

The Texas procedure followed renewed scrutiny of capital punishment after Tennessee's failed execution of Christa Pike in September. Pike survived two doses of pentobarbital that her lawyers say probably did not enter her bloodstream correctly and instead pooled in her arms, leaving them burned and blistered. She has been discharged from a hospital and returned to prison.

Texas prison officials said the execution proceeded in accordance with state protocol despite the difficulty in placing the lines.
