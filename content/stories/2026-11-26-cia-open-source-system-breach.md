An unclassified computer system at the Central Intelligence Agency was breached in a cyberattack, a person familiar with the incident told the Associated Press on condition of anonymity. The person said the breach involved no classified information and had been resolved.

The compromised system handles open-source intelligence, the collection and analysis of publicly available data from the internet, commercial satellites and private data brokers. Such information can give analysts significant insight on its own or combined with intelligence gathered through classified programs.

It was not clear what specific information was compromised or who was responsible. The New York Times first reported the breach on Tuesday. The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence had not responded to requests for comment.

Cybersecurity outlets reported that the intrusion had been described to news organizations by a person familiar with the matter, and that the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency had also been informed. TechRadar reported the incident was possibly linked to a campaign by the hacker collective ARTEX, which claimed to have penetrated systems at three US intelligence agencies and released unclassified data. Those claims could not be independently verified.

The disclosure follows a run of disruptions across federal systems. The National Security Agency's classified network suffered a days-long outage last week; the agency attributed it to scheduled maintenance. The Treasury Department's financial crimes network, FinCEN, also took systems offline as a precaution during a separate incident and directed banks to file reports by telephone hotline while forensic examiners worked.

Intelligence and security agencies have faced repeated intrusions in recent years, including a 2024 breach of a Treasury cloud environment that US officials blamed on Chinese actors. Open-source systems, though unclassified, aggregate commercial satellite imagery and social media feeds that analysts use to track troop movements and disasters.

The source said the CIA detected the attack and resolved the issue. The agency had not said whether any data had been exfiltrated, and no arrest or attribution had been announced.
