Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of Nvidia and stepped down from its board early Thursday, and the board asked chief financial officer Colette Kress to serve as interim chief executive while it conducts a search internally and externally, according to emails sent to staff.

Shares fell more than 16 percent in New York on Thursday, shedding more than $600 billion in value. Rivals Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom each gained five percent at the close, and Nvidia slipped about four percent further in pre-market trading Friday.

Harvey Jones, the board chair, wrote that the board had "full confidence" in Kress, chief financial officer for more than 13 years. Jones called her "a key architect of NVIDIA's growth and capital strategy." The company scheduled a company-wide meeting for Nov. 18. Bloomberg reported that Mr. Huang's surrender came late Wednesday night, after weeks spent denying allegations of infidelity and fighting to remain in his job.

The resignation capped a week of disclosures. Mr. Huang co-founded the company in 1993 and had built it into the world's most valuable chipmaker, a $4.3 trillion company. A shareholder lawsuit was filed in California alleging Nvidia and Mr. Huang misled investors about the spying scandal; the complaint claims the parties did not disclose that the matter raised national security concerns that threatened the company's reputation and business prospects. President Trump said the F.B.I. was examining Mr. Huang's ties to Alice Huang, a woman U.S. authorities have concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset whose Singapore firm moved restricted Nvidia chips toward China. The New York Times reported the alleged relationship last month. Nvidia described its executive's interaction with her as "not unusual or unexpected," adding that "meetings with customers are part of a CEO's job."

The board said it is searching for a permanent successor among both internal and external candidates. Ms. Kress inherits a company whose AI-driven growth has carried the broader market, and whose customers now await word on whether the transition alters its roadmap.

Update, Nov. 14: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the Commerce Department controlled the entity-list process and criticized delays in restricting Megaspeed, the Nvidia customer at the center of the chip-smuggling allegations, as officials defended the export-control agency, the Financial Times and Fortune reported. Fast Company reported that Mr. Huang's resignation will not end congressional scrutiny of Nvidia's customer safeguards.
