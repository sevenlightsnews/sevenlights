Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Chief Executive Jensen Huang, and directors have begun working to identify a replacement, the Wall Street Journal and Bloomberg reported, a day after reports that the co-founder had conducted a second extramarital affair.

Shares fell more than 8 per cent in extended trading on Wednesday after the Journal and the New York Times reported the second affair, this one with an executive who reports to Huang, and Nasdaq futures slipped in the same session. Earlier in the day the stock had closed up 2.9 per cent at $173.73, having pared its loss of market value since Friday to about $311bn.

The board's move follows an escalation on every front. Nvidia said this week it had retained the New York firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct "a thorough and independent" investigation into Huang's relationship with Alice Huang. She is an executive at Megaspeed, a Singaporean computing firm that US intelligence has identified as a Chinese asset. The inquiry will be led by Wachtell partner Joon Kim, a former government prosecutor, under the supervision of a committee of independent directors. The FBI is conducting its own probe.

Nvidia initially denied an improper relationship with the Chinese citizen. Huang admitted last week that he had been unfaithful to his wife, without naming the other woman. On the same day the board announced the investigation into whether its chief executive had been "the target of a foreign intelligence effort," while saying it had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. The company has since said Huang would continue to lead "without interruption," a commitment the reports of succession planning undercut.

Huang co-founded Nvidia and built it from a gaming graphics chipmaker into a company with a market capitalisation of about $4.1 trillion. Fast Company noted that his possible exit raises the question of what the company becomes without its founder. Bloomberg reported that the board was meeting to discuss Huang's removal and potential replacements.

Directors are expected to conclude their deliberations in the coming days. Huang has not publicly addressed the succession reports, and a spokesperson for Nvidia declined to comment on whether he had retained his own legal representation amid the company investigation and the separate one opened by the FBI.
