Marvel Studios approached Patrick Stewart about appearing in both Avengers sequels, but the actor agreed to only one, "Avengers: Doomsday," the film on which he worked through failing health before his death in October.

Stewart, who originated the role of Charles Xavier in the film franchise in 2000, returned to it for Doomsday knowing the shoot might be his last. In an Entertainment Weekly cover story on the film, cast and crew described his final day on the set, which proved to be his last on any film.

Kevin Feige, who was present as an associate producer when Stewart first played Xavier, recalled the actor rising to address the crew. "He stood up and he gave a speech to the crew. We didn't know necessarily it would be his last day on a set, but he was sort of indicating to us that he thought it was," Feige said.

Stewart died of complications of Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia. "It was clear that he was struggling with his health, and he worked so hard to deliver a performance and a presence — for us as directors, for his other actors, for the crew," said co-director Anthony Russo.

The finished film will carry what the directors describe as an elegiac weight. Xavier "is the conscience in the movie," co-director Joe Russo said. "He has a lovely scene with Ian McKellen, who's his best friend, in the film. It's a very emotional scene about forgiveness and I think very resonant in the wake of his passing." Stewart and McKellen, friends in life as well as on screen, shared the chess-board confrontations of their characters across several X-Men films.

Feige said the directors have been completing final cuts, including a memorial segment. "We're long done filming that movie, and he did an amazing job for us," he said. "We're looking now at how we can put something together that honors and respects our friend." Such tributes followed the deaths of Leonard Nimoy, Carrie Fisher and Chadwick Boseman among others.

Doomsday, an ensemble film in which the Avengers unite with heroes from three universes against Doctor Doom, opens December 18. The secret meetings, casting reversals and publicity deceptions that preceded it — including Chris Evans's denial, later reversed, that he was returning as Steve Rogers — filled the EW profile alongside the tribute to Stewart.

Producers have not said whether Stewart's contract covered a further appearance. No arrangements for a public memorial were announced.
