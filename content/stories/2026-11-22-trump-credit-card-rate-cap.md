President Trump said he would gladly work with the incoming Democratic majority in Congress to cap credit card interest rates and fees. In a phone interview on Fox News Channel's "Saturday in America," he said he would enact the ten per cent cap he promised during the 2024 campaign.

"I didn't even know they said it but they've been trying to get that for years. I would do it," Trump said. Asked by the program's host, Kayleigh McEnany, whether he meant a one-year cap on credit card interest rates at ten per cent, the president answered in support of the measure. Axios reported that Trump said he would work with Democrats on rates and fees.

The proposal has support in both parties. Senator Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts, poised to become chair of the Senate Banking Committee when the new Congress convenes in January, has pressed for a cap repeatedly and has said she is ready to work with Mr. Trump to pass a bill. Democrats and the president have both backed limiting rates to ten per cent, the standard formulation of the measure.

The numbers involved are large. The median credit card interest rate is roughly 22.2 per cent, according to an August survey by the New York Federal Reserve. At a ten per cent cap, a cardholder carrying a balance of five thousand dollars would pay about forty-two dollars a month in interest on that balance, against roughly twice that at the median rate. Researchers who studied the campaign pledge after it was first announced estimated substantial savings for cardholders.

The banking industry opposed the idea the first time, arguing a cap would make lending too risky and cut tens of millions of Americans off from credit. Mr. Trump did not advance the proposal after winning office, citing industry pressure and reluctance in his own party. Axios reported that it remains unclear how far Democrats will prioritize the cap once they take control of a chamber in January.

The president's pledge puts him alongside the Democrats who have led the campaign for a cap, and squarely against the banking industry. A fight over the measure is expected when the new Congress convenes in January.
