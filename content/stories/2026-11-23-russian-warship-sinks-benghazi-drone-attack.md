A Russian navy corvette sank at the naval base in Benghazi after Ukrainian sea drones struck it on Sunday, satellite images published Monday showed.

Two Libyan military officials said the attack at the Benghazi Naval Base severely damaged the corvette Merkury and set a fire at the facility. The fire was extinguished later the same day. An initial investigation found that several Ukrainian sea drones were launched at the ship from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya, the officials said. At least four drones were intercepted and destroyed and two hit the warship, they said.

The officials spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to brief reporters. They are affiliated with the self-styled Libyan National Army, which controls eastern and southern Libya.

The government based in eastern Libya expressed serious concern over the strike and condemned it as a violation of Libyan sovereignty. There were no casualties in the attack on the warship, authorities said.

Hours before the strike on the corvette, a cargo ship was hit by a sea drone in a second attack in the same area, Libyan officials said. President Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed on Monday that Ukraine carried out the operation. There was no immediate comment from Moscow.

Satellite imagery showed the warship capsized at its berth. The attack was the latest in a series of strikes in the North African country linked to the war between Russia and Ukraine.

Libya has been divided since a NATO-backed uprising toppled Muammar Gaddafi in 2011. A UN-supported government sits in Tripoli and a rival administration in the east. The oil-rich country has remained a focal point of rivalry between Russia and the Western powers, and Moscow has long cultivated military partners in the east.

Ukrainian forces have stepped up long-range strikes in recent months. Ukrainian sea drones have struck Russian ships and port facilities in the Black Sea; Sunday's attacks were the first reported against Russian vessels in Libyan waters.

The extent of the damage to the Merkury and the fate of its crew were not fully detailed by Libyan officials. Investigators in eastern Libya were examining the launch route of the drones, the officials said.

Update, November 24, 2026: President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Monday that Ukraine carried out the attack, deploying seven maritime drones against the corvette Merkury, which Mr. Zelensky described as one of the Russian navy's newest vessels. Mr. Zelensky called the operation, conducted about 2,400 kilometers from the front line, "exemplary" and said "the targets we set were achieved." Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov declined to comment, saying the Defense Ministry would provide information if deemed appropriate. Ukrainian officials said a second vessel, the cargo ship Sparta, was also struck in the same area; Russian and Finnish vessels helped extinguish the fire, its crew was rescued and the ship was towed away, Reuters reported. The European Commission on Monday proposed a further round of sanctions against Russia, targeting dozens of banks and crypto platforms and banning Russian teams and athletes from competing under the flag; the measures require the endorsement of all 27 member states.

Update, November 25, 2026: Ukraine's security service formally confirmed the operation, saying seven domestically produced naval drones were launched from a mothership against the two Russian vessels. Mr. Zelensky released footage showing a drone striking the warship while at anchor, and said Ukraine would continue operations in designated waters in response to Russian aggression.
