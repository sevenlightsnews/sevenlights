A federal judge in Washington ordered the Trump administration on Saturday to suspend its plan to close East Potomac Park and its historic public golf course, issuing a temporary restraining order hours before the site was scheduled to shut on Sunday for a 15-month renovation.

U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes enjoined the government from closing the park and the East Potomac Golf Links during the hours in which they were open as of November 14. Her order runs through November 28, or further order of the court. It does not require active management of either site and leaves the government free to conduct repairs with the notice required by an earlier preliminary injunction.

The administration had announced the closure by social media post. President Trump said on October 26 that the site would close on November 15 "in order to renovate and rebuild for Competitive, Safety, Modernization, and Aesthetic reasons," describing the goal as a world championship public facility. Reyes wrote that the posts "indicate that the Washington National Plan is final and that NPS will close the Park to implement it," and that both points favored granting the plaintiffs' motion.

The DC Preservation League and two local golfers challenged the project in February. They argue the redevelopment would change the character of an accessible public course and make it unaffordable, a charge the president denies. The group's lawyers had earlier won an order preventing the National Park Service, part of the Interior Department, from cutting trees or making other irreversible modifications without advance notice.

Reyes, a Biden appointee whom the president has called a "lunatic," ruled in a case brought by the preservation league with representation from Democracy Forward. Its senior legal adviser, Catherine Carroll, said the drive to destroy the park "would have taken away a treasured park that could never be replaced."

The Justice Department said the judge acknowledged that access may be limited where the public is at risk of harm. "Nothing in the Court's order precludes planning for the development of a world class golf course to continue," the department said.

The ruling is the latest in a series of court challenges to the administration's projects in the capital, including a White House ballroom, the renaming of the Kennedy Center and a proposed arch. The case returns before the restraining order expires on November 28.
