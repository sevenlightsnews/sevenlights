![White marble sarcophagus of the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier in winter light, an Army sentinel in dress uniform and gloves standing rigid at the rail, rows of headstones receding into fog behind, bare winter oaks, overcast grey sky, photorealistic, 3:2](/images/stories/hegseth-tomb-unknown-soldier-reprimand.webp)

The Army sentinel guarding the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier loudly admonished Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and the senior staff accompanying him at Arlington National Cemetery on Wednesday, Veterans Day, after the group stopped on the tomb steps for photographs and laughter was heard.

Video of the incident shows Hegseth taking photographs on the empty steps in front of the tomb with his wife, Jennifer; his chief of staff, Ricky Buria; his deputy chief of staff, Patrick Weaver; and senior adviser Tami Radabaugh. As the group broke from taking photographs, a loud laugh could be heard. The soldier guarding the tomb shouted: "All visitors will maintain an atmosphere of silence and respect at all times."

The guard issued a verbal reprimand to the advisers and staffers after the annual Veterans Day ceremony, in which President Trump laid a wreath at the tomb and spoke to mark the day. The Pentagon press secretary, Sean Parnell, said the sentinel had "reminded everyone to maintain decorum."

The exchange was captured on video that circulated within hours and drew criticism from military groups and Democrats, who said the scene showed disregard for the cemetery's conventions. The tomb's guard operates under a standing instruction to all visitors to "maintain an atmosphere of silence and respect at all times," and visitors are routinely corrected; the shouted admonition followed that rule.

Republican officials largely kept their distance from the episode. Democrats seized on it in a week that had already tested the secretary's standing: Hegseth narrowly survived an impeachment effort in the House the day before the Veterans Day incident. The defense secretary has survived repeated episodes of friction with the officer corps and the Hill since taking office.

The Pentagon gave no indication that any of the group would be disciplined, and no Army statement addressed whether the reprimand would carry any consequence beyond the recorded rebuke. The cemetery's rules on photography near the tomb steps were unchanged.

The incident closed a Veterans Day that began with a wreath-laying by the president at the tomb, and it was likely to shadow the secretary's next public appearance at the cemetery.
