![Giant deflated cartoon balloon lying on a wet broad city avenue at dawn, wind bending bare trees, grey storm clouds over tall stone buildings, deserted sidewalk with confetti and caution tape, editorial photojournalism photograph, 3:2](/images/stories/macys-thanksgiving-parade-postponed-weather.webp)

Macy's postponed its Thanksgiving Day parade on Wednesday for the first time in the event's history, citing an approaching nor'easter that was expected to put forecast winds above the limits for flying its giant balloons. The hundredth edition of the parade will be held Saturday at 8:30 a.m., the retailer said.

"The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority, and after close consultation with our City and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year's celebration," Macy's said. The announcement came hours after the balloons, a record 100 inflatables for the centennial, had been inflated near the parade route. NBC will air last year's parade in the Thanksgiving time slot instead.

Under rules announced by City Hall in 1998, the balloons may not fly when sustained winds exceed 23 miles an hour or gusts exceed 34. The New York Police Department holds final authority over whether they are grounded. The National Weather Service issued a high wind warning for Manhattan on Wednesday, forecasting sustained winds of 20 to 30 miles an hour and gusts to 55 on Thursday. The forecast for Saturday along the route was mostly sunny with a high near 55 degrees.

The balloons had been grounded once before, in 1971, when wind kept them tethered. The parade itself was cancelled only in 1942, 1943 and 1944, when the materials needed to build the huge figures were diverted to the second world war effort.

The storm threatens travel and holiday plans well beyond Manhattan. Forecasters expect rain and wind across the East Coast through the holiday weekend and warned of scattered power outages from the gusts.

It was unclear whether all scheduled performers would take part on Saturday. Thousands of volunteers and performers take part each year. This edition was to include a tribute to Dolly Parton and numbers from nine Broadway shows.

The parade began in 1924 as a holiday promotion for Macy's Herald Square store. Its first editions borrowed live animals from the Central Park Zoo; the animals were dropped in 1927 after children were frightened, and inflated figures replaced them.

Officials said the Saturday route and start time had been set with city agencies, and that the balloon decision would be reviewed again on Friday against the latest forecasts.
