Miley Cyrus paid tribute to Dolly Parton on Monday after the two received a Grammy nomination together in the Best Country Duo/Group Performance category for their remake of "Light of a Clear Blue Morning," recorded with Lainey Wilson, Queen Latifah and Reba McEntire. Parton died in August at age 80 following a brief battle with cancer.

"My Aunt Dolly is one of the greatest songwriters the world has ever known," Cyrus, 33, said in a post on Monday. "She never made music for recognition but to make the world a happier place through her songs."

She added: "To be nominated alongside her, Lainey Wilson, Queen Latifah and Reba McEntire for Light of a Clear Blue Morning is a beautiful way to honor her enormous heart."

The 2026 recording updates Parton's inspirational 1977 single and was released to raise money for pediatric cancer research at Vanderbilt University's Monroe Carell Jr. Children's Hospital in Nashville, a cause close to Parton. Cyrus noted the cover "supported pediatric cancer research at Vanderbilt's Monroe Carell Jr. Children's Hospital." She also recalled that Parton gave her a vinyl copy of the 1977 album "New Harvest…First Gathering," which carries the original, "a couple Christmases ago."

"I miss her calling on big days like this but feel her in every moment. Love you Aunt Dolly," Cyrus wrote. "Congratulations to these beautiful women and all the other nominees."

Parton received a second nomination in the same category, for her duet "Butterfly" with the Zac Brown Band. For Cyrus the joint entry is the most personal of her 11 nominations; she won the same duo and group category with Beyoncé at the 67th Grammys in 2025 for "II MOST WANTED," and holds three awards in all.

Cyrus publicly mourned her godmother on Instagram on August 25, the day Parton's representative announced her death. "What I KNOW is Dolly would want me to feel it, write a song about it, and then continue on and be strong," she wrote then. The 2027 ceremony will decide the category next year, with Parton nominated posthumously.
