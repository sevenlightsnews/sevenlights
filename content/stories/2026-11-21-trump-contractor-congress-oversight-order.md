President Donald Trump is directing federal contractors and subcontractors to consult his administration about inquiries they receive from Congress and to withhold any communications the White House considers privileged, under an executive order signed late Friday. The order applies to all contractors and subcontractors, a universe of tens of thousands of businesses.

The companies must also coordinate with the White House and federal agencies on "appropriate responses" to congressional requests, the order says. If they fail to comply, the White House warns, their contracts could be terminated and their future work with the federal government suspended.

The order is the administration's latest effort to resist what are expected to be wide-ranging oversight efforts when Democrats take control of the House in January following this year's midterm elections. Democrats have made government contracts one of their top investigative targets, noting that federal money has flowed to companies linked with Mr. Trump's family, particularly at the Pentagon.

White House officials had already begun tightening control over how agencies answer Congress. On Monday, White House counsel Will Scharf told US agencies they must win approval from his office and the office of legislative affairs before agreeing to appear before Congress. In a recent letter to Representative Robert Garcia, the ranking Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, Scharf suggested that some congressional investigations may amount to "blatant criminality." Garcia this week sent letters directing members of Mr. Trump's family and top administration officials to preserve documents. Scharf dismissed the demands in a letter to Democratic lawmakers, noting they are not yet in the majority and cannot compel compliance.

In a fact sheet, the White House framed the new requirements as "protecting executive privileges from congressional overreach." The administration argued that congressional leaders have tried to intrude on executive branch privileges by "harassing private companies that contract with the federal government."

Presidents of both parties have long invoked executive privilege to resist congressional inquiries. The doctrine allows a president to withhold information from Congress, the courts and the public to protect the confidentiality of White House deliberations, but it is far from absolute and typically produces protracted legal fights over whether it applies. Whether contractors can be compelled to surrender records they hand to Congress has no clear precedent, and court challenges are widely expected once Democrats assume the majority in January.
