Leeds United beat Chelsea 4-1 at Stamford Bridge on Saturday, their first victory at the ground since a 2-0 win in December 1999, and a result that left manager Xabi Alonso's side eighth in the Premier League and in open crisis.

Emmanuel Emegha gave Chelsea the lead on his full debut, finishing well after Morgan Rogers profited from a James Justin error to send the striker through. The forward's first goal since joining from Strasbourg last summer did not last. Leeds equalized in the 28th minute when Josh Acheampong mistimed his jump under a delivery, Dominic Calvert-Lewin found Noah Okafor, and Okafor slipped a low shot past Emiliano Martínez.

Calvert-Lewin converted a penalty, his sixth goal of the season, after Martínez brought him down early in the second half, and Okafor added a second with what the Guardian called a clinical strike. Brenden Aaronson completed the scoring late. Leeds, superbly coached by Daniel Farke, moved up to sixth and were tactically superior throughout; Chelsea faced 21 shots and were jeered off at half-time.

Chelsea lost Moisés Caicedo early, injured in a collision with Justin, and were already wobbling when the penalty came. Levi Colwill misplaced simple passes after returning from a knee injury, and Wesley Fofana was involved in the goals. The defeat followed a home loss to Tottenham earlier this season — the first time in 67 years Chelsea have lost grudge matches at home to Leeds and Tottenham in the same campaign.

"Chelsea are fragile and need to toughen up," Alonso said afterward. The Spaniard, appointed in the summer, appeared bewildered on the touchline and is under mounting pressure from supporters. Few players have found a role in a team with no discernible pattern of play, and the absence of João Pedro in attack has compounded the problem.

The result ended a run of 15 league matches in London without a win for Leeds, dating to May 2022, and was their biggest winning margin in the capital since April 2003. It was the first time Leeds had scored four goals away at Chelsea since a 5-2 win in January 1970.

Chelsea now sit eighth but have lost more league games than Manchester United. The club can expect another season without European football unless results sharpen quickly, and Alonso's next test comes in midweek. Farke's side travel with confidence for the first time in years.
