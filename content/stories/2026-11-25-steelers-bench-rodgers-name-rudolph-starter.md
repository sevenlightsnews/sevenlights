The Pittsburgh Steelers benched quarterback Aaron Rodgers and named backup Mason Rudolph the starter for Friday's home game against the Denver Broncos, a decision taken two days before Rodgers turns 43.

The move followed a week in which head coach Mike McCarthy said on Monday that Rodgers remained the starter, and the team then announced the change on Tuesday. Rodgers was not listed on the injury report.

Rodgers, sacked 31 times this season, posted a career-low 86.6 passer rating. In Sunday's 19-16 loss at Philadelphia he completed 30 of 44 passes for 227 yards and a touchdown with no interceptions, and led the team with 29 rushing yards. He was sacked five times in that game. Wide receiver DK Metcalf was ejected after catching one of four passes thrown his way, Michael Pittman Jr. did not play, and Max Hurleman, in his first NFL game, led the Steelers in receiving.

Rudolph, the 31-year-old backup drafted by Pittsburgh in the third round in 2018, carries a career passer rating of 84.3 and career totals of 4,927 passing yards with 30 touchdowns and 22 interceptions.

The Steelers' playoff position suffered in Philadelphia, and the team felt the offense needed a spark, according to a report by NFL insider Jordan Schultz. He said the decision to bench Rodgers was one the club "intends to revisit in the near future." The report noted confusion over choosing Rudolph over younger options on the roster, Will Howard and Drew Allar, and the possibility that Rodgers could regain the job within weeks.

The timing gives Rudolph a first start of the season against a physical Denver defense, on a Black Friday afternoon at home in Pittsburgh. Denver's suspension of running back J.K. Dobbins for the same game removes one element of the opponent's attack.

The Steelers return to practice this week before hosting Denver on Friday. Whether Rodgers resumes the job afterward, as the reported intention to revisit the decision suggests, falls to McCarthy to decide.
