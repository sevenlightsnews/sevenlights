Cam Skattebo, the New York Giants running back known for backflip touchdown celebrations, said Wednesday he will stop doing them, a week after the Detroit Lions pass rusher Aidan Hutchinson suffered a season-ending knee injury while celebrating a sack on Sunday.

"It's hard to see," Skattebo told reporters at the Giants' facility in East Rutherford, New Jersey. "I'm saying, yeah, no backflips. Mentally, I'm like, 'I'm not doing that. That ain't going to happen to me.'" He did not rule out the celebration permanently, joking he might reconsider if the Giants reach the playoffs. He said Hutchinson likely never saw the injury coming: "Didn't think about the risk either, and it happened."

The Lions placed Hutchinson on injured reserve, ending his season, and activated safety Kerby Joseph to clear a roster spot. A Detroit linebacker said his "heart dropped" watching Hutchinson's reaction on the field.

Skattebo's decision drew wide comment online, most of it supportive given the Giants' injury record. One analyst called it "a smart long-term decision"; another wrote that people "don't grasp tail-end risk until they see it happen." Skeptics noted that Skattebo's own season-ending ankle injury in 2025 had not stopped the flips.

The warnings had accumulated. The Giants told Skattebo to avoid the celebration before the 2026 season. He flipped anyway, after touchdowns against Dallas in Week 1 and Washington last Thursday. A backflip landing went wrong at a fan festival this summer, and he revived the move in May after hitting a home run at a teammate's charity softball game.

Skattebo is central to the Giants' plans through the final stretch of the season; through eight games he has carried the backfield the club has built around him. Coaches had tolerated the flip as a crowd-pleaser while treating the risk as manageable. Hutchinson's injury has now settled the argument internally.

The Giants play again Sunday. Team officials said no formal ban on celebrations had been issued; the change was the player's own.
