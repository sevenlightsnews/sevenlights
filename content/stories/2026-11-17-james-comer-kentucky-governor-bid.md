Representative James Comer of Kentucky announced Monday night that he will run for governor in 2027, opening the Republican contest to succeed Democratic Governor Andy Beshear, who is term-limited. Comer, chairman of the House Oversight Committee, entered the primary as the immediate front-runner.

Comer launched the candidacy through a tele-town hall in which he took questions from callers. He built the evening around the economy and affordability. "Families are worried about losing their children and grandchildren to other states with better opportunities, and communities, both urban and rural, are struggling to provide basic services and maintain infrastructure," Comer said.

"Kentucky needs a leader whose policies will lead to growth and good-paying jobs."

He added: "The government doesn't have all the answers, but we have a responsibility to solve problems and lift people up when we can."

The announcement followed this month's midterm elections, which returned both chambers of Congress to Democratic control. Kentucky voters, while re-electing Beshear for two consecutive terms, backed Republicans for statewide offices in November. Comer had teased a bid for months and previously indicated he would enter shortly after the midterms.

Comer, first elected to Congress in 2016 from the state's western district, used the launch to create some distance from the President. "I think that some of the things the president has tried to do on the national level had good intentions, but there are always unintended consequences," said Comer, who like many House Republicans aligned himself with Trump over the years. The Oversight Committee he chairs oversaw Congress's inquiry into Jeffrey Epstein.

Kentucky is one of three states, with Louisiana and Mississippi, that hold off-year gubernatorial elections. The 2027 contest will be an early test of how durable the Democratic wave proves one year after the midterms and a year before the 2028 presidential and Senate elections. The Democratic nominee has not been named. The race will test whether the state's habit of electing Democrats statewide survives a national election that went the other way; Comer leads in early Republican polling and is expected to face a field still taking shape.
