Skip Hinnant, an original cast member of PBS's children's series "The Electric Company" and the originating voice of Schroeder in the Off-Broadway musical "You're a Good Man, Charlie Brown," died Sunday, November 8, in Denver. He was 86. His family announced the death and said he passed peacefully surrounded by loved ones.

Joseph Howard Hinnant was born September 12, 1940, and raised on Chincoteague Island, Virginia. He reached television early, playing the boyfriend of Cathy Lane on ABC's "The Patty Duke Show" from 1963 to 1965. In 1967 he originated Schroeder in "You're a Good Man, Charlie Brown" Off Broadway, a cast that included Gary Burghoff as Charlie Brown, Bob Balaban as Linus and Hinnant's brother Bill Hinnant as Snoopy.

He moved into voice work through the late 1960s and found his most notorious role in Ralph Bakshi's adult animation. Hinnant voiced the title character in the X-rated "Fritz the Cat" (1972) and its 1974 sequel "The Nine Lives of Fritz the Cat," adaptations of Robert Crumb's underground comics.

In 1971 Hinnant became a featured performer on "The Electric Company," PBS's answer to "Sesame Street" for beginning readers. Over six years and 780 episodes he played Fargo North, Frankenstein, Jay-Jay, Supper Man and other roles, beside castmates including Morgan Freeman, Rita Moreno and Bill Cosby. His later voice credits included the Easter Bunny in Rankin/Bass's 1977 stop-motion special "The Easter Bunny Is Comin' to Town," and Pogo Possum in the 1980 film "I Go Pogo." He played various roles in "Spidey Super Stories" and "My Little Pony," and worked in the 1990 television movie "Alexander and the Terrible, Horrible, No Good, Very Bad Day" and "HBO Storybook Musicals" that year. On screen in the late 1980s he appeared in single episodes of "As the World Turns" and "Kate & Allie."

Hinnant served on the national board of the Screen Actors Guild from 1980 to 2003 and held memberships in Actors' Equity and AFTRA. His family said he "was proud of his work to preserve commercial voice contracts and the everyday actor's rights." He is survived by his longtime partner Ruth Nerken; four children, Chris, Jamie, Julie and Sara, and their spouses; nine grandchildren; and his ex-wife Miriam Hinnant. No public arrangements were announced.
