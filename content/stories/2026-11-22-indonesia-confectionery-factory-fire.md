At least three people died and 36 were missing after a fire engulfed a confectionery factory in Banten province, Indonesia, on Saturday afternoon, and search crews pulled 15 survivors from the wreckage on Sunday.

The blaze began at the Fonusa Agung Mulia factory in the industrial town of Cikande, in Serang regency about fifty kilometres west of Jakarta. Fire officials said the alarm was raised at about 4.30 p.m. and that crews found the building fully ablaze on arrival.

"When we arrived a large area was already on fire. So we could not determine whether the company's protection system was functioning properly or not," fire official Tubagus Machfudz told local broadcaster Kompas TV.

Banten governor Andra Soni said an estimated 60 people were inside when the fire started. The search and rescue agency, Basarnas, said four people were injured and taken to a hospital and a health clinic. Bodies were recovered on Sunday morning, and small fires were still burning inside the structure as crews worked to account for the missing.

"All elements of the joint search-and-rescue team are making maximum effort to locate the victims while prioritising the safety of personnel in the field," Basarnas official Rizky Dwianto said. Drone footage showed emergency crews moving through the blackened site. Relatives gathered at the factory gates seeking news of the missing.

The local disaster management agency said the cause remained under investigation. Indonesia has no mandatory fire-inspection regime for many industrial buildings, and deadly workplace fires recur. A blaze believed to have started with the explosion of a drone battery swept through a seven-storey building in Jakarta in December last year, killing at least 22 people. An explosion at a nickel-processing plant in eastern Indonesia in 2023 killed at least 13.

Rescuers faced unstable structure and residual heat inside the plant, officials said, which slowed the search. The joint operation was expected to continue until all the missing were accounted for.
