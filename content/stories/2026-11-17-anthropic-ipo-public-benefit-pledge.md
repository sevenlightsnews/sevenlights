Anthropic has pledged in its IPO filing to place humanity's long-term benefit above shareholder returns. The prospectus commits the San Francisco AI company to "pursue long-term benefits of humanity through the responsible development of advanced AI." It states the company "strive[s] to help bring humanity safely into an era where transformative AI exerts tremendous and potentially decisive influence over a broad range of human undertakings."

The company has attached dollar commitments to the language. Anthropic has pledged at least 5 per cent of its stock to a public benefit fund, and its founders have committed to donate 80 per cent of their wealth.

Unlike Google's 2004 prospectus, which featured the then-motto "Don't Be Evil" without legal force, Anthropic's pledge is built into its corporate structure. Like its rival OpenAI, Anthropic is a public benefit corporation, a designation for companies that balance shareholder interests with the public good. A Long-Term Benefit Trust of five trustees with expertise in AI safety, national security, public policy and social enterprise holds a single Class T share that lets them vote as a unit. The trust "generally has the authority to elect and remove" Class T directors, who together will constitute a majority of the board. The share carries no economic rights.

Legal scholars call the arrangement untested at the scale an IPO would reach. Sarah Kreps of Cornell University's Tech Policy Institute said the real test emerges "if and when the question of public benefit becomes costly," or when the public benefit conflicts with profit. Stavros Gadinis of UC Berkeley Law said the charter is designed to prevent the situation in which a board's fiduciary duty to maximize value overwhelms its mission, citing Elon Musk's 2021 takeover of Twitter.

The mechanisms give Anthropic wide leeway to decide how to measure whether it is benefiting society, the scholars said. The offering's size, timing and price range were not disclosed.
