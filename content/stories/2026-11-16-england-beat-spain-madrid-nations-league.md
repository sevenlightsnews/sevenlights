England beat Spain 3-2 in a Nations League group decider at the Metropolitano stadium in Madrid on Sunday, surviving a half-hour with ten men after Elliot Anderson's sending-off to end Spain's run of 43 matches without defeat and finish first in Group A3.

Harry Kane opened the scoring from the penalty spot after getting his body between Pau Cubarsi and the ball in the area; the video assistant referee checked an earlier incident before confirming the award. Mr. Kane had missed decisively when Spain beat England 3-2 at Wembley in this competition's opening match. He won the corner from which Declan Rice's outswinger found Ezri Konsa unmarked for the second goal, and Jude Bellingham set up Ben White for the third as England led 3-0 after something over an hour.

The match turned when Jarell Quansah, the last defender, was dispossessed by Lamine Yamal, who carried the ball into the area and was fouled by Anderson, a summer signing from Manchester City. The referee showed Mr. Anderson a straight red card for denying a goalscoring opportunity and awarded a penalty, which Mikel Oyarzabal converted. Substitute Nico Williams made it 3-2, finishing from another Yamal chance, and England spent the closing stages defending. James Trafford, selected over Jordan Pickford in goal, secured the result with a double save in stoppage time, first denying Fermin Lopez and then Aymeric Laporte.

Thomas Tuchel called it "a very complete performance and the best I've had so far," while questioning the officiating. "The red card and the penalty is always a big decision - and I need to understand why it's a double punishment," he said, adding that without the sending-off "we had a clear win" and could have scored more.

Spain had been beaten only once in the 43 matches, on penalties against Portugal in last year's Nations League final. England advance to the quarter-finals as group winners, leapfrogging Spain in the table.
