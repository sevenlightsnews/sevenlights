Crystal Palace have been deducted three points in the Women's Super League after an independent tribunal found the club used four substitution windows in their 1-0 opening-day win over Everton on 6 September, a breach of competition rules that carry automatic point removal.

Under WSL rules a manager may make five substitutions in a match, but only across three stoppages in play. Palace head coach Jo Potter made changes in the 62nd, 83rd and 90th minutes at Gander Green Lane, then brought on Kirsty Howat for Shannon O'Brien in the seventh minute of second-half stoppage time to see out the victory — a fourth separate window.

The tribunal concluded the breach was inadvertent and that Palace had not sought an unfair advantage. None of the parties is eligible to appeal, and the club has been stripped of the three points won in the match, one of only two WSL victories Palace have managed this season since promotion back to the league.

Palace objected to the sanction. "Everyone at the club is obviously frustrated that such a minor infraction, given that the player was on the pitch for only 46 seconds while the ball was in play, has led to such a significant penalty — a penalty which we do not agree is reasonable or proportionate," the club said. "We are disappointed for the players and staff who worked so hard to secure a deserved victory on our return to the WSL."

Everton, who lost the match, were understood to be disappointed with the outcome but were not formally represented because the case was brought as the WSL against Crystal Palace. Everton wrote to the tribunal arguing the late substitution had a sporting impact, noting seven shots after the 90th minute and 72 per cent possession in stoppage time while "chasing an equaliser."

Evidence put to the tribunal showed the administrative failure behind the breach: the match operations manager's report stated the fourth official had not recorded Palace's third substitution window in her notes because she was simultaneously dealing with requests from the broadcast match manager. Potter told the tribunal her assistant had suggested the stoppage-time change.

The deduction takes effect immediately in the current WSL table. The tribunal's findings closed the case; with no route of appeal for any party, the corrected standings stand for the remainder of the season.
