Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Monday that Congress would compel answers from Nvidia and the Trump administration over the reinstatement of chief executive Jensen Huang, whose return to the company his board had removed this month sent shares sharply higher.

"The Trump Administration's rush to reinstate Jensen Huang underscores the need for Congress to act," Mr. Schumer wrote on X. "Trump is putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security, emphasizing that he's learned nothing from his resounding election defeat. Democrats will wield their full power to investigate."

Mr. Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3. His aides have said his reinstatement changes nothing about his planned testimony before House committees in December, including foreign affairs and intelligence.

Senator Tom Cotton of Arkansas, a Republican, joined the call for answers on whether Mr. Huang's affair facilitated the smuggling of advanced chips to China. "The controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg, and it's not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back," Mr. Cotton said. He said Congress would pass bipartisan chip export-control measures, including his Chip Security Act, "one way or the other."

Megaspeed, a Singapore-based distributor formerly run by a woman identified in reports of Mr. Huang's affairs, has been under federal investigation on suspicion of diverting semiconductors in breach of export controls. On the day of Mr. Huang's reinstatement, the government added Megaspeed to a federal trade blacklist, barring American companies from selling it chips and other advanced technology.

Nvidia's board said Monday that it had voted unanimously to restore Mr. Huang after consultations that included input from senior officials in the United States government, and that it was "confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk." President Trump told CNBC that he had "convinced" the board to act.

The oversight demands follow a week in which the episode convulsed equity markets. Nvidia's shares rose more than 30 percent on Monday after the announcement, lifting broad indexes out of a steep AI-driven selloff.

The Senate Banking Committee has not scheduled a separate session on the administration's role. House Democrats have called for the company's internal investigation materials.
