The Pittsburgh Steelers released quarterback Aaron Rodgers on Wednesday, hours after he accused head coach Mike McCarthy of falling asleep during team meetings in a surprise 34-minute appearance on ESPN's Pat McAfee Show.

"We have released QB Aaron Rodgers," the Steelers said in a statement on X. The move came two days after the team announced that Rodgers would be deactivated for Friday's game against the Denver Broncos and that backup Mason Rudolph would start. Rodgers, speaking during the interview, had said his time with the Steelers was over.

Rodgers, a four-time league MVP, said McCarthy arrived late to a quarterbacks meeting the previous week and fell asleep. "Mike came in late, sat down, and within 10 minutes began snoring," he said. "Woke himself up, was rattling his papers. Five minutes later, you hear the snoring again. And less than five minutes after that, he walks out of the room like nothing happened." Rodgers said McCarthy also missed walk-throughs in the weeks before the benching, and described his coach as "unhealthy." "If you're sleeping in meetings, you're unhealthy," he said.

He said he learned of the coaching decision only when teammate Pat Freiermuth telephoned him. Freiermuth told him, "I can't believe they're doing this," Rodgers said.

A former teammate criticized the public airing of grievances. "I think it's despicable," said Lang, who played with Rodgers and remains on speaking terms with McCarthy. "I think that Aaron Rodgers just became everything that he has bitched about and moaned about over the last 10 years with former coaches, former teammates." Lang said several former teammates in a group chat shared the view.

The Steelers are 6-4 and have lost two consecutive games, Sunday's defeat by the Eagles among them. Rodgers had said he intended to retire at the end of the season. Unless another club claims him on waivers, he will become an unrestricted free agent; the Indianapolis Colts and New England Patriots are among the teams in need of a starting quarterback.

McCarthy, who led the Green Bay Packers to a Super Bowl title with Rodgers before reuniting with him in Pittsburgh, had not responded publicly to the accusations. The team's statement made no reference to the interview.

Rodgers' representatives had no immediate comment on his next steps. The Broncos game proceeds Friday with Rudolph at quarterback.
