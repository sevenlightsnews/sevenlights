A Russian cargo ship bound for the Baltic exclave of Kaliningrad caught fire after a drone attack in the eastern Gulf of Finland on Saturday and later drifted into Finnish territorial waters, Finnish and Russian authorities said. The vessel, the Sparta II, was struck in the morning; the Finnish coast guard said it was alerted at about 08:50 local time and located the ship roughly 50 kilometers west of the island of Suursaari.

Finnish border officials imposed temporary aviation and maritime restrictions around the area. The Finnish Border Guard was leading operations involving the ship and is responsible for the investigation into the attack, cooperating with other authorities, Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen said. He added that NATO had been informed. Finnish defence forces said Ukraine was likely behind the strike. Kyiv had not commented by Saturday evening.

Kaliningrad Governor Alexei Besprozvannykh said two drones had been used, based on preliminary information, and that there was "no threat" to the territory's supplies. A Russian rescue vessel approached the stricken ship, and the crew told it that no evacuation was needed, Besprozvannykh wrote on Telegram. The Finnish Border Guard later said the crew had been taken off to another Russian vessel already en route to Russia. The rescue vessel remained at the scene. The Border Guard said no environmental damage had been detected and that there was no danger to maritime traffic or people.

Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the attack showed the war was "spilling over into our immediate vicinity." He added: "Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor."

The Sparta II was sanctioned by the United States in 2022 and by Ukraine earlier this year. According to the Ukrainian and US governments, it is run by a Russian company that says on its website it was established within the structure of Russia's defence ministry in 2011 and has operated within the military construction complex since 2015. France 24 reported the ship is affiliated with a major Russian military logistics company.

The Gulf of Finland, which borders Russia, Finland and Estonia, flows into the Baltic Sea, where undersea cables and shipping have been subject to heightened security since 2023. Finland has tightened security across the region, and the incident is certain to add to friction between Helsinki and Moscow. Häkkänen said the investigation would proceed with other relevant authorities.
