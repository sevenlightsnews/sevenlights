Jake Elliott kicked a 59-yard field goal with two seconds remaining Thursday as the Philadelphia Eagles rallied from 21 points down to beat the Dallas Cowboys 39-38 in Arlington, Texas, the largest comeback on Thanksgiving Day in N.F.L. history.

Dallas led 31-10 early in the third quarter and 28-10 at the half, with Javonte Williams scoring three times and Dak Prescott throwing for 291 yards and two touchdowns. Philadelphia answered with 29 points in the fourth quarter.

The rally began when defensive lineman Ty Robinson blocked a Bryan Anger punt, which Kenny Moore II returned three yards for a touchdown. Robinson then sacked Prescott on fourth-and-one with 55 seconds left, and a pass-interference penalty against Markquese Bell moved the Eagles into range for Elliott's kick.

In between, Joe Milton III ran the comeback. Filling in for Jalen Hurts as the Eagles' third starting quarterback of the season, Milton completed 21 of 31 passes for 331 yards with three touchdowns and one interception, and ran 15 yards for a score. DeVonta Smith caught 11 passes for a career-high 200 yards and two touchdowns, including a 45-yard strike that opened the fourth-quarter surge. Britain Covey's 55-yard kickoff return set up Milton's rushing score, and a 24-yard pass to Makai Lemon closed the gap to 38-36 with 2:10 left. The Eagles failed on two-point attempts on consecutive possessions.

Dallas had chances to put the game away. Brandon Aubrey, usually reliable, shanked a 40-yard field goal wide left with 3:27 remaining, and Prescott had answered the blocked punt with a bruising 14-yard touchdown run that restored a two-touchdown lead. None of it held.

"This is absurd!" Tom Brady said on commentary as time expired. Coach Nick Sirianni celebrated in front of the Cowboys' fans.

The Eagles, who also lost running back Saquon Barkley to a stinger in the first quarter, improved to 4-7 and won consecutive games for the first time this season after beating Pittsburgh last weekend. Milton, a former Cowboy and a restricted free agent after this season, burnished his case for a larger role; the Athletic noted his performance puts him in discussion for the club's future plans.

Dallas fell to 5-7, losing ground on the division-leading New York Giants, and travels to Seattle and Los Angeles over the next two weeks.
