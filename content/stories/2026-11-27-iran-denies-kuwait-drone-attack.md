Two Italian technicians working on an air-defence system in Kuwait were killed in a drone attack on Thursday, the first fatal strike in the Gulf state in months. Kuwait and Italy blamed Iran for the assault; Tehran insisted it was not behind it.

"Around 2pm today, several Iranian-made drones targeted Kuwaiti military installations, including an air-defence detection station where two Italian technicians from Rheinmetall were working," Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, told the Senate. The men worked for the German arms manufacturer, Italy's foreign ministry said.

Kuwait's defence ministry said four people, the two Italians, a Kuwaiti national and a Pakistani national, were injured by falling shrapnel after a number of drones from Iran were detected and intercepted. The Kuwaiti Army said the attack struck "a vital site in the country" and was the result of "heinous Iranian aggression," while confirming the deaths of the two Italians.

Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, held phone calls with his Kuwaiti counterpart, Jarrah Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah, and with Mr. Tajani to deny Tehran's involvement. "Iran had no role or involvement whatsoever in the occurrence of this event," the Iranian foreign ministry said. Mr. Araghchi said the incident looked "suspicious" and resembled a "precooked recipe for war." He offered a joint investigation, saying Tehran was prepared to cooperate with Kuwait and Italy to "determine the origin of the incident."

The armed forces spokesman, Abolfazl Shekarchi, said earlier that "no military or offensive operations have been conducted" by Iran. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni offered condolences on X: "My thoughts are with all those affected by the attack and their families," she wrote.

The strike came as Iran has ratcheted up attacks in the last week on Kuwait and Gulf neighbour Bahrain while it continues to contest control of the Strait of Hormuz with the United States. Just over a week ago, Kuwait said it had repelled overnight Iranian drone attacks on an airbase hosting US forces, the first strikes on the country in more than two months.

The broader conflict began when the United States and Israel attacked Iran in late February. Iran retaliated by launching missiles and drones at Israel and at Gulf Arab states hosting US military assets and personnel. Recent exchanges of fire between Washington and Tehran, and a deadlock in negotiations over the war, have raised fears of a major escalation.

The deaths of European defence workers marked a broadening of the toll. Kuwait, a longstanding American treaty partner, has avoided mass casualties since the war began, and Thursday's strike tested whether that restraint would hold as drone traffic over the Gulf intensified.
