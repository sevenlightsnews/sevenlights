New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani underwent successful surgery Friday to repair a torn Achilles tendon and is expected to make a full recovery, his press secretary, Joe Calvello, said. Mr. Mamdani, 35, tore the tendon last week during a friendly soccer game between City Hall and the New York City press corps, a match in which he scored a goal.

The procedure began just after 11:30 a.m. and ended about 70 minutes later. "The surgery was successful, and the Mayor is expected to make a full recovery," Mr. Calvello said Friday afternoon. The mayor was discharged from the hospital and was "resting comfortably at Gracie Mansion," he said, adding that City Hall was "extremely grateful for the first-class care the Mayor received from the medical staff today."

Mr. Mamdani appeared at a news conference on Nov. 20 on crutches and described the injury himself. "I forgot that I was 35, and soon after, I tore my Achilles," he said, noting that he had not stretched before the game. "I will be in a boot for six to eight weeks. I will play soccer in about nine months."

First Deputy Mayor Dean Fuleihan has been leading day-to-day city operations in the mayor's stead. No transfer of authority was contemplated.

The first-term mayor has made the sport a visible part of his public life, playing in community matches and appearing at professional games. His office has not said when he will resume public appearances on foot; the coming weeks' schedule is expected to favor indoor appearances at City Hall.

Achilles repairs of this kind typically require months of rehabilitation, with an initial period of immobilization before strength training. Mr. Mamdani's own timeline — a boot for six to eight weeks, a return to soccer in about nine months — is consistent with that course.
