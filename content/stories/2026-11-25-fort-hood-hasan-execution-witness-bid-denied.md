![Rows of military helmets placed atop rifles beside pairs of combat boots on a grass field at dawn, memorial display, soft grey light, blurred flagpole in background, no people, no readable text, editorial photojournalism photograph, 3:2](/images/stories/fort-hood-hasan-execution-witness-bid-denied.webp)

Three survivors of the 2009 Fort Hood shooting will not be allowed to witness the execution by firing squad of Nidal Hasan, the Army said Tuesday, rejecting their request for reconsideration days before the punishment is set for 3 December. Hasan on Tuesday asked a federal court to stay the execution, US court records showed.

Military officials told the three on 10 November that they could not attend because none was the direct victim of the murder and attempted-murder charges of which Hasan was convicted. The survivors argued for a broader definition of "victim" drawn from the uniform code of military justice, which covers anyone who suffered "direct physical, emotional or pecuniary harm," and said the refusal implied they had not been victims because no bullet struck them.

An email sent Tuesday and shared with the Associated Press said officials cited security and space issues in denying reconsideration. A letter signed by the commandant of Fort Leavenworth, the military prison where Hasan is held, said the three could not appeal again. "Please understand that this is not meant as a judgment on any emotional injury you or others suffered as a result of CP Hasan's crimes," it said.

Retired Colonel Kathy Platoni, who would have been Hasan's supervisor during a planned deployment to Afghanistan, said the denial felt like a betrayal. "I would desperately like to attend," Platoni said from her home in Ohio. "To look Hasan in the eye and say: 'You didn't get me.'" She said the building where Hasan first opened fire had been crowded with returning soldiers and others preparing to deploy, and that she had been moved to another building when the attack began.

Hasan was found guilty of multiple counts of murder and attempted murder in the attack that killed 13 people and wounded more than 30 at the Texas army post. The execution on 3 December would be the first carried out by the US military since 1961 and the first by firing squad since the Second World War. A plan to livestream the execution was rescinded after bipartisan backlash. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth had first raised the prospect of a public broadcast, telling a conservative news outlet he would ensure "people are able to watch it."

The Army said regulations specify who may witness a military execution and reiterated size and space restrictions. "Efforts have been made over the years, from the trial to notifications to memorials and now to the execution, to recognize the impacts and support the victims," it said.

The stay application was pending. The execution remains scheduled for the afternoon of 3 December.
