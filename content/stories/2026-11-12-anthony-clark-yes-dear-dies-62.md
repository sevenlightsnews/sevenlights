Anthony Clark, the stand-up comedian who played Greg Warner on the NBC sitcom "Yes, Dear," died Tuesday, November 10. He was 62. His co-star Mike O'Malley announced the death in an Instagram post, and no cause of death was made available.

O'Malley recalled first seeing Clark perform well before they became co-workers and friends. "When my wife Lisa and I were dating back in the mid-nineties, Anthony was already famous in Boston, regularly selling out comedy shows," he wrote. "The first time we saw him at The Comedy Connection we laughed and laughed and laughed. He was so unique — one of those comics who is laughing along with you at the absurdity of his stories and observations and it just made you laugh harder because he was laughing. I was a fan before I was a friend."

"Yes, Dear" ran for six seasons and 122 episodes. The sitcom followed Greg Warner and his family as they adjusted to his sister-in-law and her family moving into their guest house. The role made Clark a familiar face to network sitcom audiences of the 2000s.

Tributes came from comedians who shared the Boston circuit. Dane Cook, who performed alongside Clark in their early years at the Faneuil Hall Comedy Connection, wrote that Clark was "one of the best live acts I've ever seen." He added: "Destroyed to a point he made you wanna quit right then and there while watching him do it effortlessly. This is really terrible to hear."

By O'Malley's account, Clark had been an established headline draw in Boston, selling out the city's clubs, years before his move to network television. Obituaries marking his career were published by Deadline, Variety and The Hollywood Reporter.

No memorial arrangements were announced. The cause of death had not been released as of Wednesday.
