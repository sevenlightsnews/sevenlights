Luca Guadagnino confirmed this week that his adaptation of Bret Easton Ellis's "American Psycho" will not be made, bringing his involvement in the Lionsgate project to an end two years after he signed on to direct.

Speaking on Deadline's Crew Call podcast with the outlet's Anthony D'Alessandro, the Oscar-nominated director was asked about the film's status. "American Psycho, which is a fantastic script by Scott Z. Burns. Unfortunately, it's not happening for me as we speak," he said. Deadline reported that he is no longer attached to the picture. The Hollywood Reporter said the film is not moving forward, and that Burns had written a new take on Ellis's novel for the studio.

The stall had been signalled. In a Vulture profile published last month, Guadagnino suggested the film might be stalled, saying: "Maybe one day I would do." Vulture noted the project met a fate similar to several of his other unrealized films.

Casting had been pursued without resolution. Austin Butler and Patrick Schwarzenegger were considered for the role of Patrick Bateman, the Wall Street investment banker at the center of the novel, but Guadagnino confirmed nothing. Ellis himself said the part frightened off leading actors. "A couple of high-profile actors, whom I can't name, have turned it down," the author said on his podcast earlier this year. "I think maybe because they don't want to be in the shoes of Christian Bale."

Bale played Bateman in the 2000 film version of the novel, the only screen adaptation to reach theaters. Asked about the planned remake, Bale said this year that he loved brave people, without endorsing or attaching himself to it.

Guadagnino gave no indication the property had reverted to Lionsgate or that another director had been approached. Burns's screenplay remains written. Whether the studio revives the novel with other filmmakers was not immediately clear.
