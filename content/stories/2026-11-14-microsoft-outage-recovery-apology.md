Microsoft said Saturday that all its services were restored after an outage that ran roughly 18 hours and disrupted governments, businesses and households across the globe. "We are deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners," the company said on social media. It stated that the failure was "a technical issue not related to cyber or AI/agents."

The outage began on Thursday and struck products including Outlook email, Teams and Microsoft 360 business tools. The outage tracker Downdetector showed disruption in 74 countries and territories across the Americas, Europe, Asia and Africa. Healthcare services were interrupted, corporate earnings reports were delayed, and schools struggled to communicate with families. By Friday the company reported "broad recovery and stability across our services," with full restoration confirmed Saturday.

The damage was heaviest in Australia, where experts said the economic cost could run into the billions of dollars and force some small businesses to close. Analysts and officials said the disruption exposed the country's reliance on foreign technology providers, since government services, banks, retailers and schools run on a small number of overseas cloud platforms. Microsoft's president of Australia and New Zealand offered the company's apology locally and said engineers had addressed the underlying issue. Hospitals reported cancelled appointments and diverted workloads, and several companies confirmed their quarterly results filings slipped past their intended dates.

A single provider carrying such a share of everyday commerce concentrates risk, the Australian commentary ran, and the outage renewed calls for redundancy requirements and data-sovereignty rules for critical services. Microsoft has not announced the root cause beyond the technical classification. The company said it would publish a formal post-incident report, the standard step after major Azure and 360 outages, in the coming days. Australian small-business federations have asked their government to quantify the losses; no official estimate had been released Saturday.

Update, Nov. 15: Microsoft attributed the outage to failed automated health checks on its servers rather than to a cyberattack, Ars Technica reported. The disruption had affected Outlook, Teams and other Microsoft 360 services in 74 countries and territories before restoration was confirmed Saturday.
