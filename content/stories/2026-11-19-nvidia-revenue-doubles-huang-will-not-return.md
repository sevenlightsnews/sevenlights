Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $115.84 billion on Tuesday, doubling the figure from a year earlier, in the chipmaker's first earnings report since its board forced out chief executive Jensen Huang. The chairman of the board, Harvey Jones, said the company "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO."

The third-quarter figures beat the Bloomberg analyst consensus of $109.15 billion in revenue and $2.47 in earnings per share. Nvidia posted earnings of $2.68 a share, with revenue up 103 per cent on the year and 20 per cent on the quarter. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer named interim CEO last week, delivered the results and said: "We delivered another outstanding quarter. Our ambition will not change." Asked about staff retention after Mr. Huang's departure, she said the staff was "committed" and that running the business was "business as usual."

The report came days after the board announced Mr. Huang's resignation "effective immediately" last week, following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman described as a suspected Chinese asset and two with Nvidia employees. Mr. Huang co-founded the company in 1993 and built it from a video-game chip maker into the centre of the artificial-intelligence economy; it controls about 90 per cent of the market for AI training chips, and OpenAI, Anthropic and Google depend on its hardware.

The upheaval cost the company dearly in the short term. Shares fell more than 10 per cent after the resignation, cutting the valuation to $3.7 trillion from a record near $6 trillion in October, though the decline also tracks a broader retreat in AI shares. Nvidia stock rose 5 per cent in after-hours trading Tuesday.

Mr. Huang issued his first public statement Tuesday morning, apologising for his mistakes and saying he would return "if the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America." Mr. Trump, Elon Musk and other Silicon Valley figures have called for his reinstatement; the investor Bill Ackman said the board "rushed to judgment."

Jones said Mr. Huang had stepped down voluntarily and credited Ms. Kress's 13-year tenure at the centre of the company's growth. Mr. Huang has agreed to transcribed interviews in early December with three House committees, including foreign affairs and intelligence. The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that the Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's business in China.

Update — November 20, 2026: The California attorney general has served Nvidia with a subpoena over Mr. Huang's alleged connection to Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy, Forbes reported. Mr. Huang has retained the law firm Paul, Weiss, including former federal prosecutors Melinda Haag and Walter Brown, and Nvidia last week retained Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an independent investigation into whether Mr. Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence officer, a conclusion United States intelligence agencies have reportedly reached, CNBC reported. Securities lawyers said Mr. Huang "faces the Department of Justice, the SEC and private litigation"; a shareholder class action over statements concerning Megaspeed and Alice Huang is pending in San Francisco federal court. Nvidia shares are down 19 per cent since November 8.
