President Trump said he hopes Nvidia will rehire Jensen Huang, the chief executive its board forced out this month, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Huang may fit the bill for the job.

Trump said rehiring Huang would be best for the company and for the United States, Business Insider reported. Bessent said Huang is now "on the outside" of the company and, by that fact alone, a candidate to return, The New York Times reported.

The remarks put the weight of the White House and the Treasury behind a board that has said the opposite. Nvidia's chairman, Harvey Jones, said last week that the company "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." The board accepted Huang's resignation "effective immediately" after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman described as a suspected Chinese asset.

Huang apologized for his mistakes in a statement the day after his departure. He said he would return "if the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America." Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, was named interim chief executive.

The Senate will also put Huang before the public. Tim Scott, the chairman of the Senate Banking Committee, said Huang is set to testify publicly before the panel. Huang has separately agreed to transcribed interviews in early December with three House committees, including foreign affairs and intelligence.

The company's legal position remains unsettled. Nvidia has retained Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an independent investigation into whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence officer, a conclusion United States intelligence agencies have reportedly reached. The California attorney general has served Nvidia with a subpoena over Huang's alleged connection to that woman, described as a suspected spy, and the Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's business in China. A shareholder class action over statements concerning the chip distributor Megaspeed and the woman is pending in San Francisco federal court.

Nvidia reported record quarterly revenue of $115.84 billion last week, doubling the figure from a year earlier, but the shares are down sharply since the resignation, cutting the valuation from a record near $6 trillion in October. Trump, Elon Musk and investor Bill Ackman are among those who have called for Huang's reinstatement; Jones said the board acted on its own authority.

A board election or shareholder action on the question has not been announced.
