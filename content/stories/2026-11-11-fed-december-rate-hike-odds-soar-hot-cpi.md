Consumer prices rose faster than forecast in October, and traders now price a Federal Reserve rate hike at this month's meeting as close to certain, with odds on CME Group's FedWatch tool at 98.8 per cent for a move lifting the policy rate into a range of 4 to 4.25 per cent, up from 84 per cent before the report.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said Tuesday that the Consumer Price Index climbed 0.4 per cent last month against the 0.3 per cent analysts expected. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3 per cent against a 0.2 per cent forecast.

The print confirmed the warning of Fed Governor Christopher Waller. Speaking in London, Mr. Waller said he was "leaning toward" a rate hike even if the October reading came in soft, and that a hotter number would compel "more robust action." In that case, he said, "a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered at the December meeting. Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take."

Mr. Waller said the Fed's communications before and at its October meeting had led markets to price further increases, and that the central bank must now follow through. "When this communication is clear and credible, what really matters is that those hikes are eventually carried out," he said. "If, however, the hikes are not carried out, markets will reduce expectations of rate hikes and rates will immediately fall, which would loosen monetary policy in an undesirable way." Hikes "will be needed sooner rather than later," he added.

Thomas Barkin, president of the Richmond Fed, said earlier this month that he expects the central bank to keep raising rates, with the pace unsettled. "Unemployment is near record lows, and inflation is too high," Mr. Barkin said in a CNBC interview, calling the direction "pretty straightforward." "The question, of course, is pace. Gradual makes sense if you think there's uncertainty in the world," he said.

Bond markets reacted at once. The Wall Street Journal reported that Asian government bond yields rose on growing expectations of a Fed hike. Energy prices, a driver of inflation through the year, may ease in coming weeks as oil prices fall on hopes the war with Iran will not return to full-scale hostilities.

The December meeting is the Fed's last of the year. Officials' last projections and Mr. Waller's remarks point to at least one further increase; whether it is a quarter point or a half point now turns on the remaining inflation and labor data before the meeting.
