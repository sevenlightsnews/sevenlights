Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Thursday, a model tuned for search that becomes the default in AI Mode and answers the most complex queries in AI Overviews. The rollout begins in Search worldwide.

The model parses multi-part questions, tolerates typos and vague phrasing, and fans out into a larger number of subqueries, producing more source links than its predecessors. Answers carry more visuals — images, tables, graphs and maps. Google said the model handles "questions with multiple parts, hard-to-understand typos, or highly specific requests."

Hydrogen offers thinking levels from low to extra-high, with high as the default. Google said it runs 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, and that on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index it beat Gemini 4 Argon, a larger model, at the default high-thinking setting. On Agent Arena, an independent benchmark of multi-step tasks such as deep research, data analysis and codebase navigation, the model ranked fifth. It is optimized for speed and uses fewer output tokens than comparable models.

Koray Kavukcuoglu, senior vice president at Google DeepMind, wrote that Hydrogen is "built for rapid execution across diverse agentic workflows." He said the frontier models "excel at deep, long-horizon reasoning." Robby Stein, vice president of product for Google Search, called it "the biggest model upgrade to Search yet."

Google's AI search features have drawn criticism for fabrications and oversimplification, and the company has competed with OpenAI and Microsoft for the traffic that once flowed to publishers through the search box. Hydrogen's larger subquery fan-out returns more links per answer, a point Google emphasized.

The model rolls out to all Search users, with the most complex-query handling applied first in AI Overviews. It is also available in AI Studio, the Gemini API and the Gemini app, where users can select it from a model dropdown. Paid AI Pro and Ultra subscribers can use it in the Antigravity development platform.
