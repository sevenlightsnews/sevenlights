![A red open-wheel race car kicks up a plume of water spray on wet night asphalt, with motion-blurred neon-lit casino-style towers and a lit grandstand streaking past in the background.](/images/stories/russell-fastest-rainy-vegas-practice.webp)

George Russell set the fastest time of the opening day at the Las Vegas Grand Prix on Thursday, 1:47.272, after heavy rain erased first practice at the Strip circuit entirely and left the second session run on a damp, slowly drying track.

No driver set a timed lap in the first session. When the weather relented, Russell's Mercedes got its lap in late, when the racing line was at its driest, to lead Alpine's Pierre Gasly by 0.071 seconds, with Ferrari's Charles Leclerc third and three tenths behind. "Realistically, we were not the fastest today. McLaren looked exceptionally fast," Mr. Russell said. "We just got the lap in when the track was at its driest." Lando Norris, the race's last winner in Brazil, was seventh. The new world champion, Kimi Antonelli, was eighth.

The day produced almost no usable information about the competitive order, and penalties will reshape the grid before a lap is counted. Mr. Antonelli takes a five-place penalty for exceeding his engine allocation for a second time this season. McLaren's Oscar Piastri, Williams's Carlos Sainz and Aston Martin's Lance Stroll face ten-place penalties for additional power-unit components; Mr. Stroll is on his ninth battery against a limit of three. Haas's Oliver Bearman will start from the back.

Max Verstappen completed only ten laps and complained on the radio of "no power," an assessment his engineer attributed to the power unit warming in cold conditions. Red Bull said it would examine the car overnight. The only red flag of the day came when a plastic bag blew onto the high-speed final corner.

Ferrari brought neither driver's running program to completion; Dino Beganovic drove Lewis Hamilton's car through the first session as a rookie outing. Mr. Hamilton was sixth and said the team made "a couple of good findings" moving from wet to intermediate tyres.

Forecasters expected dry conditions for final practice and qualifying on Friday, compressing the teams' programs into a single hour. "Tomorrow will be a reset for everyone, and that's when the weekend will really start," Ferrari deputy team principal Jerome d'Ambrosio said. The race is set for Saturday afternoon local time, the final United States round of the season.
