The attorneys general of Oregon and Virginia filed suit Monday against SpaceX and X Corp., alleging the companies violated consumer protection law by misrepresenting the safety of Grok's image tools as sexualized depictions of real people, including children, spread across their platforms. It is the first state litigation against SpaceX over the tools.

The complaints were filed separately: Virginia in the circuit court for the city of Richmond, Oregon in Multnomah County circuit court. Both name SpaceX and X Corp. SpaceX acquired xAI, the developer of Grok and the owner of X, in February, taking ownership of the image tools at the center of the case.

At issue is Grok Imagine, an image and video generator built by SpaceXSI, the division formed from xAI. The tool added a mode marketed for adult content shortly after its launch last summer. It was soon found to be producing non-consensual intimate imagery of women and child sexual abuse material, including images generated without users requesting them.

The states allege the companies' later assurances were false. After public outcry, SpaceX's businesses apologized and said safeguards had been added and policies redesigned. The complaints call those statements "misleading" and allege the division instead embraced sexualized content to win market share, with the prohibited output continuing today.

The complaints also attack the companies' internal safety work. Rather than conduct rigorous red-teaming before releasing the adult mode, the states allege, the X Safety team responsible for testing Grok was cut to two or three staff members. SpaceXSI has declined to identify the officers responsible for safety governance, they say.

"xAI not only knowingly endangered kids and our communities, but they also lied to our faces about non-existent safety measures," Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield said.

Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones's complaint stressed the permanence of the harm. Once generated and shared, it says, such images "can never be fully erased." The material becomes "a permanent record of the child's abuse that may resurface for the rest of the child's life," the complaint alleges.

The states seek court orders restraining the generation of the imagery and civil penalties, and ask to represent every Grok and X user in their states. SpaceX did not respond to a request for comment.
