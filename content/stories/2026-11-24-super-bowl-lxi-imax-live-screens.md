IMAX will screen Super Bowl LXI live on its theater screens on Sunday, Feb. 14, 2027, the first time the company has carried the National Football League championship game. The presentation begins at 6 p.m. Eastern and includes pregame and postgame coverage, the four-hour game window and the Halftime Show, headlined by Jay-Z.

The company said the broadcast will run in hundreds of IMAX theaters across North America and in selected international markets: Australia, New Zealand and Brazil. Tickets are expected to go on sale in January, with specific theater locations and ticketing details announced in coming weeks.

The arrangement is a partnership between IMAX, the NFL, ESPN and Disney. ABC, Disney and ESPN hold the broadcast rights for the 2026-27 season, the network group's first Super Bowl in more than a decade, and the theatrical feed will carry ESPN's Monday Night Football commentary team of Joe Buck, Troy Aikman, Lisa Salters and Laura Rutledge. IMAX has staged live sport before, though never an American football title game. The company screened the opening ceremony of the Paris Olympics, a series of Formula One races with Apple TV this year, and NHL and NBA Finals games in international markets. The Super Bowl is the largest single-night audience it has attempted.

The date, Valentine's Day, and the window before the holiday film season give the booking commercial weight for exhibitors. "It's really a boon for theaters to do added business on a day and weekend when most theaters are pretty empty and the new movie options are few and far between," IndieWire's business coverage noted. IMAX's scale distinguishes the deal from earlier cinematic broadcasts of sporting events, which were limited to individual venues or single markets.

"Watching the Super Bowl with your fellow fans is one of the great shared experiences in sports, and this first-of-its-kind collaboration" brings that audience into theaters, the company said.

Two teams are still to reach the game. IMAX has not said how many screens will carry the match in each market.
