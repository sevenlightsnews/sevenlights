Miguel Angel Felix Gallardo, the former police officer who built the Guadalajara cartel and welded Colombia's cocaine trade to Mexico's smuggling corridors, died Sunday in a hospital in Jalisco state, two days after his transfer there from prison. He was 80.

The security ministry of Jalisco said he died at the medical facility after suffering multiple organ failure, having remained under observation with a guarded prognosis. He had been serving his sentence at the Puente Grande prison in the state.

Born in Sinaloa state on January 8, 1946, Mr. Felix Gallardo served as a state and federal police officer and as a bodyguard to a Sinaloa governor, a role that experts say taught him how to secure the complicity of politicians. He co-founded the Guadalajara cartel with the veteran trafficker Rafael Caro Quintero and made it the transportation arm of Pablo Escobar's Medellin cartel, joining Andean production with Mexican distribution across the United States border. At one point an estimated 80 percent of the cocaine entering the United States moved along the Medellin routes, and Mr. Felix Gallardo's organization carried much of it. He was known as "El Jefe de Jefes" and "El Padrino", the boss of bosses and the Godfather.

The network unraveled after the February 1985 kidnapping, torture and killing of Enrique "Kiki" Camarena Salazar, an undercover agent with the US Drug Enforcement Administration, whose investigation had destroyed a vast marijuana plantation belonging to the cartel in Chihuahua. Under intense pressure from Washington, Mr. Caro Quintero was arrested the same year and is now imprisoned in the United States on trafficking and murder charges. Mr. Felix Gallardo was arrested in April 1989, convicted of Mr. Camarena's murder and sentenced to 40 years. He continued to control the business from prison.

The Guadalajara cartel imploded in the 1990s. Its regional successors produced the Sinaloa cartel, built up by Joaquin "El Chapo" Guzman, whom Mr. Felix Gallardo had once put in charge of his organization's logistics, and Ismael "El Mayo" Zambada, both later sentenced to life terms in the United States.

In ill health, Mr. Felix Gallardo was granted house arrest in 2022, but technical problems with his electronic ankle monitor prevented the transfer, and he remained behind bars.

His death closes the founding chapter of Mexico's cartel era. His name endures in the narcocorridos; one of the ballads written about him, "El Jefe de Jefes," is banned in public spaces. It endures on television, where the Netflix drama "Narcos: Mexico" portrayed him.
