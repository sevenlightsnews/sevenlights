Stephen Curry left Wednesday's game at Houston after six minutes with right abdominal soreness and went for an MRI, and Golden State coach Steve Kerr said concern was high about the extent of the injury.

Curry appeared laboured early and departed after the first timeout at the 6:25 mark of the first quarter, holding his right side. Kerr said Curry told him he felt something tweak in the abdominal area, and that it was the first injury of that kind the player could recall. "Concern is high, but I have no idea the extent of the injury," Kerr said after the Rockets' 115-108 win. "I just saw him holding his side and tried to get him out as quickly as I could."

Kerr said he did not know when, where or how the injury occurred. Curry left the arena during halftime for imaging. Kerr added that Curry himself believed he would be fine.

The Rockets took the game behind 25 points and 10 rebounds from Yaxel Lendeborg, with Brandin Podziemski adding 22 points, six rebounds and four assists for Golden State in Curry's absence. The defeat snapped an eight-game winning streak and opened a five-game road trip on a losing note.

Curry, 38, has carried the Warriors' offense at a level no teammate approaches. He entered Wednesday averaging 30.6 points, 4.3 assists and 4.1 rebounds, shooting 50.3 per cent from the field and 45.7 per cent from three-point range.

The abdominal muscle is central to the rotational motion of shooting and driving to the basket, and core injuries of this type are typically managed carefully, though Kerr offered no framework for Curry's case beyond the pending scan.

The Warriors had no timeline for his return on Wednesday night and faced the middle of the road trip before results were in. Until the MRI is read, the staff planned to treat subsequent sessions as precautionary, with Curry limited in shooting work. Kerr said the team expected an update as soon as the imaging was reviewed.
