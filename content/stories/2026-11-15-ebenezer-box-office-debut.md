Johnny Depp returned to the top of the North American box office as "Ebenezer," Paramount's adaptation of Charles Dickens's "A Christmas Carol," which collected $19 million from 3,535 theaters in its opening weekend. The figure fell short of pre-release tracking that had placed the film near the $20 million mark.

The Ti West-directed version of the Dickens novella earned $23 million in 62 overseas markets for a global opening of $42 million against a production cost of $38 million before marketing spend. Britain was the strongest foreign territory at $5.4 million from 633 locations, lifted by a London world premiere that transformed Leicester Square into a winter setting and a campaign that stressed the film's British cast. In France the film took $2.5 million but finished behind a local release. Italy contributed $2 million and Mexico $1.7 million. Audience surveys showed the familiar holiday pattern of older moviegoers and families.

The debut was judged serviceable rather than strong. Analysts noted that few major studio live-action Christmas pictures have opened at comparable scale since the pandemic, and that the film's prospects rest on steady weekday business through the holidays rather than a single weekend. Depp's draw carried the picture in territories where "A Christmas Carol" adaptations have historically underperformed, including Mexico and Japan.

The weekend's other wide release faltered. "How to Rob a Bank" opened to $5 million, well below expectations for a thriller with an established cast. Holdovers held their ground: "Ramayana" approached $150 million worldwide, "Godzilla Minus Zero" passed $100 million abroad, and "Wildwood" sat near $70 million.

Distributors now turn to the holiday corridor, the season's most reliable traffic. Paramount counted on the Dickens title to anchor its fourth quarter after a thin slate, and the coming two weekends will show whether the picture earns its cost the way holiday pictures traditionally do, slowly and at length.

Update, November 17, 2026: The film opened at No. 1 at the North American box office with $19 million, giving Johnny Depp his first US number one in nearly a decade. "Godzilla Minus Zero" fell 60 per cent in its second weekend, and "How to Rob a Bank" finished fifth with $5.175 million.
