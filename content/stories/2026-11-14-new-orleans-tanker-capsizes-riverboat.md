![A large dark-hulled tanker beside a capsized white paddlewheel riverboat in brown river water under gray sky, small rescue boats nearby, industrial port cranes on the far bank, overcast light, photorealistic, 3:2](/images/stories/new-orleans-tanker-capsizes-riverboat.webp)

The asphalt tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck and capsized the river cruise ship American Heritage, which carried 129 passengers, on the Mississippi River at New Orleans on Friday at about 12:30 p.m. local time, near the Port of New Orleans at mile marker 96. Thirty-two people were taken to local hospitals, none with critical injuries, and the Coast Guard said all passengers and crew were accounted for.

New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno said the tanker "lost control" and hit the riverboat. The vessel then sank, toppled and rolled onto its side. Video showed the larger ship striking the stationary boat, which listed until it lay partly submerged. Moreno called the absence of deaths and missing persons "a miracle on the Mississippi."

The American Heritage, a paddlewheel vessel built in 2015 with capacity for about 150 passengers, had been docked for roughly two hours and was scheduled to begin a seven-night round-trip cruise up the river. Passengers in the dining room watched the tanker come toward them before the first impact broke through the windows.

"After the initial crash, it hit us again and we flipped over and I had to crawl over the railing and people to save myself," passenger Rhonda Akers told NBC News. Her husband was hospitalized with a head injury. Others in life jackets were pulled from the water by nearby boats as the response began.

The Coast Guard, New Orleans police, firefighters and emergency medical crews mounted the rescue, evacuating guests to shore and to the port administration building. The Coast Guard closed the river to all vessel traffic between mile markers 93 and 96 while responders assessed the wreck. American Cruise Lines said it was working closely with investigators.

The Atlantic Asphalt is registered in the Marshall Islands, and public records cited by NBC News identify Chinese and Liberian corporate interests in the vessel. President Trump was briefed on the collision, and Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry said he was in contact with local officials and traveling to the city.

Investigators had not determined why the tanker struck the docked riverboat, and authorities announced no findings on mechanical failure or navigational error. The closure of the shipping corridor remained in effect Saturday as response operations continued.
