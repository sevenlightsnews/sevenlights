![Neoclassical federal courthouse facade with tall columns at dusk, a broadcast transmission tower silhouetted beside it against a slate grey sky, a stone gavel sculpture on the steps, moody blue light, editorial illustration, 3:2](/images/stories/judge-blocks-fcc-abc-license-review.webp)

A federal judge in Washington blocked the Federal Communications Commission on Wednesday from moving forward with an unusually early review of ABC's broadcast-station licenses, granting the Walt Disney Co. a temporary restraining order in a dispute the company has cast as an attack on free speech.

US District Judge Loren AliKhan wrote that she concluded Disney was "likely to succeed on their retaliation claim" against the agency. Her order bars the commission "from taking any continuing or further action against Plaintiffs in relation to the early license renewal proceedings," and denies an FCC request to dismiss Disney's suits.

The FCC had ordered Disney to file early renewal applications for ABC's owned-and-operated television stations. Government lawyers say the agency has been investigating the broadcaster since last year over its compliance with anti-employment-discrimination rules, and contend the responses to information requests were insufficient. "The commission decided to look for information that it needed by calling for early renewal of plaintiffs' broadcast license," Justice Department attorney Dimitar Georgiev-Remme said at a hearing last month.

Disney denies the regulatory explanation. Its lawyer, Beth Wilkinson, told the court last month that FCC Chairman Brendan Carr's conduct amounted to "unending threats and retaliation," and said the review left the company sitting with its "head on a guillotine" while it awaited a decision. The FCC says it is not seeking to punish ABC for its speech.

AliKhan appeared skeptical of the government's account. She noted that the commission acted days after ABC late-night host Jimmy Kimmel joked that first lady Melania Trump had "the glow of an expectant widow." In her ruling she wrote that "it is no coincidence that President Trump criticized Mr. Kimmel's skit on social media and the very next day Defendants initiated the early license renewal proceedings."

The confrontation has deepened over two years. After an earlier Kimmel controversy last year, Carr signaled on a podcast that the commission could punish the network. "We can do this the easy way or the hard way," he said. Carr has since suggested networks could lose licenses over coverage of the Iran war, and Mr. Trump has called for ABC's licenses to be revoked. The networks and Disney argued in a filing that they face a choice "between exercising independent editorial judgment or risking the loss of valuable broadcast licenses," which they said chills protected speech.

The early renewal proceedings stand suspended while the case proceeds. The commission had not said whether it would seek review of the order.
