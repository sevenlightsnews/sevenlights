A London judge on Monday sentenced Russell Brand to ten years and six months in prison for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. The fallout from the case widened this week as Netflix moved to make a documentary about it and Meta removed the comedian's social media accounts.

At the sentencing hearing, Judge Joel Bennathan called Brand a "sexual predator" and said his fame had prevented him from being held accountable until a 2023 investigation by Channel 4 and The Times of London brought the allegations to light. Bennathan also cited Brand's "staggering arrogance." The rape conviction stemmed from a 2009 incident; the sexual assault convictions came from incidents in 2004 and 2009. Brand, 50, has been in custody since the jury returned its verdict last week.

Deadline reported that Meta removed Brand's Instagram account in the wake of the conviction. The Playlist reported that Netflix is developing a documentary on the case.

The week's scenes outside the court were unsettled. Supporters held a banner reading "Russell Brand, we stand with you. Christ was crucified for telling the truth. Russell is the truth." After the verdict, Brand's lawyer Ian Winter reportedly confronted The Times journalist Rosamund Urwin, publisher of the investigation. Winter told her "I hope you're pleased with yourself"; she answered that she was "pleased that justice has been done," and Winter replied that it had not been. After the exchange drew press coverage, Winter self-reported the encounter to the Bar Standards Board, according to the BBC.

The case followed the 2023 investigation by Channel 4 and The Times, which prompted a police inquiry and charges. Brand denied all counts. He rose through stand-up to film roles and bestselling memoirs before the allegations ended his career in mainstream entertainment.

The documentary and the account removals are the media industry's first substantial moves since the verdict. No appeal has been announced. Brand remains in custody while his legal team weighs its options.

Update, Nov. 13, 2026: Six additional women accused Brand of sexual misconduct in the days after his conviction and sentencing, Deadline and Us reported. Us confirmed that Brand's marriage to Laura Gallacher played a role in Judge Joel Bennathan's assessment of whether the actor poses a future risk to women; Gallacher has stood by her husband throughout the case.
