Robert Pattinson will play James Bond, the franchise's producers announced Monday, naming the star of the 26th instalment and closing the most closely guarded casting decision since Amazon took control of the series. A statement released with the announcement said Pattinson "has the timeless soul of 007 and brings a spirit that is completely new." A first look at the actor in the role accompanied the news.

Amazon took over the franchise last February, and in May confirmed the search for the next 007 was officially underway. Pattinson and Nicholas Hoult completed screen tests at Bray Studios in Berkshire in recent weeks, as reported at the time. Villeneuve, fresh from "Dune: Part Three," directs from a script by Steven Knight, with Amy Pascal and David Heyman producing. Pascal had said this year that the new Bond would be named before the end of 2026, and that the producers were "being really, really methodical."

The choice repeats a pattern for Hoult. He was the only other name considered for the title role in "The Batman," which went to Pattinson; he fell short of Superman to James Gunn's reboot, taking the villain Lex Luthor instead; and he has now lost Bond to the same actor. Elizabeth Debicki, Pattinson's "Tenet" co-star, said he will be the "perfect" James Bond. "I'm so thrilled," she said.

The casting drew immediate comment beyond the industry. Jon Stewart, on Monday night's "Daily Show," summed up the reaction of Hoult's supporters: "They ended up giving the role to Robert Pattinson. Pattinson! You already got Batman. Enough's enough!"

Pattinson is the first actor named to the role since Daniel Craig's announcement in October 2005; Craig played 007 in five films from "Casino Royale" in 2006 to "No Time to Die" in 2021. No release date for the new film was announced. Filming has no confirmed start, and the producers declined further detail on the script.
