The Asian Football Confederation postponed the 2027 Asian Cup on Monday, moving the tournament in Saudi Arabia from its planned January and February window to December 23, 2027, through January 21, 2028, because of the conflict engulfing the region.

"The Organising Committee for AFC Asian Cup approved to reschedule the flagship national team tournament in light of the current exceptional circumstances arising from developments in the region, including substantial logistical complexities and travel disruptions," the AFC said in a statement. It added that the new dates "will be presented to Fifa for approval".

The decision follows a series of attacks by Yemen's Houthi movement, allied with Iran, on Saudi airports and oil facilities. A Houthi missile strike on Riyadh's international airport last month killed at least 12 people and wounded more than 300. Airspace restrictions and flight cancellations have complicated the travel arrangements that a continental tournament spanning dozens of national federations depends upon.

The disruption reaches well beyond football. Golf's Asian Tour has postponed the Saudi International indefinitely, cycling's Urban World Championships were cancelled, the Six Kings Slam exhibition tennis tournament in Riyadh has been moved from October to December, and Fifa postponed the FIFAe Finals 2026 competition in Jeddah until an unspecified date.

For the host kingdom, the rescheduling carries a symbolic cost. Saudi Arabia has invested heavily in professional sport through its Public Investment Fund in recent years, and the Asian Cup was pitched as an early rehearsal for staging the World Cup in 2034.

Australia's Socceroos, described by the ABC as among the tournament favourites, now face a schedule that places the competition in the middle of domestic league seasons across Asia and Europe.

The AFC gave no new host arrangements; the tournament will remain in Saudi Arabia under the revised calendar. Further changes will depend on Fifa's approval and on the trajectory of the regional conflicts that forced the move.
