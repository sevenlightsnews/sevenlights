Representative Jimmy Gomez of California announced Monday that he will resign from the House on Dec. 1, two days after the House Ethics Committee reported substantial evidence that he engaged in sexual misconduct. Mr. Gomez, a Los Angeles Democrat who served since 2017, lost his primary this year to Angela Gonzales-Torres and would have left Congress in January regardless.

The committee said it found substantial evidence of sexual misconduct with one staffer on two occasions. The first involved unwanted sexual contact; the second, in his office, was described in the report as "forceful." The panel said the conduct reflected a pattern of misconduct and contributed to a toxic work environment in his office. Mr. Gomez said he disputed the findings but acknowledged "personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage," and said he had cooperated fully with the investigation.

Democratic leadership called the report "serious and deeply troubling." Representative Hakeem Jeffries, the incoming majority leader, said the findings demanded a full review of House rules protecting members and staff from harassment. Representative Katherine Clark and Representative Pete Aguilar issued a parallel statement supporting reforms to the chamber's anti-harassment procedures.

The committee made no disciplinary recommendation, noting that the Constitution charges the House with policing the conduct of its own members and that the chamber may take whatever action it deems appropriate. The resignation removes that question from the floor.

The case extends a string of ethics departures this cycle. Representative Eric Swalwell resigned in April after sexual assault allegations ended his California gubernatorial campaign. Representative Tony Gonzales of Texas resigned after admitting an affair with a staff member who died by suicide. Representative Chuck Edwards of North Carolina announced he would not seek re-election after ethics findings of sexual harassment and a hostile work environment. Representative Cory Mills of Florida lost his primary amid ethics accusations, and Representative Max Miller of Ohio was defeated amid an investigation into drug use and domestic abuse allegations.

Mr. Gomez said his staff would be cared for during the transition. Ms. Gonzales-Torres takes his seat in January.
