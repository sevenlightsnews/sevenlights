President Trump announced Thursday that he had granted a "Complete and Total Pardon" to Pete Rose, Major League Baseball's all-time hits leader, who died in 2024 at age 83. Mr. Trump posted the news on Truth Social as he returned from the trilateral meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, calling Rose "a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND." The post at first credited Rose with 4,256 hits "in a Season"; it was later edited to correct the record, which spans Rose's 24-season career.

Mr. Trump did not specify what offenses the pardon covered, and a White House official pointed to Rose's tax case. Rose was never charged with gambling. He pleaded guilty in April 1990 to two counts of filing false income tax returns, admitting he had failed to report $354,968 in income over four years. Facing as much as six years in prison, he served five months and repaid the Internal Revenue Service $366,042.

Rose's ban predated the tax case. He accepted a permanent suspension from baseball in 1989 after an MLB investigation concluded he had bet numerous times on games, including games of the Cincinnati Reds, while playing for and managing his hometown team from 1985 to 1987. Rose denied betting on baseball for nearly fifteen years before acknowledging it in his 2004 autobiography, while maintaining he never bet against his own team. He petitioned for reinstatement four times, in 1997, 2015, 2020 and 2022, without success.

The pardon closes a campaign Mr. Trump had pressed for years. He argued in 2015 that Rose had "paid the price" and announced plans to pardon him in March 2025. His stance was that Rose "was treated terribly by Major League Baseball in not allowing him to go into the Baseball Hall of Fame."

The practical effect is limited. Pardons reach federal crimes, and Rose completed his sentence in 1991; pardons do not apply to Hall of Fame eligibility. Rose's path to the Hall runs instead through Commissioner Rob Manfred's decision last May that permanent ineligibility ends at death, a ruling covering 17 men including Rose and "Shoeless Joe" Jackson. The Hall of Fame has said Rose's candidacy can be considered when the Classic Baseball Era Committee meets in December 2027, with induction possible no earlier than 2028.

Rose set the career hits record at 4,256, breaking Ty Cobb's 4,191, and won three World Series, the 1973 Most Valuable Player award and the 1975 Series MVP award.
