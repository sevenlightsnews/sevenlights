The suspected Chinese spy at the centre of the Nvidia scandal has returned to her home country and is in Beijing, people familiar with the matter said, according to Bloomberg. Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, is not related to Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, and US intelligence has determined she is an asset of Beijing's intelligence services.

The New York Times, profiling her, said she emerged from the high-flying world of Shanghai finance to work with technology companies and obtain restricted artificial-intelligence chips, and that her ties to Jensen Huang date to at least 2023. Her channel to Chinese buyers ran through Megaspeed, a Singapore-based "neocloud" that supplies computing power.

Fortune, citing The Information, reported that Alice Huang used her personal relationship with the Nvidia chief executive to move at least $7.5bn worth of Nvidia chips through Megaspeed and its Malaysian subsidiary, Speedmatrix. Megaspeed was incorporated in 2015 as 7Road International, a Chinese gaming company with ties to state-backed investors, and took its current name in early 2023, the year Alice Huang joined it. A Singapore-registered firm, Swiftdata, acquired the company in November 2023; Alice Huang stepped down as managing director while the Singaporean businessman Tan Yong Pong, Swiftdata's owner, became its sole director. Tan established Speedmatrix in Malaysia in 2024. The New York Times reported that Speedmatrix received more than 200 shipments of restricted Nvidia technology between June 2024 and June 2025, and that the chips powered data centres built by Shanghai Century Litong, a Chinese firm co-founded by Alice Huang's younger sister, Huang Yu.

Washington bans the export of Nvidia's top-end Blackwell and Rubin chips to China for fear Beijing could use them to accelerate its military AI. Ja Ian Chong of the National University of Singapore told Fortune that Southeast Asian data centres let Chinese firms tap computing capacity without importing restricted semiconductors. The ease of shipping through Singapore and Malaysia, he said, "makes them attractive for firms and people who wish to circumvent U.S. controls."

Singapore said it is investigating Megaspeed for "suspected breaches of our domestic laws," while denying reports that its authorities were examining Alice Huang over the espionage allegations. US officials have also investigated Megaspeed for potential export-control violations. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing.

Nvidia has previously claimed that it found no evidence of illicit chip transfers. Secretary of State Marco Rubio sent a memo to senior officials earlier this year warning of the relationship, according to the New York Times, and Beijing's foreign ministry has rejected the claim that Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence. It was unclear whether Wachtell's investigators would be able to interview Alice Huang.
