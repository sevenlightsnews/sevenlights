The Democratic National Committee announced Monday that it will hold 12 presidential primary debates between June 2027 and March 2028, seating as many as 20 candidates in a contest the party expects to be its largest in a generation.

Participants must accept new conditions. Candidates would pledge to reject financing from outside groups that do not disclose their donors and to take part in no unsanctioned debates, a measure aimed at forums staged by campaign-aligned organizations outside party control.

Qualifying standards will include measures of a candidate's support, among them grassroots fundraising. The committee said it would publish the criteria for the first two debates in January, along with dates, locations and media partners. Because of the size of the field, early debates may run over two consecutive nights, with qualifying candidates assigned at random to one evening. The committee said "creators and influencers" would also be incorporated into the process, without elaborating.

Ken Martin, the party's chair, framed the schedule as momentum rather than housekeeping. "Democrats are on offense after our historic victories in the midterms, and the DNC is keeping our foot on the gas as we head into the 2028 cycle," he said in a statement. "The fair and transparent process and experienced leadership team we're announcing today will keep our party united, strengthen our eventual nominee, and put Democrats in position to take back the White House."

No candidate has declared. The names most frequently raised include Gavin Newsom, the governor of California; Kamala Harris, the former vice-president and 2024 nominee; Josh Shapiro of Pennsylvania; JB Pritzker of Illinois; and Andy Beshear of Kentucky. Supporters of Pete Buttigieg, the former transportation secretary, are beginning a grassroots effort to persuade him to try again, Politico reported.

Early polling points away from the governors. An Emerson College survey put Senator Jon Ossoff of Georgia at 17 percent and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez of New York at 12 percent among Democratic primary voters. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found 29 percent regarded Mr. Buttigieg and Mr. Ossoff as excellent candidates. Hundreds of organizers from a new group, Ready for AOC, held a call this month urging Ms. Ocasio-Cortez to run.

The first debate is set for June 2027. The final round falls in March 2028, as delegates are chosen.
