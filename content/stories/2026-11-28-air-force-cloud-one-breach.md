Hackers broke into Cloud One, the U.S. Air Force's flagship cloud computing platform, and the intrusion was discovered within the past month during upgrade work, according to people familiar with the matter, as first reported by Reuters. The Air Force and the platform's commercial contractors declined to discuss the scope of the breach.

Initiated in 2017, Cloud One was built as a one-stop shop for the Air Force's information-technology needs, combining the resources of four operators — Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet — to power scores of applications. Among them are personnel and pay systems and logistics and maintenance software. Two of the most sensitive are the Air Force's Cloud-Based Command and Control, which collates air-defense radar and other data feeds from across North America, and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks bombs and missiles across more than 200 Air Force locations around the world. The reports did not establish what the intruders accessed or whether classified systems were affected. The Air Force did not confirm the reports.

Security researchers said a breach of a military cloud front end, even confined to unclassified workloads, would be significant because such platforms interconnect with networks that touch operational systems. They pointed to the concentration of defense workloads on a handful of commercial providers as a structural risk: a compromise of one tenant's credentials can expose shared management layers. The reports did not say how the intruders entered or whether the access involved misconfiguration, stolen credentials or a software flaw. The discovery has paused application migrations and blocked updates while U.S. Cyber Command assesses the damage.

Congressional defense and intelligence appropriators have pressed the Pentagon in recent budget cycles over cloud security and the pace of modernization across the services.

The episode follows a series of intrusions into U.S. government and contractor networks this year, including the breach of a software supplier's repository that exposed credentials used by federal agencies and private companies. The Air Force has migrated most of its business systems to commercial cloud environments under contracts awarded over the past decade.

The reports said an investigation was continuing. The Air Force said further disclosure could compromise the inquiry.
