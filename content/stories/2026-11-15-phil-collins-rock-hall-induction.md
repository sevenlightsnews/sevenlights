Phil Collins was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a solo artist at the annual ceremony Saturday night at the Peacock Theater in Los Angeles, sixteen years after his 2010 induction as a member of Genesis. He became a two-time inductee and received a standing ovation.

Travis Barker of Blink-182 delivered the induction speech, paying tribute to Collins's drumming and his success with Genesis and as a solo artist. Barker recalled hearing "In the Air Tonight" for the first time and called its drum break "arguably the most badass drum fill of all time." Cynthia Erivo then sang the 1981 single in a stripped-back arrangement, with Barker on the kit for the famous fill.

Collins, 75, walked to the stage using two canes. "I'm really not as bad as it looks," he told the audience, assuring them his health had improved. He said he had "grown up just wanting to play the drums" and that "everything else has been a really happy accident," and thanked his three former wives and his five children. His daughter, the actress Lily Collins, attended beside him. Closing his remarks, he promised fans: "You haven't heard the last of me. I promise you that."

Collins had ruled out performing at the ceremony earlier this year, saying he was not physically ready for rehearsals and live singing. A spinal injury in 2007 left him with nerve damage that affected his drumming, and he has undergone multiple knee operations. He performed seated on Genesis's final tour in 2021 and 2022, with his son Nic on drums. He has said he is three years sober after an illness in 2024 that left him critically ill and on life support. In accepting, he described himself as "a well man now" and allowed "the possibility of doing some shows."

The ceremony inducted Oasis, Joy Division and New Order, with performances by the Deftones, Lauren Mayberry and Peter Hook. An edited version will air on ABC on December 8 and appear on Disney+ and Hulu the following day.
