Kayla Harrison retained the UFC women's bantamweight championship by submitting Amanda Nunes in the fourth round of UFC 334 on Saturday night at Madison Square Garden.

Harrison, a two-time Olympic judo gold medalist, grabbed Nunes by the right leg, tripped her to the canvas, took her back and applied a rear-naked choke. Nunes tapped at 1:29 of the fourth round, to a startled reaction from the Garden crowd. Nunes, 23-6, spent the first two rounds wrestling on her back against the wrestler, and Harrison landed short punches and elbows that appeared to exhaust her. Nunes had ended a three-year retirement for the bout and later acknowledged difficulty in cutting weight to 135 pounds.

It was Harrison's first title defense and her first fight since June 2025; a spinal fusion operation in January to address a neck injury postponed the contest. Nunes walked out of the octagon under her own power, while Harrison, weakened, required assistance returning to the locker room with the belt around her waist.

"I think I'm still in shock," Harrison said. "I've got a lot of decisions to make when I get home. Every time I make weight, I say never again, so we'll see."

Nunes, widely regarded as the greatest female fighter in the sport's history, had returned to pursue the title Harrison took during her absence. Harrison staked a claim to be the best fighter in the division now.

In the heavyweight bout, Cyril Gane retained the undisputed championship by unanimous decision over Josh Hokit, who is 10-1. Gane took the interim title by defeating Alex Pereira at the June card at the White House and was promoted to undisputed champion in November in Tom Aspinall's absence. The two traded damage in a slugfest that left both bloodied, but neither landed sustained offense, and the judges settled it. Hokit was booed and cheered in equal measure for taunting Gane between exchanges.

Stephen Thompson, 43, a two-time welterweight title challenger, closed his career with a third-round stoppage of Charles Radtke, finishing him with a spinning wheel kick.

Harrison's next defense had not been announced.
