Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, a former state representative from Sitka, won the race for governor of Alaska, with the Associated Press calling the contest early Saturday. A Democrat carried the office for the first time since Tony Knowles left it in 2002. At 37, Kreiss-Tomkins will be the youngest governor in state history and, next year, the youngest sitting governor in the country.

Kreiss-Tomkins, known statewide by his initials, JKT, ran on what he called a "high-energy, bipartisan, problem-solving approach" and described himself at a debate as a "man of the middle." He has said he intends to build an administration of Democrats, Republicans and independents. He campaigned on school funding, lower energy costs, Medicaid expansion and protection of Alaska's fisheries.

The victory completed a two-step consolidation of the vote. Under Alaska's top-four primary, the four highest vote-getters advance to the general election regardless of party. Kreiss-Tomkins led the August primary, and former state Senator Tom Begich, a Democrat who finished second, withdrew soon after to unite the party behind him. Three Republicans remained on the November ballot: Anchorage businesswoman Bernadette Wilson, backed by President Donald Trump; former Anchorage Mayor Dave Bronson; and former state Attorney General Treg Taylor. NBC News projected that Kreiss-Tomkins won a majority in the first round of ranked-choice tabulation, so no instant runoff was needed. Wilson finished a distant second.

Senator Lisa Murkowski, a Republican, endorsed Kreiss-Tomkins a week before Election Day, citing what she called his common sense and ability to work with others. Fox News attributed the Republican loss in part to division among the party's candidates and to the fallout of Trump's backing of Wilson.

The win extended Democrats' gains in this year's state elections. The party will hold 30 governorships next year after flipping six states. Alaska has voted Republican in statewide federal races for decades, and Kreiss-Tomkins takes an office Republicans held for 24 years.

He succeeds term-limited Governor Mike Dunleavy, a Republican who has served since 2018. Kreiss-Tomkins will be sworn in when the new term begins next year.
