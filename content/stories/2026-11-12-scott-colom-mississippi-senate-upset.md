Democrat Scott Colom defeated incumbent Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith of Mississippi on Tuesday, a victory that handed Democrats a seat they had not carried in the state since 1982 and made history in a state that had seemed beyond their reach.

The Associated Press called the race Wednesday. Colom becomes the first Democrat elected to Mississippi's Senate delegation since 1982 and the first Black senator ever chosen directly by the voters of Mississippi. Hiram Rhodes Revels, the nation's first Black senator, was elected by the state legislature from Mississippi in 1870; the last of the Reconstruction-era senators, Blanche K. Bruce, served until 1881.

The win pushes the Democratic majority in the new Senate, which convenes in January, to at least 55 seats and extends a string of Deep South victories in this year's elections that gave the party control of both chambers of Congress.

Colom, a 43-year-old district attorney from Columbus, campaigned on lowering the cost of living, raising wages and protecting rural hospitals. The campaign's signature was a viral advertisement urging Mississippi to pick another senator. He spoke to supporters at Tougaloo College in Jackson on Wednesday, introduced by his wife, Nadia Colom, and by Representative Bennie Thompson. Standing behind a sign reading "Picked Another Senator," he invoked Revels by name: "For people that don't know, the first African American to be a United States senator was from the great state of Mississippi." He began with, "This journey started with a prayer, y'all, and in Mississippi, our prayers have often changed the course of history."

Hyde-Smith appeared to concede shortly after the call in a video message posted online. "While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters," she said, congratulating Colom. Worries about a recount appeared moot; Mississippi has no recount law, and a recount would require a court order.

National figures could not rescue the incumbent. The Hill reported that President Trump himself had campaigned for Hyde-Smith in Alcorn County, where he won by 68 points in 2024. The race was one Republicans expected to survive comfortably in a state they have carried in every statewide contest for a generation. Democratic campaigners, including former President Bill Clinton, had stumped for Colom across the same weeks.

Colom takes his seat when the new Senate convenes in January. Hyde-Smith was appointed to the Senate in 2018 by then-Governor Phil Bryant, won a full term in 2020 by beating Democrat Mike Espy by 10 points, and now leaves after eight years in the seat.

Update, Nov. 13, 2026: Analyses published after the win measured its breadth. Marvin King, a political scientist at the University of Mississippi, told Axios that Democrats gained ground in 75 of the state's 82 counties, with Black turnout a decisive factor. PBS reported that the Associated Press called the contest on Wednesday only after local officials spent several days processing absentee and provisional ballots, known in Mississippi as affidavit ballots, and noted that the last new Democratic senator from the state, elected in 1947, was a staunch segregationist. NPR reported that Colom "defied the odds" in a state Republicans have carried at every level for a generation. Politico reported the win settled the incoming Democratic caucus at 55 seats.
