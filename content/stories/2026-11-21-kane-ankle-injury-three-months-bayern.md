Bayern Munich confirmed Friday that striker Harry Kane will be "out for the next few months" after an examination showed a tear of a ligament in his left ankle. The England captain was substituted after 20 minutes of Friday's 1-1 draw with Cologne in the Bundesliga, limping off after a challenge by Cologne defender Joël Schmied.

Sporting director Max Eberl said club doctors were "discussing whether or not to recommend surgery," and told Sky Germany the injury was a syndesmotic ligament tear. Coach Vincent Kompany, back at the bench after a month away following a collapse in training, said the layoff would be measured in months rather than weeks.

The absence removes Bayern's leading attacker through a crowded stretch. Kane will miss Champions League matches at Lille next week and at home to Slavia Prague next month, the January trip to Manchester United — a meeting with his former Tottenham coach Mauricio Pochettino — and the league-phase finale against Real Betis. Part of the recovery falls in the Bundesliga's winter break from December 20 to January 8.

The timeline also endangers England's spring. The Nations League quarter-finals open away against Norway on March 27, just over four months away; if Eberl's three-month outline holds, Kane faces a race for fitness.

Kane, 33, was plagued by ankle injuries in his later years at Tottenham Hotspur but had largely avoided serious trouble at the ankle since joining Bayern in 2023, a turnaround he has credited with a change of physiotherapists after a hamstring tear on New Year's Day 2020.

The injury deepened Bayern's crisis list. Michael Olise also left Friday's match with a foot contusion, joining Luis Díaz, Serge Gnabry, Alphonso Davies, Ismael Saibari and Manuel Neuer on the sidelines, and Jamaal Musiala was only fit for the bench. Kompany finished the first half with a front four of teenagers and young reserves.

Cologne led after 12 minutes through Tottenham loanee Mikey Moore before Nathaniel Brown equalized before the interval. Kompany called the result fair and said Cologne deserved the point. Bayern remain top of the table but can be overtaken by Borussia Dortmund, who visit Borussia Mönchengladbach Saturday. Cologne rose to 14th, one place off its first win at Bayern since 2009.

A decision on surgery is expected after further consultation.
