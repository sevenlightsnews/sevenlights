US negotiators have raised the possibility of a Ukrainian withdrawal from the Donbas in exchange for Nato membership, President Volodymyr Zelensky said Saturday, following the latest round of peace talks with Russia in Abu Dhabi. Zelensky said it was the first time the question of linking alliance membership to territorial concessions had been put on the table during the negotiations.

"The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins Nato? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?'" Zelensky told reporters in remarks released by his office. "That is why I believe Nato is back on the agenda. But you have also heard Putin's signals, and this is an extremely difficult conversation."

Moscow has long described Ukrainian membership of Nato as a red line and cited the alliance's eastward advance as one reason for its 2022 invasion. Zelensky reiterated that Kyiv was not ready to concede territory. If Ukraine received a package including security guarantees, a well-funded army and funds for reconstruction, he said, he would put the question to a national referendum.

Zelensky also dismissed a Russian demand that a broad agreement on the security architecture of Europe be reached before the specifics of ending the war are negotiated. "We cannot turn everything upside down and start by talking about changing the security framework in the world or across the continent overall, and only then talk about ending this war," he said. "This war must be ended first."

He rejected as well a Russian proposal for a local humanitarian ceasefire in Kostiantynivka, a contested city in the eastern Donetsk region. Zelensky said the offer reflected Moscow's inability to take the city by force. "Putin simply wants us to leave Kostiantynivka so that they can move in and occupy the entire city that way," he said. Ukraine has listed seven or eight other frontline settlements where civilians, including children, are trapped, and proposed humanitarian truces there. Zelensky said Russia preferred to use those places as military targets. Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians.

The two days of US-brokered talks between Ukrainian and Russian delegations concluded in the Emirati capital on Wednesday without a major breakthrough. The sides agreed to a prisoner exchange involving more than 1,000 people, Zelensky said, with final numbers still under discussion. European leaders at a summit in Dublin on Friday echoed Zelensky's rejection of a security-first sequencing, and the territorial question is expected to dominate the next round of negotiations.
