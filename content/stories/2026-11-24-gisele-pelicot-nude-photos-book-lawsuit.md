Gisèle Pelicot is taking legal action against a French publishing house and a psychoanalyst after nude photographs of her, which she says were taken by her husband during years of abuse, were sent to journalists to promote a book about her ordeal. The 73-year-old, who became a symbol of France's reckoning with sexual violence after her testimony drew global attention, told BFM TV on Monday she was "deeply shocked" by the promotional campaign.

Dominique Pelicot was convicted in 2024 of drugging her and inviting dozens of men to rape her while she lay unconscious in Mazan in southern France. He photographed and filmed the assaults; police recovered thousands of images from his computer. Ms. Pelicot said the photographs now in circulation were taken by her then-husband during that abuse.

The publisher Tinbad and the author Sabine Prokhoris sent the images to journalists in recent days to promote a book on coercive control, according to French media reports. "I will not allow my intimate life, my most private images, to be used for commercial purposes," she said. "Once again I find myself humiliated, exposed and hurt."

Ms. Prokhoris is identified in the book's promotional material as examining relationships of psychological domination. The publisher presents the work as an inquiry into coercive control rather than a retelling of the trial.

Tinbad acknowledged that the photographs and video material had been distributed to the press, and said no reprint beyond the initial print run was planned, according to French reports. Lawyers for Ms. Pelicot said the distribution violated her privacy and that proceedings would begin this week.

The dispute renews a question that attended the trial itself: the exposure of Ms. Pelicot's suffering to public view. Her decision to attend court without a face-covering and to speak openly shaped the trial's resonance, and supporters filled the galleries in Avignon. Dominique Pelicot and several co-defendants received sentences ranging from three years to 20 years in prison.

Ms. Pelicot said she would pursue the case "with the same determination" she showed at trial. No hearing date has been set.
