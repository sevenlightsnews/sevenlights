United States equity futures erased steep early losses overnight. The Democratic Party's capture of both chambers of Congress in the midterm elections was judged largely priced into markets that had fallen in the run-up to the vote on concern that the artificial-intelligence buildout would face greater regulatory scrutiny.

Contracts on the S&P 500 fell 0.3 per cent at 2:05 a.m. in New York, having dropped as much as 1.4 per cent earlier. Nasdaq 100 futures slipped 0.4 per cent after a decline that had reached 2.4 per cent, Bloomberg reported. The Wall Street Journal's market coverage recorded the same sequence: futures dropping, then reversing course as investors weighed the sweep.

Shares linked to the artificial-intelligence trade moved separately from the index futures. Tesla and Space Exploration Technologies Corp slipped on the German venue Tradegate in European trading, an early sign that the election outcome may carry consequences for the companies of Elon Musk, the billionaire who emerged as one of the campaign's largest Republican donors.

Betting markets had anticipated the result well before the polls closed. Odds on a Democratic sweep of the federal races reached 99 per cent in the run-up to voting, Forbes reported, leaving little surprise for portfolio managers to absorb at night.

The result installs a divided federal government for the final two years of the president's term. Democrats take command of both chambers when the new Congress convenes on January 3, while Mr Trump retains the veto. Democrats have said their legislation will target Medicaid cuts, subsidies under the Affordable Care Act, congressional authority over tariffs and the war with Iran; tariffs and health spending both feed directly into earnings and inflation expectations.

Attention now shifts to corporate results, with a fresh round of technology earnings due this week to test whether the artificial-intelligence spending cycle can outrun a friendlier-looking but still divided regulatory outlook. Cash equity markets open on Wednesday under a Democratic Congress for the first time since 2023.

**Update, November 5, 2026:** In the first full session after the vote, US stocks surged and the dollar slid. The Dow Jones rose close to 3 per cent, its best day in more than a year, while the Nasdaq and the S&P 500 gained about 1.5 per cent each at Wednesday's close, turning around the overnight futures falls. Investors bet the result would restrain the president's turbulent policymaking, and initial fears about growth eased. The dollar fell to a one-month low against major trading partners' currencies as investors moved into Treasuries against anticipated gridlock; the 10-year yield dropped 4.7 basis points to 4.918 per cent. Brent crude fell below $100 a barrel for the first time since early October as traders assessed the Democrats' win's impact on the war with Iran. The president took credit on social media: "Stock Market just hit another Record High. Your 401(k)s are through the roof."
