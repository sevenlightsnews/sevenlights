The Princess of Wales discusses her cancer diagnosis and treatment in a 30-minute authorized documentary, "Heal," scheduled to be broadcast by the BBC on Sunday. The film offers the most detailed public account yet of the illness she announced in March 2024, following major abdominal surgery.

The princess, 44, said in a statement accompanying the film that the work was intended to reframe how illness is understood, presenting recovery as a process rather than a single outcome. The documentary intercuts an interview with visits to hospitals, patient support groups and art-therapy sessions, and includes footage of meetings with clinicians and caregivers.

The most extended sequence follows the Three Peaks challenge the princess undertook in June, an attempt to climb Ben Nevis in Scotland, Scafell Pike in England and Yr Wyddfa in Wales within 24 hours. The film shows rain and hill fog on the slopes before the weather cleared. The challenge raised funds for the Royal Marsden hospital, where she was treated. Her brother, James Middleton, has spoken publicly about the family's part in sustaining her through treatment.

The princess also addresses the effect of the illness on her children, Prince George, Princess Charlotte and Prince Louis. The film includes recorded voice messages from the three children, played during the course of the programme.

Catherine announced her diagnosis after surgery in January 2024 and said in September of that year that treatment had concluded. In January 2025 she announced she was in remission. She has since returned to a full schedule of royal engagements, and the film's production was announced this month.

Royal documentaries are closely supervised affairs, and the decision to place the princess's treatment on the record marks a further step in the couple's use of broadcast media. The film runs half an hour and was produced for television rather than cinema. The BBC said it would be shown without commercial interruption, and clips released this week show the princess speaking directly to camera in a sitting room setting.

The programme's broadcasters have framed it as an account of living with and after cancer rather than a chronicle of the illness itself. It is expected to be discussed when the prince and princess attend hospital engagements in the coming weeks.
