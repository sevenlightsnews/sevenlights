Apple released the second iOS 27.3 beta to developers on Monday, adding a feature called "Unlock with Front Camera" that lets iPhone Duo owners unlock the foldable phone with facial recognition. The option appears in the Touch ID and Passcode settings. It is not Face ID.

The iPhone Duo shipped with Touch ID rather than Face ID because its thin, folding design, with a hinge and multiple display layers, leaves no room for the TrueDepth sensor array that maps a face in three dimensions. Reviewers ranked the omission among the phone's chief complaints. The Duo, the most expensive model in the current iPhone line, carries ordinary cameras on its outer and inner screens instead, and Touch ID has handled unlocking, authentication and Apple Pay.

Apple's setup screen marks the distinction plainly, warning that the feature "provides convenience" but "isn't as secure as Touch ID or Face ID." The new unlock works only when the phone is closed and only for users who have recently authenticated with Touch ID or a passcode; payments and security-sensitive tasks stay behind the fingerprint sensor. Engadget, testing the beta on a Duo, reported the feature unlocked faster than Touch ID, was not fooled when a photograph of the owner's face was held up to the camera, and worked in some low-light conditions though not in complete darkness.

The update also removes a workaround early adopters had devised to force the phone to lock when folded, a shortcut that ran automatically on closing. Apple now offers the behaviour directly in the settings under Display and Brightness.

Bloomberg has reported that Apple is working to fit the missing sensor hardware to a future version of the Duo. The beta feature answers the complaint without waiting for hardware. Apple gives no date for shipping the option to users outside the developer programme, and the company's documentation continues to steer security-sensitive tasks to Touch ID.
