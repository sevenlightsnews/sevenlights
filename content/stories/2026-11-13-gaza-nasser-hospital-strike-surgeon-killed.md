![Damaged five-story hospital building at night with shattered windows, emergency workers with flashlights carrying stretchers across a rubble-strewn courtyard, ambulance light bars glowing, photorealistic, 3:2](/images/stories/gaza-nasser-hospital-strike-surgeon-killed.webp)

An Israeli missile strike on the al-Yasin surgical building at Nasser hospital in Khan Younis killed six people shortly after midnight on Thursday, including a general surgeon, and wounded about 30, Gaza's Health Ministry said.

The hospital's director, Dr. Ahmed al-Fara, told a news conference that Israeli forces had fired two missiles at the building and that only one exploded. Among the dead was Dr. Rizq Abu Shaira, 52, a general surgeon who had appeared in "American Doctor," a documentary about physicians operating in Gaza. He was killed alongside one of his patients, on whom he had just operated.

The ministry said medical staff, patients and companions of the wounded were among the dead. A patient who had been lying in a bed near Abu Shaira said the surgeon had been "the kindest person in this department."

Al-Fara said the strike forced the suspension of four departments: radiology, the laboratory, the surgical operating rooms and an inpatient surgical ward. Nasser is the largest hospital in southern Gaza and serves almost one million people in the Khan Younis area.

Israel's military said it had targeted a Hamas commander and several operatives who used the hospital as a headquarters, and said it had taken measures to limit civilian casualties. It named the commander of Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade, Mohammed Abu Ismail, as a target, and released an audio recording of what it said was an advance warning given to a senior Gaza health official. Dr. al-Fara said the hospital received no warning. Hamas denied the site was used for military purposes and called the allegations a "blatant attempt to cover up a new war crime."

The strike was the deadliest single attack in Gaza since Israeli elections last month, France 24 reported. Dr. Ayman Abu Ubaid, a surgical specialist wounded in the attack, said the bombing filled the rooms with thick smoke and set fires. A surgery specialist, Dr. Mohammed Qdeih, said he was trapped under rubble before crawling free.

Nasser has been struck seven times during the war. A double strike in August 2025 killed 22 people, including journalists and health workers. The World Health Organization says it has documented 1,056 attacks on health care in Gaza over three years, killing 1,010 people.
