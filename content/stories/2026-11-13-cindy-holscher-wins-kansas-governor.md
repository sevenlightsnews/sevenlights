State Senator Cindy Holscher won the Kansas race for governor, the Associated Press projected on Thursday at 5:43 p.m. Eastern time, defeating Ty Masterson, the president of the State Senate, by 50.3 percent to 49.7 percent after two days of counting.

Holscher declared victory on Tuesday, before the call, as absentee and provisional ballots narrowed the margin to less than one point. "Kansans have sent a clear message that they want to keep our state moving forward," she said.

She campaigned on halting the construction of new data centers in the state, a pledge that resonated in farming communities wary of industrial development. Masterson, the Senate president, built his campaign on property tax cuts, and Republicans outraised and outspent her throughout the race.

The result keeps the executive mansion in Democratic hands and extends the party's strong year. Holscher will succeed Laura Kelly, a Democrat who was term-limited, and Masterson remains president of the Senate. Kansas voted for President Trump by 16 points in 2024, and Republicans had expected to hold the office. Adam Hamilton won the state's other marquee contest this month, a United States Senate seat last carried by a Kansas Democrat in 1932, and Chris Mann unseated the incumbent attorney general, Kris Kobach. Democrats had already captured a majority of American governorships for the first time in two decades before the Kansas race was called.

Democratic strategists said the pattern — down-ballot candidates winning statewide in a deeply red state on cost-of-living and land-use issues — tested the assumption that the party's path to power runs only through the suburbs. Republican officials did not immediately contest the count.

The projection ended a two-day count that had kept the race within a point since Tuesday's election. Masterson had held a lead through most of the early returns as in-person ballots broke for him before absentee counties reported.
