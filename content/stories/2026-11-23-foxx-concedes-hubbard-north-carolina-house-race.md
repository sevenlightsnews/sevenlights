Rep. Virginia Foxx, a Republican, conceded her race in North Carolina's Fifth Congressional District on Sunday to Democrat Chuck Hubbard, a former journalist who won the seat after a recount in one of the closest contests of the midterm cycle.

CBS News projected the race for Hubbard 19 days after Election Day. The Hill's nonpartisan election-results partner, Decision Desk HQ, also projected Hubbard. Foxx phoned Hubbard to concede on Sunday, after a recount had been conducted in the tight race.

Hubbard, 64, challenged Foxx for a second time; he fell short by almost 10 points in 2024. The Cook Political Report had rated the seat as likely Republican going into the election. He became the latest Democrat to overcome expectations and flip a House seat.

Foxx has represented the state in the House since 2005 and chairs the Rules Committee. She is the longest-serving member of North Carolina's congressional delegation.

"Let me be the first to thank Congresswoman Foxx for her decades of service and countless sacrifices," Hubbard wrote in a post on X. "I deeply appreciate her commitment to a smooth transition of the office and constituent casework. We may not always agree, but we both love North Carolina and the people of the Fifth District."

The victory completed a strong night for North Carolina Democrats, who flipped several House seats in the state and a Senate seat. Nationally, Democrats secured 258 seats in the House, the largest majority either party has won in the lower chamber in a general election in three decades. Hubbard takes his seat in January, when Democrats take control of both chambers of the new Congress.

The delay reflected the margin and the recount that followed it. The Fifth District was among the last seats in the House to be settled, with the projection coming nearly three weeks after the vote. Lawmakers had already left Washington for the Thanksgiving recess, with the new Congress set to be sworn in in January. Foxx did not immediately comment.
