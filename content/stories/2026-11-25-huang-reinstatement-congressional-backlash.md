![Columned office building of the US Capitol at early morning, bare winter trees along Constitution Avenue, a lone commuter walking beneath cast-iron lampposts, pale diffuse light, no readable text, editorial photojournalism photograph, 3:2](/images/stories/huang-reinstatement-congressional-backlash.webp)

Nvidia's board voted unanimously on Monday to restore Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and board member, one week after saying it did not intend to reinstate him, and the reversal drew demands for answers from lawmakers of both parties on Tuesday.

The reinstatement followed a public pressure campaign by President Donald Trump, who urged the board to bring Mr. Huang back. Senator John Cornyn of Texas, the ranking Republican on the Intelligence Committee, asked in a social media post: "No national security risk? Based on what." Senator John Curtis of Utah said the board's change of course required explanation. "Last week, the board said it didn't intend to reinstate him. I'd like to know what changed," he said. Nvidia's board has said it relied on input from senior US officials in concluding that Mr. Huang's return posed no national security risk, a claim Curtis said he wanted examined.

Senator Kevin Cramer of North Dakota said the risk of extortion "by a Chinese asset" barred from Singapore was "risk enough to not rehire" Mr. Huang, whose conduct the board had earlier described as lacking candor. Senator Pete Ricketts of Nebraska cited Mr. Huang's affairs, including one with a woman identified by US intelligence as a likely Chinese spy, and said his behavior "does not support reinstatement." Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich called the return "a national security issue of enormous proportions," adding: "There is something profoundly wrong" about it.

The House Intelligence, Foreign Affairs and China Select committees announced that Mr. Huang would testify next week. An Intelligence Committee spokesperson said his reinstatement "changes nothing," and that the committees' questions on national security implications remained open. Mr. Huang is scheduled to appear before the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Democrats would open investigations into the reinstatement, and into Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's role, when they take control of the chamber in January. He called for "ironclad export control legislation" to be written into the annual defense authorization bill. Senator Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the Banking Committee, called the decision "reckless" and said the board still owed Congress answers on the company's compliance with US export controls.

Rep. Robert Garcia, top Democrat on the Oversight Committee, said no president "who has invested in a private company should be involved in their board decisions."

Mr. Huang addressed employees on Tuesday, telling them he was "thankful" for a second chance, according to a recording of the meeting. Nvidia shares rose more than 30 per cent on Monday, the largest one-day gain for a single company on record.

Committee staff were setting the terms of next week's testimony.
