Anthropic raised a record $93.5 billion in its initial public offering this week, with technology giants buying an additional $26 billion at the offer price, and its shares gained 17 per cent on Thursday's debut, lifting the artificial intelligence developer's market capitalisation to $1.8 trillion. By week's end the figure had eased to about $1.6 trillion on a fully diluted basis, still placing the six-year-old company behind only six others in the United States by value.

The debut arrived in the middle of an AI-driven market slide. Led by Nvidia's 23 per cent weekly plunge, its sharpest weekly pullback in 18 years, the Nasdaq fell 10 per cent over five trading days, its worst week since the early days of the coronavirus market in 2020. The listing and the selloff together laid bare the distance between Silicon Valley, which treated the offering as a vindication, and Wall Street, which spent the week questioning whether AI spending can support such valuations.

Venture backers, who poured $141 billion into the company as of September according to its prospectus, measured the week in returns. Menlo Ventures and Lightspeed Venture Partners each held about $25 billion of stock at the offer price; Bessemer Venture Partners held a stake worth roughly $10 billion. A founding partner of Tenacity Venture Capital, which did not invest, said he was planning a celebration at his home this month, calling the offering the most significant event in venture capital's history.

For the industry, the offering marked the reopening of an exit window that had been largely shut. Venture-backed IPOs raised $73 billion in 2021; over the following four years the total was $39 billion, according to data from the University of Florida's Jay Ritter. The number of technology companies going public fell from 126 in 2021 to 34 in 2025, as private funding grew large enough to keep companies private longer.

Investors and analysts spent the week debating whether Anthropic will trade alongside AI hardware companies, on whose chips and data centres it depends, or stand apart as the first public pure-play developer of AI models. Friday's trading suggested scepticism about its ability to grow into its valuation. The question will follow the stock into the holiday week.

Update, November 24, 2026: Bond investors are pricing Anthropic's debt below investment grade. The company has committed approximately $900.9 billion to computing power and infrastructure through 2045, and investors expect credit rating agencies to assign a speculative-grade rating, reflecting doubts that cash flow will cover the commitments. Separately, philanthropic organizations aligned with the company's founders are preparing for a sharp rise in giving. Coefficient Giving, the vehicle founded by Chief Science Officer Jared Kaplan, has directed more than $8 billion since 2014 and raised its commitment to the grantmaker GiveWell to $1 billion in July, a step its chief executive, Alexander Berger, attributed to expectations of "considerably more future funding."
