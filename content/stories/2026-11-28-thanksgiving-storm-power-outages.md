Roughly 490,000 customers remained without electricity Friday afternoon after coastal storms and an Arctic front battered the Northeast and Midwest on Thanksgiving, according to the tracking site poweroutage.us. Nearly 201,000 of those outages were in New Jersey and Pennsylvania. The count had fallen from a peak above 850,000 on Thursday, when the system knocked out power across the mid-Atlantic and the Midwest.

The storms grounded aircraft, flooded coastal towns and postponed the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York for the first time in its history, moving the procession to Saturday. At Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport, heavy rain and wind halted flights Thursday, and airlines reported more than 2,400 delays and 140 cancellations Friday after more than 2,200 delays and 400 cancellations the day before.

New Jersey officials described the worst flooding in the coastal counties since Hurricane Sandy in 2012. Tidal flooding persisted into Friday as an unusually high tide met storm surge along the shore. In Missouri, 7.3 inches of snow fell around St. Louis, and the roof of an ice arena in Wentzville collapsed under the weight of snow and ice; no injuries were reported. In New York City, a section of construction scaffolding collapsed during high winds, and no one was hurt. A body found along the Staten Island shoreline was being investigated, and officials said a connection to the storm had not been established.

The system drove an Arctic front deep into the South, bringing hard freezes to the Deep South after the rain and wind. "The south and south-east US will start with very cold morning temperatures today and Saturday as lows drop into the upper 20s and the middle 30s," the National Weather Service said, with freeze headlines in effect for portions of the deep south. Forecasters said the cold air mass would linger, keeping temperatures below normal across much of the interior states into next week.

Restoration crews worked into the weekend across New Jersey and Long Island, where flooded substations and frozen ground complicated repairs to the hardest-hit rural circuits.
