Indonesia's Vice-President Gibran Rakabuming Raka formally resigned on Thursday, handing his resignation to the upper house of parliament in the midst of a dispute over his academic credentials that has embroiled the country's highest offices.

Ahmad Muzani, speaker of the upper house, said Gibran had also submitted a resignation letter to President Prabowo Subianto. Parliamentary leaders were to convene to discuss the next steps, Muzani said.

Under Indonesia's constitution a vice-presidential vacancy does not trigger a new election. The office remains vacant until the People's Consultative Assembly, the country's highest legislative body, elects a replacement from two candidates nominated by the president within 60 days.

The crisis centres on Gibran's high school diploma. His eligibility to hold office had been contested in a long-running dispute over his schooling papers, a question that gained traction this year and shadowed every stage of his tenure. ABC reported that the absence of a high school diploma brought down the vice-president, the eligibility question having shadowed his time in office. The New York Times reported the resignation formally, without immediate comment from either principal.

The Economist said the resignation escalates a destabilising feud between the two most powerful figures in Indonesian politics, a rupture with uncertain consequences for a presidency that has depended on the support of the previous administration's network.

Gibran had not publicly acknowledged the resignation before Thursday's submission. Neither the presidency nor the upper house announced a timetable for selecting a successor, and Prabowo made no immediate statement naming candidates.

The assembly's 60-day clock now begins, though Indonesian politics has often run past such deadlines. Lawmakers will have to weigh the political cost of anointing a nominee acceptable to Prabowo and to a legislature in which his rivals retain substantial influence. The vice-presidency has remained vacant before for extended periods, and the longer the office stays empty, the more the feud is likely to dominate the legislative agenda.
