Oliver Glasner said standards at Nottingham Forest were not high enough when he arrived and questioned why some of his players cannot speak English after years in England, a day before his team host Chelsea with one point separating them from the relegation zone.

"Stability in a company is having standards," Glasner said. "Everybody who joins commits to the standards. I think, very honestly, the standards were not high enough. We have players who are here for four years, can't speak English. If you go to Italy, after three months, every player gives his interview in the Italian language."

Glasner applied the rule to himself. "For me, if I want to manage in England, I have to speak English. If I want to manage in Italy, I have to speak Italian. Otherwise, Oliver Glasner, if you want to speak in your dialect, stay at home in Austria, everybody understands you, but nowhere else."

Forest have won two of their first eleven Premier League matches and are one place above the bottom three after successive 2-0 away defeats at Brentford and Bournemouth. They narrowly avoided relegation last season, though they also reached the Europa League semi-finals, and have worked under a succession of managers in recent years.

Glasner, 52, declined to blame that upheaval for the problems he has inherited but said standards must rise. "We want to create a stable Nottingham Forest team that can consistently play in the top half of the Premier League," he said. "We are a few steps away from this but we really believe we know which steps we have to take. We are still very ambitious but it looks to take a little bit longer than we all hoped. We knew this is a transition season."

The manager said he had held talks with the club's ownership about reinforcement in the January transfer window, as the club looks to add players who fit both the level and the long-term plan.

Forest finished last season four points above relegation. The Chelsea match on Friday opens a December run against sides above them in the table. Glasner said the club's goal remained "getting stability into this club," adding: "We're still working on this."
