Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has moved from his home in Washington to a military base in the surrounding region because of threats to his life linked to Iran, officials and a person familiar with the situation said.

The relocation followed the publication of a Wall Street Journal article whose photograph allowed the location of Bessent's Georgetown home to be geo-located, a source told CBS. Security concerns began to climb after the image circulated, the source said, and authorities moved the secretary to a military base in the area. Georgetown is an affluent quarter of the capital that houses numerous government officials.

The severity and nature of the threat against Bessent remained unclear. US intelligence has warned that Iran has "substantially increased" efforts to target Americans, including US officials, in retaliation for the confrontation between the two countries this year. Bessent has stood at the centre of the economic campaign against Tehran, designing sanctions and the wider blockade intended to isolate Iran financially.

A Treasury Department spokesperson declined to discuss the secretary's movements, saying only that the department takes the safety of its officials seriously and coordinates with security agencies. The Pentagon and the base authorities did not identify the installation or confirm housing arrangements.

The Hill first reported the move, citing the person familiar with the situation, who said security concerns regarding the secretary had intensified in recent weeks. CBS reported the relocation was connected to Iranian threats among other safety worries.

The episode underlines a widening threat picture on the domestic front. Iranian plots against US persons, including former officials, have been prosecuted in American courts in recent years, and intelligence officials have repeatedly testified that Tehran views retired and serving officials alike as legitimate targets. Cabinet secretaries receive protective details as a matter of course; a relocation to a military installation is an unusual step.

Bessent continued his public schedule this week, including appearances on the sanctions file. Officials said no new threat had been announced against him since the move, and the review of his security posture was ongoing.
