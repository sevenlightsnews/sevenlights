Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland announced Thursday that he will challenge Chuck Schumer for the leadership of the Senate Democrats, arguing "we need new leadership" days after the party's sweeping midterm victory. "This is not about any one person — it's about answering the call of the American people," Van Hollen said. Schumer answered that "I have the votes to continue serving as Democratic leader."

The contest splits a caucus that some members credit to its leader for a historic blue wave and others say elected in spite of him. Van Hollen, a senator since 2017, has disputed any leadership claim on the results, arguing the wave was powered by voters rather than by leaders in Washington.

Schumer holds the publicly declared endorsements of 14 senators, including himself, against five for Van Hollen, according to Axios. A candidate needs 28 or 29 votes, the figure depending on the outcome of the Alaska Senate race, where votes are still being counted. Van Hollen told MS NOW he has "more than 10" senators on his side. Four have endorsed him publicly: Senator Bernie Sanders of Vermont, who endorsed Thursday night; senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan, who joined when the challenge was filed; Senator Jeff Merkley of Oregon, Friday afternoon; and incoming Senator Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota, Friday morning.

Senator Elissa Slotkin of Michigan has called for change without endorsing either candidate. Senator Peter Welch of Vermont said Democrats "absolutely are committed to change" but did not rule out supporting Schumer. Senators-elect Adam Hamilton of Kansas, Juliana Stratton of Illinois, Troy Jackson of Maine, Annie Andrews of South Carolina and Chris Pappas of New Hampshire have also called for new leadership.

Progressive critics say Schumer has fought the president's agenda too softly. His role in advancing a Republican-backed government funding bill in March 2025 sharpened the backlash; the Working Families Party urged him not to seek another term in a letter first reported by Axios, and a labor group has endorsed Van Hollen. Senator Angus King of Maine, an independent who caucuses with the Democrats, captured the counterargument: "Why would you fire the manager when you just won the World Series?"

The caucus vote has no announced date. Its threshold will not be final until Alaska's count is certified, leaving both camps lobbying through the lame-duck session.
