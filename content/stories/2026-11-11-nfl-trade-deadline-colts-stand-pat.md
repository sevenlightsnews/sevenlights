The National Football League trade deadline passed at 4 p.m. Tuesday with 23 of the league's 32 teams completing 18 trades that moved 19 players and 34 draft picks, and the Indianapolis Colts emerged as the week's conspicuous non-mover: they held running back Jonathan Taylor despite multiple offers.

Pro Football Talk reported the Colts declined to trade Mr. Taylor despite several proposals before the cutoff. Yahoo Sports placed the Colts at the head of its list of the deadline's three biggest losers. The Athletic judged the decision defensible only on a delay: "Indianapolis kept its star running back ahead of the trade deadline, but revisiting Taylor's market in the offseason remains the smart play."

The Colts were active on other fronts. San Francisco obtained defensive tackle DeForest Buckner for a 2027 fourth-round pick and wide receiver Keenan Allen for a 2027 sixth-rounder, shoring up its two weakest positions at minimal cost, CBS Sports reported. Indianapolis, at 2-7 and out of contention, also dealt wide receiver Josh Downs to Kansas City, where Patrick Mahomes had the league's most limited passing attack.

The deadline's biggest spenders were contenders. Jacksonville surrendered a 2027 first-round pick, a 2027 second, two 2028 picks and a 2029 second-round pick for defensive tackle Jordan Davis of the Eagles, safety Jessie Bates III of the Falcons and defensive tackle Davon Godchaux of the Saints. The Jaguars, fourth in scoring offense and second in scoring defense, judged the AFC's top seeds beatable and went accordingly.

Minnesota sent a 2027 third-round pick to Las Vegas for quarterback Kirk Cousins after Carson Wentz's 8-of-25 passing disaster on Monday night. Mr. Cousins, 38, ranks second in the league with 20 touchdown passes this season and shares the best quarterback-receiver pairing in history by yards per game with Justin Jefferson. Tennessee traded All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons to Chicago for two first-round picks; Philadelphia sent Jordan Davis to Jacksonville. Three future first-round picks changed hands in all, part of 11 traded since 2019.

The Colts' roster now turns to the offseason, when The Athletic and others expect Taylor's market to be tested again. Jacksonville, Minnesota and San Francisco will learn this winter whether their outlays hold.
