Shane Gillis addressed the reaction to his Saturday Night Live monologue on The Joe Rogan Experience on Friday, saying he told the show's staff ahead of the broadcast that it would contain nothing objectionable. The set, in which the comedian called women who wear workout clothes to the gym "whores," drew criticism from figures including Dan Levy and prompted coverage on the show's British edition.

Gillis said the program's staff asked for the gist of his remarks before air but do not write or approve a comedian's monologue. "Not to defend SNL, they don't approve it. I mean, they could shut it down," he said. "But you don't write your set and give it to them." Staff-written monologues are the norm for non-comedian hosts, with Taylor Swift's self-penned 2009 appearance a notable exception. He recounted the pre-broadcast exchange: asked what the monologue held, he said he answered, "It's not great but I don't say anything bad."

His own review was unsparing. Gillis called the set "a solid C minus of a monologue," and noted that its first ten minutes rested on self-deprecation before turning to the gym material that drew the complaint. "I'm calling myself fat for fucking 10 minutes. That's it," he said of his promise to the show.

The criticism was not the only friction from the broadcast. Gillis also said an expletive during the live show, an incident the monologue's reception has largely displaced in public memory.

The exchange illustrates the narrow vetting that surrounds the program's opening monologue when the host is a stand-up comedian. Producers solicit a summary of the material, but the writing, and the consequences, remain the host's. Gillis placed the responsibility on himself and declined to characterize the jokes as a bid for offense, describing them instead as a failed routine.

No further response came from the show or the network. The comedian's remarks on the podcast stand as his only public accounting of the episode.
