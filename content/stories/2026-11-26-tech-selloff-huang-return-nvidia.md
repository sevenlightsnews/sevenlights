Nvidia shares fell almost six per cent by Tuesday's close as the euphoria over Jensen Huang's reinstatement as chief executive dissipated one day after a 30 per cent surge that added more than $900 billion to the chipmaker's market value.

The artificial-intelligence company Anthropic also slumped, giving up about 6.5 per cent to $81.90, below the $83 price of its initial public offering last week. By the end of trading in New York the Nasdaq had shed 1.48 per cent and the S&P 500 lost 0.5 per cent, while the Dow ended fractionally higher. Europe's main markets closed a handful of percentage points lower after jumping on Monday in line with Wall Street; the FTSE 100 closed down 0.13 per cent.

Huang resigned less than two weeks ago over extramarital affairs, reported to have been with a senior Nvidia executive and with a woman described in some reports as an alleged Chinese spy. President Trump then noisily declared he should be brought back, and Nvidia's board reinstated him on a unanimous vote. Huang apologized to staff on Tuesday in a company meeting, according to accounts of the session.

"Jensen's return takes away one of the biggest unknowns hanging over the AI trade," said Charu Chanana, an investment strategist at Saxo Markets. "He is not just Nvidia's CEO; he is arguably the biggest salesman for the entire AI ecosystem. His relationships with customers, suppliers and capital providers are difficult to replicate."

But "Jensen Huang alone cannot support the AI trade in the long term," said Kathleen Brooks, research director at XTB. She called Tuesday's decline "to be expected after such a large rally in the previous session."

Analysts were broadly agreed that the reinstatement settles neither the investigations nor the doubts that have clouded the sector for six months. Nvidia remains under scrutiny by investigators at the Federal Bureau of Investigation and in Congress over allegations that it helped Chinese firms obtain its top-line chips, and the Justice Department's interest has drawn comment from Vice President JD Vance. Monday aside, AI stocks had sold off sharply through November on worries about profitability and the vast sums flowing into the sector.

Some Silicon Valley figures who promote diversity and inclusion called the reinstatement unfathomable, saying it signaled that money outweighs conduct.

Traders now await earnings and congressional hearings for a reading of the sector's course.
