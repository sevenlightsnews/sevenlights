California's investigation into whether Nvidia illegally sold advanced chips to China and misled investors about the conduct now covers potential criminal violations of state law, Attorney General Rob Bonta said Friday, raising the stakes for the world's largest chip designer.

Bonta said a variety of California laws could have been broken if Nvidia or former chief executive Jensen Huang gave false information to the public about such sales. The inquiry, opened with an investigative subpoena served this week, examines whether Nvidia's or Mr. Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information. It remains in a preliminary stage and has reached no conclusions, Bloomberg reported.

"Companies cannot mislead their investors," Bonta said Thursday, describing Nvidia as "in a messy, transnational situation" against a clear principle of state corporate securities law. Nvidia has its global headquarters in Santa Clara, California, placing the state's attorneys within reach of the company's records.

The probe follows the disclosure earlier this month that Mr. Huang had an extramarital relationship with Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy who was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data centre company and a major Nvidia customer. Members of Congress have urged the Commerce Department to investigate whether Megaspeed diverted Nvidia chips to China. Megaspeed has said the reports contain assertions "not supported by verifiable evidence."

Mr. Huang resigned as chief executive last week after acknowledging relationships with subordinates. Board chair Harvey Jones said this week the company had no intention of reinstating its co-founder, after Mr. Huang said he would be "honoured" to return and some technology leaders campaigned on his behalf. Nvidia shares have fallen 22 percent since The New York Times reported on the affair in early November.

The California inquiry compounds regulatory pressure on the company. The Securities and Exchange Commission is separately investigating Nvidia's accounting practices, the Financial Times reported, adding to the difficulties facing the chip group since Mr. Huang's departure. Nvidia reported soaring sales this month despite the upheaval.

California's announcement makes explicit that the state is weighing criminal, not merely civil, exposure for the company and its former chief executive. The investigation's next step is expected to turn on documents produced under the subpoena.
