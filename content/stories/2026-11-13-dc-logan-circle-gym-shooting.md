![Urban crime scene on a wet November morning at a city intersection, police tape in front of a brick townhouse with a ground-floor fitness studio, patrol cars with glowing light bars, detectives in raincoats, grey dawn light, photorealistic, 3:2](/images/stories/dc-logan-circle-gym-shooting.webp)

A physician who lived above a fitness studio in the Logan Circle neighborhood shot and killed a 46-year-old building manager and wounded four others shortly before 8 a.m. Thursday about a mile from the White House, then killed himself during a standoff with negotiators, the interim police chief said.

The interim chief of the Metropolitan Police Department, Jeffrey Carroll, identified the gunman as Matthew Lambert, 55, an emergency room physician for two decades who had more recently worked in health information technology. Lambert had been locked in a long-running conflict with the homeowners association of the building that houses the VIDA Fitness studio.

The dead man was James Stuart, the building's manager, who was shot inside the gym. Carroll said investigators believe he was specifically targeted because of the gunman's dispute with the association. Four other victims were hospitalized: one in the building's lobby or alley, and three members of a construction crew working across the street. Carroll said the four hospitalized victims were shot after Lambert had retreated to his seventh-floor apartment and fired at them from its patio balcony. The initial attack unfolded within about five minutes.

Lambert barricaded himself inside his apartment, fired through the apartment door at police on two occasions, and spoke with negotiators for about an hour before shooting himself while still on the telephone, Carroll said. Investigators recovered a shotgun, a rifle and a handgun, and believe he used all three. A fire broke out inside the building during the incident; Carroll said Lambert had broken the gas line in his apartment's fireplace and that gasoline and other flammable materials were found in the unit.

Joel Braithwaite, a lawyer who represented Lambert in his dispute with the building's management, said he was shocked. "The person I knew as a client, Dr. Matt Lambert, was an emergency room physician, someone that was in the business of healing people," Braithwaite said. He said Lambert had bought his unit about a decade ago and was to start a new job on Monday.

Police said most of the negotiation centered on Lambert's conflicts with the association. Officers ordered residents of surrounding blocks to shelter in place during the standoff, and the F.B.I. assisted the department.
