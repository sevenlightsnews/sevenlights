Tidal said Tuesday it will ban and remove songs generated entirely by artificial intelligence, beginning 9 December, in a bid to stem a flood of submissions to the streaming platform. The company expects the process to delete more than 29 million songs from its library by the end of the year.

Over the past 90 days, more than 43 per cent of tracks uploaded to the platform were generated by AI, Tidal said, and that share drove the decision. Under the new rules the company expects to reject roughly 80,000 to 85,000 tracks a day. Tidal will also bar distribution services from delivering fully AI-generated tracks, and will reject submissions whose metadata declares the music made wholly with AI. Music made with AI assistance, but not fully generated, will still be accepted.

The company said 27 million tracks, or 6.3 per cent of its library, are fully AI-generated, and that 96 per cent of those have no streams. "It's true that not all songs catch on immediately, and artists should have time to build their fanbases. But this situation is different — at this scale, the authentic music and artists that Tidal subscribers are looking to listen to and discover get buried," the company said in a post.

Tidal began de-monetizing songs made with AI in June, but that step did not deter the volume, said Robert Anderson, who leads Tidal Music.

The move sets Tidal alongside Bandcamp, which has banned AI music outright, and goes further than Deezer, which offers detection tools and will introduce a toggle letting users filter out AI music. Larger platforms including Spotify and YouTube Music have adopted a milder course, labeling AI-generated tracks rather than removing them or offering filters.

The decision lands while record labels pursue a different path, striking deals with platforms and AI model makers to license catalogs for training or remixing. Goldman Sachs has estimated that AI-related licensing and new monetization could lift record labels' revenue by 10 per cent by 2035.

Tidal's removals begin 9 December, with the purge of the existing library to be completed by year's end.
