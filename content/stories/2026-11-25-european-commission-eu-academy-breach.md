The European Commission confirmed on Monday that personal data was stolen from its EU Academy online training platform in the cyberattack it detected on 22 October, one in which the intruders used an AI-assisted hacking tool.

Spokesperson Thomas Regnier told a press briefing that the investigation had uncovered a breach of the EU Academy, an e-learning platform used by people outside the Commission to take courses on European Union policies. The compromised information includes the names and email addresses of EU Academy users, and the full extent of the theft is still being investigated. Access to the platform has been suspended and the European Data Protection Supervisor has been notified.

Affected individuals have not been informed directly. "The exact scale of the data leak is still subject to the investigation, so there was no reach-out to individual users, but rather a general message to all users on the website out of transparency," Mr. Regnier said.

The Commission first publicly acknowledged the intrusion last Thursday, nearly a month after suspicious activity was detected. It disclosed then that hackers had breached its systems using the ARTEX AI-powered penetration testing framework, causing service disruptions that also affected the internal Sysper human resources platform. Several internal applications were taken offline as a protective measure; most have been restored, though some remained unavailable at the time of Thursday's briefing. The Commission said at that stage it had found no evidence that personal data of Commission staff had been compromised.

Mr. Regnier said Monday that the Commission had received no ransom demand and that attribution could be made only once the investigation concluded. He declined to say whether the incident was over. Euronews reported that the Commission suspects a China-backed threat group, a judgment based partly on the use of Artex, a framework with Chinese roots that has been widely used by threat actors. That outlet also reported the attack has yet to be fully contained.

The EU Academy serves users across the Union's institutions and partner organizations. The data-protection supervisor said it was in contact with Commission services over the incident and would not discuss possible supervisory action while the case is ongoing.
