President Trump held a news conference at the White House on Friday that ran almost 90 minutes. He called his trilateral meeting with China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin in China an "amazing event" and said he would work with Democrats in Congress on key issues, The New York Times reported. The Hill said the president used the session to try to regain momentum, with the ostensible purpose of promoting his recent Asia trip, which produced announced trade deals and the more controversial summit.

Mr Trump spent about 30 minutes recounting his relations with Mr Xi and Mr Putin, NPR reported. He said the two governments had agreed to set limits on artificial intelligence, a position he had previously opposed. NPR noted that details were scarce and that the language of guardrails marked a shift from the president's insistence on unrestricted American advantage over China. "It's not artificial intelligence. It's not artificial. It's super," Mr Trump said of the technology. "Problem is, it may be too super. So President Xi and I agree that we're going to do some joint guardrails, the two of us, 'cause there really is no third place."

The president also took questions on semiconductor sales to China and the fallout over Nvidia. He said he had not lost confidence in Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Mr Trump brought his new chief of staff, Stephen Miller, to the lectern. Miller said no president had achieved more over the past two years. "By far in American history," he added, and promised the next two years would be the greatest in American history. Mr Trump asked whether Miller wanted to run for president, and later called him "a talent" with "a big heart."

The NPR coverage said the president made few new announcements, opening with highlights of his first two years in office before turning to the trip. CBS reported that Mr Trump touted the Asia meetings throughout.

The news conference closed the week after the Shenzhen summit without a published framework for ending the Ukraine war or a detailed account of the trade agreements.
