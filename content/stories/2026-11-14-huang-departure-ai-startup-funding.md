Jensen Huang's resignation as chief executive of Nvidia after 33 years has removed one of the largest single sources of capital in the artificial intelligence startup economy, and investors and founders say the effect on dealmaking, valuations and competition could be severe.

"It's like a bomb went off," a neocloud founder told TechCrunch on learning of the resignation. "Nvidia is carrying almost every AI-native startup from application to neocloud," said Shamillah Bankiya of Dawn Capital. Umesh Padval of Seligman Ventures said most significant decisions to invest in startups came from Huang himself.

Under Huang, infrastructure companies needing $100 million to $500 million in graphics chips often raised half that amount, at valuations of $1 billion or more, from Nvidia alone to get started, one investor said. The chipmaker's presence on the capitalization table drew other investors into lofty rounds. "When Nvidia was on the cap table, everyone felt safe," Padval said.

Investors expect the shift to be immediate under interim chief executive Colette Kress. "She's much more conservative," one investor said. "I don't think she's going to commit the same amount of money to keep the early-stage ecosystem going." Nvidia is expected to be more selective in new investments and to write smaller checks into its existing portfolio, contributing to falling valuations, the investors said.

Some expect rivals to compete for the vacancy. Dharmesh Thakker of Battery Ventures said he expects Google, Amazon, Microsoft and AMD to weigh backing startups that drive adoption of their chips and clouds, though he doubted they would pour hundreds of millions into pre-revenue infrastructure companies as Nvidia did.

Huang stepped down after reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with a woman identified as a possible Chinese intelligence asset. Nvidia is valued at about $4 trillion. Internal correspondence reviewed by WIRED showed some employees circulating a petition urging the board to reconsider, under the heading "Bring Back THE Boss."

Few investors believe the pullback will halt the broader AI boom. But with traditional venture firms expected to think twice without Nvidia's anchor checks, the financing that carried hundreds of startups through the data center build-out is entering its most uncertain stretch.
