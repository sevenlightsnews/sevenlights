Carmelle Collado of the Philippines won the inaugural Eurovision Song Contest Asia on Saturday night at the One Bangkok Forum, taking the title with her dance-pop entry "Sobra-Sobra" at the first edition of the contest staged outside Europe. The 19-year-old, who performs under the single name Carmelle, finished on 211 points, 41 clear of host Thailand. Vietnam placed third.

Eleven countries competed in the two-and-a-half-hour grand final. The winner was chosen by votes from national juries in the participating countries and by a public ballot worldwide; Carmelle received the most votes in both. "I made history, since this was the first time," she said after the result. "It's such a big responsibility for me, because this one is a global stage, so thank you everybody who voted for me." She said the song celebrated female empowerment and was intended as a tribute to Filipino women.

Collado grew up beside the railway tracks in the Bicol region of southeastern Luzon and had been competing and performing since her childhood. Her victory gives the Philippines the first title in the Asian edition of a franchise begun in 1956 to help reunite a war-torn continent through music.

The Asian edition did not match the scale of the parent contest, which drew more than 130 million viewers to its May final in Vienna. India, China and Japan, the region's largest music markets, were among the absent nations. The eleven entrants came from Bangladesh, Bhutan, Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia, Mongolia, Nepal, the Philippines, South Korea, Thailand and Vietnam. The production leaned on spectacle, with tuktuks paraded across the stage and large-scale visual effects, and fans flew in from Australia and across Southeast Asia.

Unlike the European contest, the winner in Bangkok receives no prize money and the victory carries no automatic right to host the next edition. Organisers have not announced whether a second Asian contest will be staged or where. The Eurovision brand is licensed by the European Broadcasting Union, which authorised the regional editions; the Bangkok result settles the first of them.
