M. John Harrison won the 2026 Booker Prize on Monday for "The End of Everything," a novel set on a near-future stretch of the English coast. At 81, Mr. Harrison is the oldest author to receive the award.

The prize carries 50,000 pounds. "The End of Everything," Mr. Harrison's 13th novel, follows Philip Tennant, a middle-aged beachcomber, and his aunt Marnie, who is losing her memory to dementia, as they scavenge the shore for objects that may be more than they appear. The story moves between a care home and the beach against the backdrop of a slow collapse, following a world-changing crisis that readers are never fully told.

The chair of the judging panel, the classicist Mary Beard, told a press conference that the novel came out "ahead in terms of both its extraordinary expert writing, but also huge ambition." The theme of artificial intelligence was on the judges' minds through the year-long selection process, and the prize citation described the book as a darkly satirical, AI-tinged depiction of post-apocalyptic Britain.

The win was Mr. Harrison's first Booker shortlisting after decades as a cult figure of British science fiction, a recognition critics called long overdue. When the shortlist was announced, The Telegraph wrote: "Finally, he's getting his due." The shortlist of six was chosen from 163 entries. Mr. Harrison beat the Jamaican novelist Marlon James, the Welsh writer Rebecca Perry, Douglas Stuart, Elizabeth Strout and Luke Kennard.

The novel's setting, along the Kent coast, grounds its speculation in a working shoreline of customs sheds and smuggling history. Mr. Harrison, born in 1945 in Leamington Spa, has written novels, story collections and comics, and was long associated with the "Kefahuchi Tract" science-fiction sequence.

The award, given for the best full-length novel written in English and published in the past year, by writers of any nationality, was presented Monday at a London ceremony. Last year's winner was David Szalay for "Flesh." Mr. Harrison joins a roll of winners that has reshaped the commercial prospects of unknown and established writers alike.
