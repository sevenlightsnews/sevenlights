![Darkened open-plan office at night, rows of monitors glowing blank blue screens, an IT technician walking an aisle carrying a laptop, server rack with green status lights behind glass, cool blue tones, photorealistic, 3:2](/images/stories/microsoft-outlook-365-global-outage.webp)

Microsoft said a fault that began with Teams in North America at about 10:40 a.m. Eastern on Thursday spread to Exchange Online mailboxes in the Americas by early afternoon and later affected users in every region, leaving businesses, schools and government offices unable to send or receive mail.

The company attributed the failure to "a large number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable," and said the resulting load overwhelmed a process that establishes secure connections. Microsoft rolled back a recent network configuration change to relieve the strain, but said some restored servers became unresponsive again.

Outlook on the web, on the desktop and on mobile devices was affected. Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, Copilot and the Defender XDR security platform also degraded. Microsoft communications director Ben Hope said the company was "aware and investigating reports of users experiencing issues with Microsoft 365 services, primarily issues with Exchange Online."

Microsoft logged the failures as incidents MO1550821 for Teams and EX1550543 for Exchange. The company had warned some users of delays before declaring the outage. It said it was not aware of malicious activity, and had not identified the root cause by Friday.

The company declared service restored for some mailboxes late Thursday and said it was working through the remaining backlog on Friday. Traffic-management measures were put in place as servers returned to service. Microsoft said late Thursday that its attempts to bring servers back online had not gone as planned and that it was exploring other ways to increase capacity. "We've monitored the status of the changes applied to improve serviceability and confirmed they're not taking as expected," the company wrote in an update on X.

The last major failure came in September, when a fault in a core authentication configuration left mail and collaboration services down for days. Recurring outages this year have prompted business customers to ask how much dependency on a single cloud provider they should carry.

Update, Nov. 14: Microsoft said it had found no evidence of malicious activity and attributed the outage to failed server health checks, according to Ars Technica. Traffic measurements by the web-monitoring firm Netcraft showed affected sites recovering after roughly 18 hours, and Microsoft said it was continuing a measured repair of affected infrastructure.
