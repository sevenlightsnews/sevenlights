Miley Cyrus, 33, married the drummer Maxx Morando, 28, on Saturday in a poolside ceremony in Palm Springs, California, with Wayne Coyne of the Flaming Lips officiating.

The couple stood beneath an arch of white flowers in front of family and friends, according to photographs published by People. Cyrus wore a white gown with a small train and was photographed wiping away tears during the vows; Morando wore a black suit. Among the guests were her mother, Tish Cyrus, and her stepfather Dominic Purcell; her sister Noah Cyrus; her father Billy Ray Cyrus; and the actress Elizabeth Hurley.

The celebrations began the night before with a rehearsal dinner, shown in photographs posted by Cyrus' stepsister Lily-Rose Purcell: tables dressed with dark red flowers and small candles, a menu topped with an "M plus M" monogram.

The wedding follows a five-year courtship that began with a blind date in the summer of 2021. Morando proposed last autumn during a trip to Japan, in a garden on the grounds of the couple's hotel in Kyoto. The engagement became public in early December 2025, when Cyrus wore a cushion-cut diamond ring on a yellow gold band, designed by Jacquie Aiche, to the premiere of "Avatar: Fire and Ash" in Los Angeles.

The marriage also closes a year in which the relationship moved into Cyrus' work. Morando co-wrote and co-produced "Let's Get Married," a track on "Bass Persuades," her tenth studio album and the first released simply as Miley. The album debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 on the chart dated October 3, with 61,000 equivalent album units, giving her sixth number one. Its video, shot at the Chateau Marmont in West Hollywood, cast Morando opposite her. The couple sang the song together on the Hollywood Bowl stage a month before the wedding; Coyne, who worked with Cyrus on her 2015 album "Miley Cyrus & Her Dead Petz," joined them onstage that night and led the vows on Saturday.

This is Cyrus' second marriage. She married the actor Liam Hemsworth in December 2018, separated from him in August 2019, and finalized the divorce in January 2020. An insider told Us Magazine last December that Cyrus had considered foregoing a traditional wedding and that Morando had changed her view of the institution.

Update, November 23, 2026: Cyrus posted the first photographs of the ceremony on Instagram on Sunday with the caption "M + M, 11.21.26," set to "Let's Get Married," Us Magazine reported. Rolling Stone published an account of the Palm Springs wedding the same day.
