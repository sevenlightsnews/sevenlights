![Interior of a neoclassical legislative chamber before a leadership vote, rows of polished desks under brass chandeliers, a lone aide walking the center aisle seen from behind, warm light on marble columns, photorealistic, 3:2](/images/stories/van-hollen-challenges-schumer-senate-leadership.webp)

Senator Chris Van Hollen of Maryland announced Thursday that he would challenge Senator Chuck Schumer for the post of Senate majority leader when Democrats take the chamber in January, opening a formal contest for the party's top post weeks before a secret ballot.

Van Hollen, a senator since 2017 and a member of the House from 2003 to 2017, claims the support of more than 10 senators. He argued that this year's Democratic sweep, which gave the party both chambers of Congress, was a mandate to change how the party is led.

Schumer, a senator since 1999 and party leader since 2017, has said he has "the votes" to stay. He has the announced backing of Senator Angus King of Maine, an independent who caucuses with the Democrats, Senator-elect Sherrod Brown of Ohio, and seven sitting Democrats: John Fetterman of Pennsylvania, Ruben Gallego of Arizona, Kirsten Gillibrand of New York, Mark Kelly of Arizona, Jacky Rosen of Nevada, Brian Schatz of Hawaii and Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island.

Six senators-elect set to join the caucus in January have opposed him: Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan, Adam Hamilton of Kansas, Julia Stratton of Illinois, Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota, Troy Jackson of Maine and Annie Andrews of South Carolina. Senator Bernie Sanders of Vermont, an independent, withdrew his support for Schumer on Nov. 11. El-Sayed, who defeated a candidate Schumer backed in the Michigan primary, endorsed Van Hollen the day he entered. Van Hollen acknowledged he was the underdog.

The contest sets an incumbent who has led the caucus through two election cycles against a challenger who argues the party's victory in deep-red states shows that its brand of opposition politics has run its course. Van Hollen has campaigned against the leadership's approach in recent months, aligning himself with the senators-elect who came in on a leftward wave.

The leadership vote is by secret ballot of the Democratic caucus and is expected in December, before the new Congress convenes.

Update, Nov. 14: Senator-elect Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota announced Friday that she would back Mr. Van Hollen, saying "Minnesotans are demanding change. Democrats need to deliver. That starts with new voices" in leadership. Senator Bernie Sanders, an independent of Vermont who withdrew his support for Mr. Schumer on Nov. 11, endorsed Mr. Van Hollen's bid, The Hill and Fox News reported.
