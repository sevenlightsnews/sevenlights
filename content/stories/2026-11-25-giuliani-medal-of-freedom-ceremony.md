President Trump awarded Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom at a White House ceremony on Tuesday, conferring the nation's highest civilian honor on his former personal lawyer and a onetime "America's mayor" whose reputation was later undone by the president's own causes.

The presentation was listed on the president's schedule as "closed press," and no reporters were admitted. Before the ceremony the White House posted a compilation video saying Mr. Giuliani had left an "undeniable mark on American history." Mr. Giuliani, reached afterward by a conservative podcaster, said he was "very, very humbled and very honored not only to receive it but to receive it from him."

His son, Andrew Giuliani, told reporters the moment was "incredibly special," and said his father's legal work defending Mr. Trump over the past six years "will go down in history as one of the most important legal jobs that has ever been done by any attorney."

Mr. Giuliani served as mayor of New York during the attacks of 11 September 2001, and his leadership in the aftermath made him a globally recognized figure. He sought the Republican presidential nomination in 2008 and withdrew after early primaries.

His standing eroded after he became Mr. Trump's personal lawyer. He lost his license to practice law after promoting the president's false claims of election fraud, and settled a multimillion-dollar defamation suit brought by the Georgia election workers Ruby Freeman and Shaye Moss, whom he had falsely accused of cheating. He filed for bankruptcy in 2023 after a $148m judgment in that case, later dismissed by a judge, and in 2025 settled voting-machine manufacturer Dominion Voting Systems' billion-dollar suit against him. State charges in Georgia and Arizona over efforts to overturn the 2020 election, to which he pleaded not guilty, were eventually dismissed.

Mr. Giuliani wrote on social media on Tuesday that "doing the right thing for others has been my guiding light," and credited Ronald Reagan, for whom he once worked in the Justice Department, with setting him on that path.

The honor drew sharp disagreement over Mr. Giuliani's record, set against his 9/11 mayoralty and his later legal battles. No public ceremony for the presentation was announced.
