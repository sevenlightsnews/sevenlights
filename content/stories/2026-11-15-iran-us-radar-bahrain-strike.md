Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had targeted and destroyed an American early-warning radar in Bahrain, the first direct Iranian attack on United States military installations in months. Bahrain issued two alerts of potential threats on Sunday, its first such warnings since September, as blasts sounded near the capital, Manama.

The Guard said in a statement that the strike answered a US airstrike on a communications tower at Sirik, on the Iranian coast overlooking the strait of Hormuz. "This is a serious warning to the aggressor enemy to end its aerial mischief and oil smuggling in the strait of Hormuz," the Guard said. It added that its aerospace force stood "on full alert to confront the enemy without constraint" if what it called violations were repeated.

US Central Command denied that the radar system had been destroyed and said American forces had intercepted two Iranian drones headed for Bahrain. The attack ended a long lull in strikes during the Middle East war and came as negotiations to end the conflict remained at a stalemate, with the United States maintaining its naval blockade and Iran holding its grip on the strait of Hormuz.

President Trump said in a series of media interviews over the past day that he would attack Iran only if necessary, claiming that Gulf countries including Saudi Arabia had urged him to hold back from planned large-scale strikes. Trump said he had asked Vice President JD Vance to end his interest in negotiating a new deal with Iran. Iranian officials had rejected Vance's request for a personal meeting with their negotiators to discuss the terms of a peace deal, having regarded him as the most trustworthy figure close to Trump. Vance had been prominent among Republicans who attributed the party's midterm losses to the war.

Iran's Assembly of Experts, the elected body of Islamic jurists that chooses the supreme leader, has become the first Iranian governing institution to call for a review of the country's defence doctrine. Such a review could open the way to religious permission for Iran to seek nuclear weapons. Two Iranians were found guilty this week in Britain of spying and planning attacks on journalists, a judgment the Iranian embassy rejected as political.

The confrontation remains frozen in place for now: the blockade holds, the strait stays closed to most shipping, and neither side has signaled a return to the negotiating table.

Update, November 17, 2026: Iran launched a second drone attack on Bahrain on Monday, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said, stating it targeted the US air-defence radar facility at al-Riffa air base with six drones. Bahrain's military said its air defence systems intercepted and destroyed the Iranian aircraft "before they could achieve their objectives." The Guard, speaking on Telegram, declared "the Strait of Hormuz is closed, and any aggression by the American enemy will be met with an even harsher and more forceful response," and claimed it had struck a US early-warning radar in Bahrain on Friday. US forces have denied the radar was destroyed and said the drones fired Friday were shot down with no injuries or damage. The attacks follow US strikes this month on Iran's Qeshm Island in the strait.
