Delta Air Lines delayed more than 1,000 flights across the United States on Sunday after the Federal Aviation Administration said the airline was experiencing issues with its automated weight and balance system. The FAA, posting on X, said the delays were "not related to any FAA air traffic systems" and directed travellers to Delta for updates. FlightAware counted nearly 1,300 delayed Delta flights by Sunday afternoon.

Weight and balance systems calculate an aircraft's total weight and its distribution before departure, drawing on passenger counts, cargo and fuel. Without the automation, employees must compile the totals by hand, a slower process that ripples through departure schedules. A Delta spokesperson said teams were "using manual workarounds this morning to safely get flights departed while work continues to restore automation." The spokesperson said the ground delay program in Atlanta, which holds flights at their departure airports to prevent congestion, had been lifted.

The disruption struck on the Sunday before Thanksgiving, one of the heaviest travel days of the American year. It comes a little more than a week after the FAA ordered a rare nationwide ground stop, halting departing domestic flights during a telecommunications and communications failure at air route traffic control centers. Officials attributed that outage to aging infrastructure being modernized, an episode that has prompted questions in Congress about the condition of federal aviation systems.

Passengers at affected airports described hours-long waits. "People have been very frustrated and confused," said a traveller whose Orlando-to-New York flight was delayed for hours. Delta's operations at its Atlanta hub bore the earliest and heaviest impact, where the ground delay program had been in effect.

The airline did not say what caused the automation failure or when full restoration was expected. The FAA declared the ground delay over in Atlanta by Sunday afternoon, and Delta said departure rates were recovering. The department said its review of the incident would proceed alongside the inquiry into last week's communications failure.
