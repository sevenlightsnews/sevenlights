![Rows of students seen from behind seated at individual desks in a vast school examination hall under fluorescent light, proctors standing at the aisles, collection bins for phones at the rear, no readable text on papers, wide editorial photograph, 3:2](/images/stories/south-korea-education-minister-ai-cheating-resignation.webp)

South Korea's education minister, Choi Kyo-jin, resigned on Sunday after an artificial-intelligence-assisted cheating scandal upended the week of the country's national university entrance examinations. The examinations, the _suneung_, were held last week under emergency security measures imposed at the last minute.

The scandal began the Sunday before the exams, when the top scorer in a national tax accountancy examination confessed to using a miniature camera and a smartphone to achieve a near-perfect score. The admission spread rapidly through an education system in which a single examination determines university placement, and parents and teachers demanded assurances that the same methods could not be used in the _suneung_.

The government tightened proctoring and device rules days before the test, requiring students to surrender tablets and smartphones before entering examination halls. Teachers' unions criticised the response as belated, and public anxiety persisted through examination day itself.

"The exam concluded without any major incidents," Choi wrote on X. "However, as the minister in charge, I do not believe I am immune from public criticism that we failed to prepare adequately in advance. Now that the exam is over, I take full responsibility" and resigned, he wrote.

The episode has become the country's most visible confrontation between generative AI and institutional fairness. Senators and commentators called the affair a warning that AI tools are spreading faster than the systems meant to police them, and the government has promised a review of examination security for future tests.

South Korea spends more on private education than any other member of the OECD, and the _suneung_ shapes admissions, employment prospects and family standing. Teachers' groups have asked the government to publish the security review's findings before the next examination. The resignation does not disturb the examination calendar: results are issued in December and admissions proceed as scheduled. The government named a successor to oversee the results process while the security review begins.
