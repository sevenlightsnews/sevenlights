Manchester United appointed Mauricio Pochettino head coach on Friday as successor to Michael Carrick, signing the 54-year-old Argentine to a two-and-a-half-year contract. United paid a release clause in the region of 7.4 million pounds to extract him from his contract with US Soccer, with a further payment for his backroom staff, BBC Sport reported.

Pochettino broke down in tears at a farewell news conference at US Soccer's national training center Friday. He called the move "one of the most difficult decisions of our careers." He had signed a new four-year deal with the federation in August, part of an expanded role in the direction of American soccer. The opportunity at Old Trafford, he said, "is difficult to turn down."

"It's difficult to talk now," he said. "I'm a very emotional guy."

The arrangement leaves Pochettino in charge of the United States men's team through the Concacaf Nations League quarterfinal matches against Haiti on Saturday Nov. 14 and Tuesday Nov. 18. He will then take over a club in its deepest slump in a generation: United sit 18th in the Premier League, one point above bottom-placed Sunderland, with one win in 10 league matches. Carrick was sacked Thursday after the club's worst start to a domestic campaign in 40 years, and said afterward that he was "incredibly grateful" for the chance to have led "the football club that I love."

Pochettino's arrival ends a decade-long courtship. United interviewed him after his departure from Tottenham Hotspur and again in 2024 after his exit from Chelsea, before hiring Ruben Amorim, who was replaced by Carrick. His tenure with the US team ends with a respectable but disappointing round-of-16 finish at this summer's World Cup.

Asked about his legacy in American soccer, Pochettino deflected, then conceded he was "a little bit arrogant, but not too much." The measure of a project, he said, is "the impact on the human being." He hoped to bring the same approach to Manchester, he added.

His first match in charge comes against Liverpool away on Nov. 22, one week after the second Haiti leg.
