Anthropic closed its first day of public trading Thursday at just over $97 a share, 17 per cent above its $83 offering price, after the artificial intelligence company completed the largest initial public offering in history. The listing raised $81.3 billion, surpassing the $75 billion SpaceX raised in June.

The Claude developer sold 980 million shares to underwriters in an upsized offering announced Wednesday, above a proposed range of $70 to $78. Shares opened on the Nasdaq Global Select Market at $111.50 under the ticker ANT, a 34 per cent pop that briefly valued the company above $2 trillion. The stock settled through the session, ending near $97 and a market capitalization of about $1.3 trillion, or $1.75 trillion fully diluted. Chief Executive Dario Amodei rang the opening bell. "Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing," he said.

Anthropic, founded in 2021, is the first company focused entirely on frontier artificial intelligence models to list, and its debut was watched as a test of the market's appetite for the AI sector. Its prospectus showed revenue of $16.2 billion in the first six months of 2026, up from $4.6 billion for all of 2025, against heavy spending on computing capacity and large commitments to Amazon and Alphabet for chips and data centers.

The enthusiasm did not lift the broader market. Stocks slumped late, with most major chipmakers closing lower; Nvidia fell for a sixth straight session after its latest earnings report, and Micron and Broadcom also declined. Analysts at Yorkville Ives said the debut "should help restore some investor confidence that the AI Revolution growth thesis is not waning," while investors weighed whether Anthropic's growth rate can support its valuation.

Market and research commentators noted the split picture of the day: a rapturous reception for Anthropic itself, and a falling market around it, leaving unresolved the question whether the AI sector's valuations can be sustained. Investors now turn to the company's first quarterly report as a public business.

The listing leaves Anthropic among the world's most valuable publicly traded companies alongside Apple, Microsoft and Nvidia, and its results will now be subject to quarterly public reporting for the first time.
