The Detroit Red Wings traded center Dylan Larkin to the Montreal Canadiens on Sunday night, ending a trade dispute that had consumed the franchise for five months.

Detroit sent Larkin, a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 third-round pick to Montreal. The Red Wings received forward Michael Hage, defenseman Kirby Dach, goaltender Samuel Montembeault, first-round picks in 2027 and 2029, a second-round pick in 2027 and a fourth-round pick in 2028. Both first-round picks are protected for the top 10 selections.

Larkin, 30, has five years remaining on his contract at a cap hit of $8.7 million a season. He has a no-trade clause and first asked Detroit for a trade after his wish became public in early June.

The request set off a tumultuous summer. Larkin had clashed publicly with then-general manager Steve Yzerman a year earlier, and Detroit missed the playoffs for a 10th straight season after a late collapse. Yzerman, the franchise icon who had run hockey operations since 2019, moved to a senior adviser role in July. The team left the general manager position vacant at the start of the season and named Shawn Horcoff interim general manager.

Larkin reported to training camp and played 10 games, scoring six points, after being stripped of the captaincy in September. Home fans booed him at the opening game and again at his first return to the ice, though some boos turned to cheers when he recorded a goal, an assist and the first star in a 5-3 win over Ottawa. He widened his list of teams he would waive his no-trade clause for to 10. In late October, after a road trip to Western Canada, he told the team he would no longer report. His agent, Pat Brisson, said Larkin had "had enough of the situation." Detroit suspended him without pay.

Montreal assembles a core with Larkin, Cole Caufield and Juraj Slafkovský, with the four forwards carrying just over $32 million a year against the cap. The Canadiens also hold team-friendly contracts for Lane Hutson and Ivan Demidov.

The deal supplies Detroit with players and picks for its rebuild. Hage, a top college player this season and a strong performer at the world junior championship, projects as a middle-six forward. Larkin, drafted 15th overall in 2014, ranks 10th in franchise history in goals and points and had five consecutive 30-goal seasons, with a career-high 34 last year. He was the last player from Detroit's 2016 playoff team.
