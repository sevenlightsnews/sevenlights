Cricket Australia widened its independent inquiry into the sandpapergate scandal on Friday to cover the entire 2017-18 season, including the Ashes series England lost 4-0, under terms of reference that rule out any retrospective punishment.

The published terms of reference set the forum's scope as "ball-management practices in the Australian men's team during the 2017-18 season." That covers the five Ashes Tests and the four Tests of the subsequent tour of South Africa. On that tour a camera caught Cameron Bancroft hiding sandpaper during the third Test at Cape Town, and the investigation that followed ended in bans for Mr. Bancroft, David Warner and Steve Smith.

All participants in the 2018 tour, including players, support staff, and the management and directors of the time, will be invited to give evidence in person, online or in writing. None will be compelled. Materials supplied will be treated as confidential unless a participant states otherwise, and the forum will run for six weeks.

"It is also very important to understand that it will not seek to sanction or recriminate," Cricket Australia chair David Boon said. He said the board was "acutely aware of the enormous toll the renewed focus on this tour eight years ago has had on those involved." Their welfare, he added, "remains our main priority." The full report and recommendations will be published within 30 days of their receipt.

The scandal was reopened by former batter Usman Khawaja's autobiography, in which he claimed the entire team knew of the tampering plan at Cape Town. The four frontline bowlers in that side — Josh Hazlewood, Mitchell Starc, Nathan Lyon and current Test captain Pat Cummins — have denied such knowledge; Mr. Cummins called Mr. Khawaja's recollection "nonsense." Mr. Warner and Mr. Bancroft have backed Mr. Khawaja's account.

The widened scope gives weight to English suspicions from the Ashes itself. In his 2019 autobiography, former England captain Sir Alastair Cook alleged that Mr. Warner admitted during post-series drinks that he had previously used strapping on his finger to alter the ball. The Sydney Test ended two months before the South Africa series began.

The forum's design answers a standing complaint about the original response: that it punished three players while leaving broader questions about team culture unanswered. Its findings, and their publication in full, will be the reckoning the sport has avoided for eight years.
