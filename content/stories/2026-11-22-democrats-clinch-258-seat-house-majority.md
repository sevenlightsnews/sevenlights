House Democrats won at least 258 seats in the midterm elections, their best general election result in decades, a milestone reached Sunday after Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina. The party has gained 44 seats, four more than the 40 it picked up in President Donald Trump's first midterm in 2018.

Republicans will hold at least 173 seats in the minority. Four House races remain outstanding: one each in Minnesota and Tennessee and two in Louisiana that are heading to December runoffs. The tally in the 435-member House exceeds any Democratic majority since the election that returned Bill Clinton to the White House in 1992, also 258, and the 257 seats won in 2008, though it falls short of the Roosevelt-era majorities that passed three hundred.

The result sets up divided government for the remainder of Mr. Trump's second term: the Senate also falls to Democratic hands, with the presidency held by a Republican. Emboldened Democrats are preparing a 100-day agenda aimed at the cost of living, and are preparing oversight inquiries of the administration in parallel.

Representative Jason Crow of Colorado, a former Army Ranger, welcomed the pressure the majority brings. "Like, hell yeah, we should feel pressure," he said, citing constituents losing healthcare and livelihoods. Party leaders enter the new Congress with a larger caucus than at any point in more than three decades, and with fewer internal votes needed to pass legislation.

The outstanding races will decide the final arithmetic. The two Louisiana contests go to a runoff in December, and recounts in other states had already resolved several close contests in the Democratic column, including Mrs. Foxx's North Carolina district, where a high-profile recount process ended with her trailing before she conceded to Mr. Hubbard.

Democrats take control of the chamber in January. The size of the majority, already the largest since 1992, may still grow depending on the runoffs.

Update, November 24, 2026: The 258-seat tally is the largest majority either party has won in the House in a general election in three decades, and the 44 seats gained exceed the 40 Democrats won in 2018. Representative Hakeem Jeffries of New York, the incoming majority leader, wrote on X that Democrats had "just secured the largest majority any party in Congress has held this century." Senate Democrats take a 56-seat majority, their largest advantage in more than a decade. Party leaders have drafted a 100-day agenda that contemplates swift votes to reverse Medicaid reductions and restore Affordable Care Act tax credits that expired, alongside subpoenas and oversight inquiries of the administration. Four races remain: one each in Minnesota and Tennessee, and two Louisiana contests heading to December runoffs.
