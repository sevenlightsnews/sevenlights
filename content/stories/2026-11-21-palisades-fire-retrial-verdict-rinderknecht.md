A federal jury in Los Angeles convicted Jonathan Rinderknecht on both counts against him Friday, finding that the former Uber driver set the brush fire that became the 2025 Palisades Fire, the most destructive wildfire in the city's history. The verdict came at a retrial, five months after a first jury deadlocked 10 to 2 in favor of acquittal.

Rinderknecht, 30, was found guilty of destruction of property by means of fire and of arson affecting property used in interstate commerce. Prosecutors said he faces a minimum of five years and a maximum of 40 years in prison.

The case held that Rinderknecht used a lighter to set a brush fire on a hillside in January 2025. Firefighters declared the Lachman fire out, but its embers simmered underground; strong winds later swept them above ground and they erupted into the Palisades inferno. The fire burned 23,448 acres, killed 12 people and destroyed 6,837 homes, businesses and other structures, displacing thousands.

Prosecutors argued Rinderknecht was at the head of the trail when the fire began, not at the bottom as he claimed, and presented digital evidence they said contradicted his account of his whereabouts. The defense pressed an alternative theory that fireworks ignited the brush blaze; prosecutors called the claim "baseless" in a filing that challenged the reliability and late introduction of a defense witness who was to testify about stains at a private residence.

Jurors in the retrial were instructed explicitly not to weigh the fire department's response to the Lachman and Palisades blazes. The department has faced criticism for failing to fully extinguish the Lachman fire before it reignited. In the first trial, some jurors wary of pinning blame squarely on Rinderknecht had cited the department's record; that panel split two for conviction against ten unconvinced.

Speaking outside the downtown Los Angeles courthouse after the verdict, one juror cited inconsistencies in Rinderknecht's account of where he was when the fire started as proof of his guilt.

The conviction closes the criminal case on the origin of the fire but not the reckoning over it. Civil suits over the losses continue, and the fire department's handling of the Lachman fire remains under scrutiny. A sentencing date was not announced.
