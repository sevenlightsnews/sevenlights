The Pittsburgh Steelers beat the Denver Broncos 27-5 on Friday night in steady rain, closing a week of public turmoil with their most complete defensive performance of the season. Aaron Rodgers, released by the Steelers three days earlier, answered his former club through the broadcast itself. He texted a message to the Amazon booth, and play-by-play announcer Al Michaels read it on air.

Rodgers, released after he criticized head coach Mike McCarthy and the organization, alleged that the Steelers barred him from their facility after he was benched. "A lot of talk about the sanctity of the locker room," Rodgers wrote in the text. "The code or sanctity exists to protect the integrity of the locker room, not to provide immunity from accountability for its leadership." The Steelers did not respond to the allegation during the broadcast. Former head coach Mike Tomlin, now a broadcaster, called the week's events "a dumpster fire." Tom Brady said on Thursday that a locker room should not be "the place for private things to become public."

On the field, Pittsburgh settled matters early. T.J. Watt recovered Bo Nix's fumble in the end zone for a touchdown, and Jalen Ramsey intercepted Nix and returned the pass for a score in the second half. Mason Rudolph, replacing Rodgers, completed 9 of 15 passes for 142 yards, including a 22-yard touchdown to DK Metcalf, and played without a mistake.

Denver's offense never advanced. Nix was harassed into numerous mistakes by Pittsburgh's pressure. The Broncos' six-game winning streak ended, and Sean Payton's team fell behind Pittsburgh in the American Football Conference wild-card race.

The Steelers improved to 7-4 and host Houston on Sunday. McCarthy, at the end of one of the most challenging stretches of his 19 seasons as an NFL coach, said: "Everything's in front of us." Defensive captain Cam Heyward said the group held each other accountable after the losses to Cincinnati and Philadelphia that set the stage for Rodgers's exit. "When tough times happen, they're just right for us," Heyward said.

Rudolph credited McCarthy for the work put in to prepare him, and said teammates made clear they were behind him: "Let's block out the noise, let's go win a game." Rodgers, 42, did not appear at the stadium Friday, and the league did not comment on the exchange.
