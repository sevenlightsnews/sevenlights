ABC renewed "Dancing With the Stars: The Next Pro," its professional-dancer search spinoff, for a second season on Tuesday and is moving production of the show from Australia to Los Angeles.

The BBC Studios-produced series succeeded in its bid for a California production tax credit, the California Film & Television Jobs Act. The act, Assembly Bill 1138, expanded the state's credits system to unscripted shows for the first time when Governor Gavin Newsom signed it in 2025. The act covers large-scale competition shows and allocates about 15 per cent of its budget to relocating productions to California. "The Next Pro" will be only the second unscripted show to receive the credit, after the Netflix series "Schooled!," and the first to win it in order to move to the state.

The decision runs against the recent direction of the industry. "American Idol" has moved production to Georgia and "The Masked Singer" to New Jersey. The first season of "The Next Pro" employed about 110 local cast and crew and contributed an estimated A$7.2m ($5m) to the Australian economy, per Screen Queensland. The spinoff will now join its parent show in California; "Dancing with the Stars," which premiered in 2005, has filmed all 35 of its seasons in Los Angeles.

Season two, due next year, will feature eight aspiring male dancers competing to become the next professional on "Dancing with the Stars." The format worked in its first run: Adele Zaikman, an Israeli-Ukrainian Latin ballroom dancer and choreographer, won the inaugural season and landed a spot as a professional on the current season of the parent show, which airs its finale this week. Superfans will again have the chance to partner with hopeful contestants and help judge whether they have what it takes for the ballroom.

Robert Irwin returns as host. It was unclear whether Mark Ballas, a three-time Mirrorball champion, and his mother Shirley Ballas, head judge of the British original "Strictly Come Dancing," will return as judges. Conrad Green serves as showrunner and executive producer alongside Deena Katz, Ryan O'Dowd, Krystal Whitney and Alex Cross.

The renewal keeps the franchise in Los Angeles at a moment when California is competing to keep unscripted television at home. A decision on the judging panel is expected as the network sets its 2027 schedule.
