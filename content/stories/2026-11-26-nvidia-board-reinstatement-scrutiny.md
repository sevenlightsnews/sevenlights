The ten-member Nvidia board that voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive is facing questions over how it reached that decision. The company acknowledged the vote came with "input from senior levels of the United States government."

Huang apologized to staff in a company meeting on Tuesday, according to a livestream reviewed by the New York Times. "I made some terrible mistakes," he said. He acknowledged that his extramarital affairs violated Nvidia's code of conduct, but pushed back on reports that President Trump had been instrumental in his return. Trump reportedly dialed into the board's Sunday meeting to press for Huang's reinstatement, according to Semafor. The board made the decision unanimous on Monday.

Corporate governance specialists say the sequence departed from normal practice. Boards in comparable cases over the past decade, where chief executives were found to have had affairs with employees, removed the executives to preserve company credibility, said Rob Lalka, a former federal prosecutor. "The board basically went around its entire process that it had set in place for that investigation to put him back into power," Lalka said.

Others defended the judgment. Betsy Atkins, a chief executive and veteran director, said Huang "is not just any CEO" but the founder of a multi-trillion-dollar company, and that the decision "requires nuanced business judgment." Her complaint was that the board had acted too quickly in forcing Huang out in the first place; the real test, she said, is whether directors have the courage to withstand pressure.

The board's admission of government input drew attention because Washington also holds leverage over Nvidia's core business through export controls. US officials have raised concerns about the alleged role of Alice Huang, the chief executive's wife, as a Chinese asset, and investigators are examining allegations that billions of dollars of advanced chips reached Chinese customers in breach of export rules. Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel recently declined questions about the investigation of Huang.

Huang potentially faces two congressional hearings next week, before the House Foreign Affairs Committee and the Senate Banking Committee. Representative Gregory Meeks of New York, ranking Democrat on the foreign affairs panel, said a hearing should proceed regardless of the reinstatement: "The American people deserve to hear publicly from Mr Huang about how potentially billions of dollars' worth of our most advanced technology wound up in the hands of our greatest competitor, and what role this administration played."

Nvidia did not respond to a request for comment on the hearings.
