JPMorgan Chase said Thursday that hackers had attacked third-party service providers that handle its marketing information, in a breach that may have exposed customers' names and email addresses. The bank said its own banking systems, payment card numbers, passwords and customer funds were not compromised.

The disclosure came three days after a software failure at the bank disrupted payment processing and left customers of several major financial institutions unable to complete transactions. "There is no connection to service disruption of some customers experienced earlier this week," a JPMorgan spokesperson said. Monday's outage was attributed to a software bug in an internal on-premises system, with no evidence of an attack.

JPMorgan did not identify the vendors involved, say when the intrusion occurred, or disclose how many customers' records may have been involved. The bank said its investigation indicated the attackers accessed marketing data, and that affected customers would be contacted directly and offered complimentary credit monitoring where appropriate. Names and email addresses alone do not let criminals enter bank accounts, but they can be used to build convincing phishing messages.

The Wall Street Journal reported that a ransomware group had published information linked to thousands of customers of JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard. Visa told the newspaper that its own systems were not involved and that payment information, including card numbers, was not affected; it identified a third-party service provider in the Asia-Pacific region as connected to the incident. The Journal's reporting did not establish that the three companies were breached in the same way or that the data came from a single compromised system. JPMorgan's own statement remained narrower, confirming the vendor attack while its investigation continues.

The episode is the second technology failure to hit the country's largest bank in a week. Law enforcement and banking regulators have pressed in recent years for banks to police the outside firms that hold their records, after a string of breaches traced to contractors rather than to the banks' own networks.

JPMorgan said its services were operating normally. The investigation continues, and the bank said it would report further to affected customers as the review proceeds.

Update, November 21: The Russian-linked ransomware group n0n claimed responsibility for the hack, according to a report by the threat-analysis firm SOCRadar, after leaked data linked to the companies' customers appeared online. Visa and Mastercard separately confirmed third-party incidents. A Visa spokesperson said the company was "aware of these claims," that the incident involved a third-party service provider in the Asia-Pacific region, that "Visa's systems were not involved and were not impacted," and that no payments data were affected. Mastercard said there was "no impact to the Mastercard payment network or the ability to use cards." JPMorgan, whose consumer division reports about 78 million active digital customers, repeated that attackers accessed certain marketing data held by vendors, which may include customer names and email addresses. None of the three firms would identify the providers involved, say when the breaches occurred or detail what data was taken.
