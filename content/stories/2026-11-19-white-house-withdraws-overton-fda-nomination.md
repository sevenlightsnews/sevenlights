The White House is withdrawing Dr. Heidi Overton's nomination to lead the Food and Drug Administration because she lacked the votes on Capitol Hill, a senior administration official confirmed late Tuesday.

"FDA nominee Heidi Overton lacks the support on the Hill to move forward with her confirmation," the official told Axios. "We need to go in a new direction at FDA, and everybody fully supports Secretary Kennedy's vision to Make America Healthy Again and get back to a more populist and common-sense approach to health in America."

Overton, a physician and deputy director of the White House Domestic Policy Council, was nominated by President Trump in August. A vote on her nomination had been scheduled for this Wednesday in the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee, which is chaired by Senator Bill Cassidy of Louisiana. Cassidy had said he had "strong concerns" about her role in the administration's efforts to change childhood vaccine recommendations, a position he called "almost disqualifying."

Her opposition ran across factions. Democrats opposed her across the board. Segments of the Make America Healthy Again movement accused her of siding with health and agriculture industry interests and undermining Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s agenda; anti-abortion groups questioned her willingness to tighten rules for prescribing the abortion pill mifepristone. The White House had tried to rescue the nomination Monday, posting an endorsement from the former dean of the University of New Mexico School of Medicine.

Conservative outlets framed the retreat as a victory for the MAHA movement. The Daily Signal headlined its report: "MAHA Won."

The withdrawal leaves the administration searching for a third FDA commissioner nominee. The agency has been led by acting Commissioner Kyle Diamantas since Marty Makary resigned in May. Kennedy has pressed for changes to vaccine policy, food regulation and drug pricing that have repeatedly drawn litigation and congressional resistance.

The White House did not name a replacement candidate. A search is underway, officials said, with a new nominee expected in the coming weeks. Overton will remain at the Domestic Policy Council.
