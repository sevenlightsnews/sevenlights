Five European governments announced on Monday that they intend to send rejected asylum seekers to a return hub in Rwanda. Germany, Austria, Greece, Denmark and the Netherlands say they will transfer people to a centre where migrants may choose to return home, settle in Rwanda or move to another country where they hold a right to stay, Kigali said. The scheme remains under negotiation with the Rwandan government.

Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen spoke at a news conference in Vienna with her Austrian counterpart and ministers from the other governments. The plan, she said, would allow the five countries "to confront the massive challenges of illegal migration," a step she called a turning point for European migration policy. Officials said the five had been working toward a return hub for several months. None of the governments published the legal basis for the transfers, which require an agreement with Kigali that does not yet exist.

The announcement fits a broader push inside the European Union to raise deportation figures and curb irregular migration. With elections approaching in several member states, centrist governments have tightened immigration policies in an effort to answer growing public support for far-right parties.

Forty-seven aid, trade union and rights groups issued a joint statement urging the five countries to abandon the plan, saying it would leave vulnerable people at risk of abuse. Their objections echo criticisms of the Rwanda settlement Britain pursued in the early 2020s; Britain abandoned that scheme and made a payment to Rwanda after it was scrapped. During a visit to Kigali in September, two European Parliament lawmakers were shown facilities earmarked for the abandoned British programme, including a hotel, that could house migrants transferred from EU countries.

The scheme has not been signed with Kigali, and its legal machinery remains untested. Lawmakers in the European Parliament took up the plan in debate on Tuesday, and the groups opposing it have pledged continued pressure on the five capitals.
