![White neoclassical triumphal arch architectural scale model on a surveyor table with rolled blueprints, beyond a chain-link fence a military cemetery with rows of small white headstones under an overcast sky](/images/stories/kennedy-family-seek-join-arch-lawsuit.webp)

Nine members of the Kennedy family asked a federal court on Tuesday to join the lawsuits seeking to stop President Trump from building a 250-foot triumphal arch near Arlington National Cemetery, arguing the monument would darken the graves of their family and of thousands of servicemen and women.

In court filings, the family members, including several children and grandchildren of Attorney General Robert F. Kennedy and Sen. Edward M. Kennedy, sought to intervene as plaintiffs in the case brought by the District of Columbia and the Commonwealth of Virginia. The three Kennedy brothers, Robert Kennedy, Edward Kennedy and President John F. Kennedy, are buried at Arlington. The relatives wrote that the project "will permanently cast a shadow over the graves of servicemen and women who made the ultimate sacrifice for this country, including the Kennedy Gravesites."

Rory Kennedy and Maxwell Taylor Kennedy said in a statement that "Americans from every corner of the country visit Arlington to honor those who served and sacrificed for our nation," and objected that a towering arch would cast shadows across the graves of their fathers. The arch would also block the sightlines of the Kennedy gravesites and the Eternal Flame marking President Kennedy's grave, the filings say. Several Vietnam War veterans are separately suing the administration over the project.

The family's move follows a restraining order issued last week by US District Judge Tanya Chutkan, who temporarily blocked construction, excavation, land disturbance and utility installation at Memorial Circle while the court weighs the project's legality. That order came hours after the National Capital Planning Commission approved the arch's final site and building plans in an 8-4 vote that divided members aligned with the administration from local representatives. More than 100 speakers appeared at the commission hearing, with preservationists, veterans and Arlington families testifying against the design.

The Kennedy filings arrive as the litigation over the arch broadens from planning law into questions of memory and mourning at the military cemetery, where some 400,000 servicemen and women are buried. The court has not yet ruled on whether to admit the family as parties, and the administration has not responded to the request. The restraining order runs for 14 days from its issuance.
