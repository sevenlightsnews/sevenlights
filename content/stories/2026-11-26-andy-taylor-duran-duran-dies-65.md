Andy Taylor, the original guitarist of Duran Duran, died on November 23 following a long battle with prostate cancer, the band said in a joint statement Wednesday. He was 65. The band said he had fought the disease for eight years.

"From the moment he arrived in Birmingham from the North East with a guitar and an unshakeable belief in what we could become, he brought an indelible fire and swagger to our music," his bandmates said. "His playing drove the songs that took us around the world, and with a dry wit that was matched only by his wonderfully unsentimental, no-nonsense outlook on life he kept us grounded and gave the band its edge through the extraordinary years that followed."

Taylor was the band's loose cannon and its purest rocker, a guitarist who brought glam-rock flash and a punk attitude to a group better known for fashion. He quit in 1986, at the peak of the band's fame, weary of what he called in a 2018 documentary "one of those rat wheels." Bandmates Simon Le Bon, Nick Rhodes and John Taylor went on to form the side project Arcadia. Taylor joined the bassist John Taylor and Chic's drummer Tony Thompson in the Power Station with singer Robert Palmer, which scored a top-five hit. He then moved to Los Angeles for a solo career.

He returned with the original lineup for the 2004 album Astronaut and left again in 2006. "I was tired of going over and over the same old creative difficulties and financial tensions," he wrote. He stayed on speaking terms with the band and produced its 2022 album, then reunited with it for the 2023 record Danse Macabre. That same year he released his final solo album, Man's a Wolf to Man.

His wife of 44 years, Tracey Taylor, said: "To the world, Andy was a brilliant guitarist, but to us, he was the most wonderful husband, a loving dad and the proudest grandfather. He was the rock of our family." She said he "faced the last few years with so much courage and determination," and thanked fans worldwide whose support during his illness "brought him immense comfort and strength." He is survived by his wife, their four children, Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew, and his grandson Albie.

The announcement came days after the band's original drummer, Roger Taylor, said he was stepping down because of tinnitus. No arrangements had been announced.
