Kilmar Abrego Garcia arrived in Madrid early Wednesday after boarding a commercial flight at Washington Dulles International Airport the previous night, completing a court-ordered departure that ends a closely watched deportation fight and leaves both the government and its critics claiming victory.

A federal judge had ordered Abrego Garcia to leave the United States by Tuesday. The Department of Homeland Security and his representatives confirmed the departure. He traveled with his wife, Jennifer Vasquez, an American citizen; his brother; his lawyer, Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg; and representatives of We Are CASA, the advocacy group that has supported the family.

"I am happy and I am celebrating my freedom," Abrego Garcia said in Spanish at a virtual press conference from Madrid on Wednesday. "I feel free from everything bad that I have been through, and now I can breathe in freedom." He said God had given him strength "because in the beginning I was afraid. But now, I am not afraid to confront — just as I have already confronted — a government."

He added that he and his family, not the US government, would decide their next steps. "The government did not decide where my story continues," he said. "My family and I decide our future." CASA said he was planning international speaking engagements with stops in several countries.

Before the flight, Sandoval-Moshenberg said his client was leaving the country his own way rather than the way the administration wanted, in shackles. The government had sought to send him to a third country, an effort that ran more than a year through the courts.

Abrego Garcia, an El Salvador native, was mistakenly deported to El Salvador last year and held in the CECOT prison there, an episode the administration defended before the Supreme Court ordered his return. He was rearrested and released under court conditions that barred his deportation while his case proceeded.

The case became an early flashpoint in the debate over the administration's deportation campaign, galvanizing opponents and drawing repeated attention from President Trump, who attacked Abrego Garcia and his supporters throughout the litigation.

Abrego Garcia said he and his family would announce their plans in time. The government had no immediate comment on his status in Spain.
