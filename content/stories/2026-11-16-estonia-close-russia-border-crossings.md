Estonia said Sunday it will close all its border crossings with Russia from December 1 after what authorities called an attempted bombing and arson attack, ordered by Russia's GRU military intelligence service, on a drone maker near Tallinn.

Eight people were detained in the attempt on Threod Systems, a defence company that builds long-range reconnaissance and attack drones, at Viimsi just north of the capital. Chief prosecutor Astrid Asi told journalists those arrested lived in Latvia, and authorities asked a court to keep five of them in custody. Estonia's internal security service, Kapo, said the attack was carried out "with the aim of causing an explosion and setting the building on fire."

Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said there was "clear evidence" the GRU had ordered the strike. "Russia must understand that hostile actions against Estonia come with consequences," he said. He notified his Finnish, Latvian and Lithuanian counterparts of the closure decision. Prime Minister Kristen Michal said Russia's aim was "to make us afraid, make us hesitate and weaken our support for Ukraine," and called the episode part of "Russia's broader hybrid war against Europe." The arrests followed warnings from security agencies to the defence industry, Mr. Michal said.

Estonia operates three crossings with Russia — Narva in the north and Koidula and Luhamaa in the south — among the few remaining overland links between Russia and the European Union since Moscow's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Russia suspended vehicle traffic through Narva in February 2024, leaving pedestrians able to pass. No date was set for the Estonian closure to end.

Moscow did not address the GRU accusation directly. Foreign ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova told the Tass news agency that Russia "will verify all this to see if it's true" whether Estonia plans to shut the crossings.

The case echoed an August incident in which Latvian nationals were arrested over an attempted arson attack on Milrem, a defence subcontractor based in Tallinn. Estonia has repeatedly accused Moscow of running a campaign of sabotage across Europe; foreign ministers across the continent have raised the threat of organised attacks in recent months.

The closures take effect at the start of December. Estonian officials said the investigation into the attempted attack continues.
