An expert panel convened by the Pan American Health Organization has determined that the United States no longer holds measles elimination status, according to multiple news reports. The panel concluded after a three-day meeting in Washington that the same strain of the virus has circulated in the country without interruption for more than 12 months. Researchers with direct knowledge of the deliberations told KFF Health News that PAHO reached the conclusion this week and planned a public announcement for November 17.

The decision strips a status the country secured a quarter century ago and caps the worst measles year since 1991, when 9,643 cases were recorded. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention counted 5,412 confirmed cases this year, against 2,289 for all of 2025. Pennsylvania is in the grip of an outbreak that has produced more than 1,500 cases and six measles-associated deaths. The tallies are widely considered an undercount because testing is not universal and mild illness often goes unreported.

Elimination status turns on whether outbreaks fizzle out within a year. With immunity gaps wide enough to sustain "ongoing chains of transmission," CBS medical correspondent Dr. Céline Gounder said, the practical consequence is that "ERs and pediatricians need to consider measles in anyone with a fever and rash, whether they've traveled overseas or not. And we may now become an exporter of measles to other countries."

Public health experts attributed the surge to growing skepticism about vaccine safety, distrust voiced by federal officials including Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., and funding cuts to outbreak response. "It is no surprise that the resurgence of measles has coincided with an administration that has inflicted significant damage to our health care system while spreading false claims about vaccines," said Richard Besser of the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, a former acting CDC director. Anne Schuchat, a former deputy CDC director, called the ruling "a very sad moment."

Regaining the status requires interrupting local transmission and returning vaccination coverage to the 95 per cent level that public health officials say is needed for herd immunity. Canada and the United Kingdom lost their own elimination status over the past year. PAHO's formal announcement next week will place the United States on a watchlist for re-verification, and state health departments face renewed pressure on school-entry vaccination rules.
