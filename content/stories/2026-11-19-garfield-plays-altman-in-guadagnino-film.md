A full trailer was released Wednesday for "Artificial", Luca Guadagnino's drama about the artificial-intelligence company OpenAI, in which Andrew Garfield plays the company's chief executive, Sam Altman. Neon will release the film in select theaters on December 25 and nationwide on January 10.

The trailer, released Wednesday, shows Mr. Garfield's Altman smiling, fast-talking and magnetic in the manner of a founder selling an improbable idea, before the film turns to the struggles around the company — boardroom confrontations, corporate restructuring and the accusations that followed. The A.V. Club noted the performance makes the executive "almost charming," a portrait of a tech figure before he became a public menace in the popular imagination, with "almost" doing deliberate work in the compliment. Vulture observed that the film "isn't subtle about Sam Altman," its Altman answering criticism "with the faint air of irritation at the mere implication" of wrongdoing.

The film follows OpenAI's founding and the struggles around it, leading, IndieWire wrote, to corruption that Mr. Guadagnino details "with scary prescience." IndieWire also reported that the production had a public stumble before release: Amazon MGM Studios dropped the film, which it produced, this summer, allegedly over its own ties to artificial intelligence and to Mr. Altman. Neon picked it up.

The casting places a British-American stage and screen actor, known for "Hacksaw Ridge" and the Spider-Man films, beside Mr. Guadagnino, the Italian director of "Call Me by Your Name" and "Challengers," in the director's first film about the technology industry. Reviews of the trailer were broadly favourable to Mr. Garfield's transformation of the role.

No release date has been announced for further titles in the emerging cycle of artificial-intelligence dramas, of which "Artificial" is the most prominent to reach screens this awards season. The film opens in the window immediately before the Golden Globe and Screen Actors Guild nominations, when its acting campaigns will be tested.
