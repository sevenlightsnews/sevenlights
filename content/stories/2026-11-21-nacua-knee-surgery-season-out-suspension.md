Los Angeles Rams wide receiver Puka Nacua is expected to miss the rest of the season after choosing surgery on a knee ligament, coach Sean McVay said Friday, one day after the NFL suspended him three games without pay for violating the league's personal conduct policy.

Nacua underwent surgery on his medial collateral ligament on Wednesday. The league announced the suspension Thursday, unaware the operation had taken place, ESPN and The Athletic reported. He was placed on injured reserve on November 12 with an MCL and PCL sprain and turf toe, meaning he must miss at least four games; the suspension runs concurrently, so it costs him no games but forfeits about $961,000 in salary.

The conduct case grew out of a New Year's Eve gathering in Los Angeles. A woman who sued Nacua this year alleges he shouted an antisemitic remark and bit her on the shoulder. His attorney denies any antisemitic statement and has called the bite "horseplay." The suit came roughly two weeks after Nacua performed an antisemitic gesture during a livestream appearance, for which he later apologized. He checked into a holistic care facility in the spring, completed a rehabilitation program and entered a diversion program with the NFL.

The NFL told Nacua that any further violation of the policy, including failure to satisfy the diversion program, could bring additional discipline, The Athletic reported. It was his first suspension; he was fined $25,000 last season for criticizing officials.

On the field the loss is severe. Nacua, 25, has 70 receptions for 715 yards and four touchdowns in seven games this season, led the N.F.L. with 129 catches last year, and is the league's career leader in receiving yards per game at 96.2. A fifth-round pick in 2023, he is a two-time Pro Bowl selection and a first-team All-Pro. The Rams are 6-4 and two and a half games behind the San Francisco 49ers in the N.F.C. West.

Injuries have dogged him throughout: a psoas strain in training camp, a groin injury that cost two games, a calf injury and now the knee and toe damage. He is in the final season of a rookie contract, and an extension worthy of his production awaits. The Rams, who view his off-field record as a maturity problem rather than a character problem, may wait for the civil suit to resolve before negotiating.

The Colts separately released quarterback Daniel Jones on Friday, turning to Anthony Richardson for Sunday's game against the Giants.
