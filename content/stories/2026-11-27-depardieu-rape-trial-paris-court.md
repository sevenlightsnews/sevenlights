A Paris appeals court on Thursday ordered Gerard Depardieu to stand trial on a charge of rape, rejecting the 77-year-old actor's attempt to escape a criminal case over allegations that he assaulted the actress Charlotte Arnould at his Paris home in 2018.

The court upheld the investigative judge's decision to send the case to criminal court. It ruled that Mr. Depardieu would be tried for allegedly raping Ms. Arnould on August 7, 2018, and dismissed a second allegation of rape on August 13, 2018. Mr. Depardieu, who has denied wrongdoing, appealed the indictment last year.

Mr. Depardieu may still appeal to the Cour de Cassation, France's highest court for criminal and civil matters. His lawyer, David-Olivier Kaminski, said the defence was waiting to decide whether to do so. Legal experts said such an appeal would face long odds and would not delay a trial for long.

Ms. Arnould, who was 22 at the time, reported the alleged rape several days after going to Mr. Depardieu's house to rehearse a play. She has waived her right to anonymity. Her lawyer, Carine Durrieu Diebolt, called the ruling a relief for her client. "She has been waiting for this moment for a long time," she said. "She is fully prepared for it." She called the forthcoming trial historic.

In a letter published in Le Figaro in 2023, Mr. Depardieu strongly denied the allegations, writing that "there was never any coercion or violence between us." A rape conviction could carry a sentence of 15 to 20 years in prison.

The case nearly collapsed in its early stages. Prosecutors sought dismissal in 2019, and magistrates initially dropped the investigation; Ms. Arnould revived it by filing a civil complaint, which led to a formal judicial inquiry in 2020.

The trial will be Mr. Depardieu's second. Last year a Paris court gave him an 18-month suspended term for sexually assaulting two women on a Paris film set. He was placed on the sex offenders register and ordered to pay compensation. He withdrew that appeal last week, accepting his guilt and offering "sincere apologies" for his behaviour on the set of director Jean Becker's 2021 film "Les Volets Verts."

Mr. Depardieu was for decades a leading figure in French and international cinema, with roles in "Green Card," "The Man in the Iron Mask" and the Asterix films. He was awarded the Legion of Honor in 1996. In recent years a series of allegations have been made against him over his conduct on film sets and elsewhere, in the wake of France's #MeToo movement.

Thursday's ruling leaves the Cour de Cassation as the last obstacle before the case is argued in open court.
