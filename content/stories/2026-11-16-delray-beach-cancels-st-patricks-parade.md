Delray Beach, Florida, will cancel its St. Patrick's Day parade, a downtown procession held every year since 1970, and withdraw its sponsorship of the city's June LGBTQ Pride celebration, officials said, after concluding that a new state law barring taxpayer money from diversity initiatives prohibits the city's role in either event.

The law, championed by Governor Ron DeSantis and taking effect in January, prohibits local governments from organizing, funding or promoting activities the state treats as diversity, equity and inclusion programs. It is among the first measures of its kind to reach a municipal calendar. The city lawyer told Mayor Tom Carney that any government-sponsored celebration of culture, ethnicity or heritage that is not a national holiday — Independence Day, for example — could run afoul of the statute. The city said in a statement that it "is legally prohibited from organizing, funding, or promoting these events in 2027."

Mr. Carney, a Republican of Irish descent, called the parade one of the city's signature events, drawing spectators several people deep along the downtown route in past years. "I've said it's engrained in the DNA of Delray and I do mean that," he told NPR. He said the decision was difficult and that the city is "working feverishly" with multiple entities to keep a 2027 parade alive.

A private model would return the event to its origins: the parade was privately run until about eight years ago, when the city took it over, Mr. Carney said. Should a private sponsor step forward for the St. Patrick's Day parade or the Pride celebration, "the city would gladly hand over the reins," he said. The city noted that private entities remain free to sponsor such events without municipal money. He did not say whether the Pride celebration could be salvaged.

The cancellation has drawn a flood of complaints. Delray Beach officials said they have been inundated with emails, calls and social media messages from constituents since the decision was announced. Mr. Carney said he remains hopeful the city can find a path that preserves the event.

The law is part of a broader state crackdown on diversity programs under Mr. DeSantis, who has championed the measure as part of an ongoing effort against diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives. Whether other Florida municipalities will treat heritage parades the same way remained unsettled.
