Strobe Talbott, a Time magazine reporter who rose to deputy secretary of state under President Bill Clinton and later led the Brookings Institution for 15 years, died Thursday. He was 80.

His son, Devin Talbott, said Mr. Talbott passed away peacefully after a long illness at his home in East Hampton, New York.

Mr. Talbott's career began in the reading rooms of two universities. Educated at Yale and Oxford, he shared lodgings with Bill Clinton, then a year ahead of him at Oxford. While at the British university, after an internship for Time in Moscow, the magazine asked him to help translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev, the former first secretary of the Soviet Communist Party ousted in 1964. He did the work, and it gave him notoriety as a young man and a job at the magazine.

He spent 21 years at Time, focusing on Soviet-American relations and holding posts including Washington bureau chief and foreign-affairs columnist. When Mr. Clinton won the presidency, he telephoned Mr. Talbott, who joined the State Department in 1994 and swiftly became deputy secretary of state.

In that post Mr. Talbott was the architect of the Clinton administration's approach to Russia and the other formerly Soviet countries, and he conducted negotiations between the United States and India after India's nuclear tests in 1998.

"Strobe was a good and brilliant man who made our world more peaceful and secure," Mr. Clinton said in a statement after the death. The former president recalled Mr. Talbott's Oxford years, when it was Mr. Clinton's job to make him breakfast every day: "somehow, I got the better end of the deal." Mr. Clinton said he and Hillary were "deeply saddened" by the loss of "a wonderful friend, and a world-class human being."

After government, Mr. Talbott served as president of the Brookings Institution from 2002 to 2017, a period in which he also chaired the State Department's Foreign Affairs Policy Board. He wrote more than a dozen books. His last, "Our Founders' Warning: The Age of Reason Meets the Age of Trump," was published in 2020.

He was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1946. He is survived by two sons, Devin and Adrian, and six grandchildren. His first wife, Brook Shearer, died in 2009; he later married the author Barbara Lazear Ascher.

Mr. Talbott spent his final decade arguing that the post-Cold War settlement he had helped build was under strain from domestic politics abroad and at home. That warning, set down in his last book, outlives its author.
