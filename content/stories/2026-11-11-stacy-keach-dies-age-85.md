Stacy Keach, the prolific stage and screen actor who made the private detective Mike Hammer one of television's most durable characters and worked across seven decades, died Tuesday at his family home in Magdalenka, Poland. He was 85.

His wife, Małgosia Tomassi, confirmed the death in a statement released through the Guttman Associates firm, saying Mr. Keach died of heart failure "after a long and difficult battle with a serious heart condition." He "peacefully departed this world," the statement said, adding that he "fought for so long, with extraordinary courage, determination and strength. He was a true warrior."

Mr. Keach accrued more than 200 screen credits, but his first fame was theatrical. He rose in the 1960s and '70s as one of the preeminent classical stage actors in the United States, taking on Falstaff and Hamlet and venturing into avant-garde productions. He made his Broadway debut in 1969 as Buffalo Bill in Arthur Kopit's "Indians," a performance that won him a Tony Award nomination, and he took the Drama Desk award four times.

The screen followed the stage. He appeared in John Huston's "Fat City" and won the role of Mickey Spillane's detective in the CBS television film "Murder Me, Murder You," a character previously carried in noirs such as "Kiss Me Deadly." The film spawned a three-season run of "Mickey Spillane's Mike Hammer" totaling nearly 50 episodes, and he returned to the role in a second series. Deadline noted his later work included the series "Titus" and a recurring role on "Blue Bloods." He received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2019.

Directors cast him repeatedly in authority roles: police officers, detectives, boxers and command officers, from "The New Centurions" onward, as The Hollywood Reporter noted in a career survey headlined with that credit.

No public arrangements were announced.
