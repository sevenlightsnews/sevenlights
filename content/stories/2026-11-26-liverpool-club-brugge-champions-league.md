Liverpool swept past Club Brugge 3-0 in Bruges on Wednesday in the Champions League, Alexander Isak scoring twice and Florian Wirtz also finding the net in a win that lifted Liverpool clear of a rival that had come into the match level on points.

The opener came from hospitality of Brugge's own making. Veteran goalkeeper Yann Sommer, the former Switzerland international, was under no pressure when he misplaced a pass to Bradley Barcola. Barcola released Dominik Szoboszlai free inside the area, and Liverpool's captain for the night, with Virgil van Dijk rested on the bench, squared for Isak, who prodded the ball between the legs of the 37-year-old keeper.

Brugge pressed with the energy their manager had promised, but Liverpool were resilient and dangerous. Nicolò Tresoldi headed a corner from Félix Lemaréchal beyond Alisson only for Szoboszlai to clear off the line, and Lemaréchal steered a tame header from Hans Vanaken's delivery straight at the goalkeeper. Barcola, whose pre-season stalled behind his move to Merseyside, offered further encouragement with his sharpness and speed as the second half wore on.

"You see 3-0 and you can think it was an easy game but I don't think it was," said Liverpool's manager, Andoni Iraola. "We were facing a very good team that was tied with us in the Champions League, having an incredible run of games and scoring loads of goals. I value a lot the clean sheet and the win."

The fixture carried memory. The clubs had not met since Kenny Dalglish decided the 1978 European Cup final with a chip at Wembley. Their only other meetings came in the two-legged 1976 UEFA Cup final, which Liverpool won 4-1 on aggregate. Brugge supporters afterward adopted You'll Never Walk Alone as an anthem. Both sets of fans joined in the song before kick-off, and Liverpool were made to feel at home.

Commentators noted Isak and Wirtz were becoming Liverpool's big-game players after a run of results that had put the season in doubt. The clean sheet answered criticism of Liverpool's recent defensive displays.

Liverpool move on in the league-phase standings with the closing rounds ahead; Brugge's long unbeaten run ends before their next European fixture.
