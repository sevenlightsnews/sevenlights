A judge in a Missouri state court in St Louis gave final approval on Friday to a $7.25 billion settlement of lawsuits alleging that Bayer's weedkiller Roundup causes cancer. Judge Timothy Boyer found the agreement "fair, reasonable and adequate," overruling objections that payments to individual claimants would be too low because earlier claimants had received larger awards. He wrote that delaying payouts could force victims, some with terminal cancers, to wait an indeterminate number of years.

The settlement announced in February is intended to resolve nearly all current and future Roundup claims in the United States. It covers users who already have non-Hodgkin lymphoma and those who fall ill in the future. Bayer had faced approximately 65,000 claims in state and federal courts from people who said they developed cancer after using the product at home or on the job.

"The court's final approval is a decisive milestone in the company's strategy to mitigate the risks of both pending and future Roundup litigation," Bayer's chief executive, Bill Anderson, said in a statement. Bayer has long maintained that Roundup and its active ingredient, glyphosate, were safe, citing decades of studies.

The company's share price has fallen 46 per cent since it bought Monsanto, Roundup's maker, for $63 billion in 2018. Bayer settled much of the litigation for $10.9 billion in 2020 but failed that year to win court approval of a separate $1.25 billion fund for future claims.

Friday's approval came five months after the US Supreme Court, in a 7-2 decision, overturned a $1.25 million jury award to John Durnell, a Missouri man who blamed years of glyphosate exposure for his non-Hodgkin lymphoma. The court agreed with Bayer that a federal pesticide law barred failure-to-warn claims brought under state law, undercutting a central pillar of many suits.

Christopher Seeger, a lawyer for many of the plaintiffs, said the Durnell ruling had narrowed victims' ability to pursue claims in court. "We look forward to getting this relief into the hands of all Roundup victims as quickly as possible," he said. The settlement excludes about two hundred cases still being litigated in federal court, where Bayer says the Supreme Court decision strengthens its position.
