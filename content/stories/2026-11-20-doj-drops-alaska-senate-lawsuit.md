The Justice Department asked the US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit on Thursday to dismiss its lawsuit against Alaska's election officials, bringing to an end its four-week attempt to block certification of the state's Senate race. The filing seeks to declare the case moot after Democrat Mary Peltola defeated the incumbent, Senator Dan Sullivan, in Tuesday's election.

"The United States filed this lawsuit to ensure that the state and its officials fulfill Alaska's duty to protect the integrity of its elections," Stanley E. Woodard, associate deputy attorney general, said in a statement. "Accordingly, the government reluctantly acknowledges that there is no longer any means to examine whether votes cast for Dan J. Sullivan were intended to be cast for the incumbent."

The department sued in the US District Court for the District of Alaska in late October, in a complaint signed by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward. It alleged that the parallel candidacy of Daniel J. Sullivan, a retired teacher who also ran as a Republican, was designed to confuse voters and peel support away from the incumbent, and President Trump called the lookalike campaign "a disgrace." The relief sought was conditional: certification would be suspended only if the incumbent lost and would have prevailed under Alaska's ranked-choice system had votes for the other Sullivan gone to him instead. Mr. Trump said he had directed the department "to do it."

State officials said no evidence of voter confusion had been produced, and legal experts widely doubted the claim's strength. Judge Sharon L. Gleason dismissed the suit the Sunday before the election, a day after Alaska's Supreme Court rejected a similar request. The department appealed to the Ninth Circuit and served grand jury subpoenas on state and local election officials over ballot records, a separate effort that drew legal challenges of its own.

With Peltola's victory certified, the seat that hung on the Alaska contest will pass to the Democrats, settling control of the Senate. The withdrawal closes the federal government's month-long effort to overturn the certification of a state election, and leaves the grand jury subpoenas as the only thread still outstanding.
