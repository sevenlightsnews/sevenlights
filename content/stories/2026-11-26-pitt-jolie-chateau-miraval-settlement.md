Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie settled their years-long legal dispute over the Chateau Miraval winery in Provence on Wednesday, closing the last open court battle from a separation that began a decade ago.

"We have settled out of court in the best interests of our family and will not be commenting further," the actors said in a joint statement delivered by their representatives. They added that any comments attributed to "sources" in media reports were not coming from either of them or anyone close to them. Terms of the settlement were not disclosed.

The suit began in 2022, when Pitt sued Jolie after she sold her half of the estate to the Tenute del Mondo wine group. Pitt contended the sale violated an agreement under which he was to buy her share, and called it "vindictive" and "unlawful". Jolie answered with a cross-complaint renewing allegations that Pitt had been drunk and violent toward her and their children on a private jet flight from Europe in 2016, the episode that preceded her divorce filing that year. The divorce itself, conducted before privately appointed judges, was settled at the end of 2024 with its details largely kept from public view; the winery case had become the channel through which the dispute surfaced.

The settlement resolves both actions. Pitt, 62, and Jolie, 51, leased the 1,200-acre vineyard at Correns in southern France in 2008 and later bought a controlling interest. They were married there in 2014.

Lawyers for the two sides had litigated the sale across courts in France and the United States. Jolie's representatives had said Pitt demanded a wide-ranging nondisclosure agreement as part of the proposed buyout, an attempt, they argued, to conceal his conduct. Pitt denied the allegations.

With the case closed, no further proceedings were scheduled. Representatives said the former partners would make no additional public statements.
