The disciplinary committee of the Spanish football federation suspended FC Andorra goalkeeper Jesús Owono for 12 matches Friday after ruling that his challenge on Jesús de Miguel caused the death of the Tenerife striker. De Miguel, 30, died on November 3, a day after the collision.

The incident occurred in a second-division match on November 2. Owono, an Equatorial Guinea international, raced out of his area into midfield while attempting to clear an aerial ball and collided with De Miguel. The Tenerife forward was placed in intensive care in hospital and died the following day. Owono was sent off during the match.

"I feel immense pain over the loss of Jesús," Owono said after the sanction was announced. The 12-match suspension, reported by the Guardian, Sky Sports, BBC Sport and The Athletic, removes the 24-year-old from most of Andorra's remaining league season. The federation's committee treated the play as a serious breach endangering player safety; the clubs have not said whether they will appeal.

Tributes to De Miguel have run across Spanish football since his death. Tenerife retired his shirt number, and clubs from the first division to the regional tiers observed moments of silence and held vigils at grounds around the country; players and staff from his former clubs lined the touchlines in his honour before recent matches.

The case has revived argument in Spain over goalkeepers' charges into challenges and the disciplinary treatment of on-field fatalities. Spanish rules punish reckless play causing injury by suspension rather than censure beyond the pitch, and the 12-match term sits near the top of the range applied for dangerous play. Andorra, sitting in the promotion places of the second division, continues its campaign without its first-choice goalkeeper. The federation's written reasons, with any appeal window, are expected next week; Tenerife's club doctor has called for a review of pitch-side response protocols for chest and head collisions at all professional levels.
