Mexican elite troops, operating with United States intelligence support, captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, the alleged drug lord known as "El Chapo Isidro," at a house in Guasave, Sinaloa, on Friday night, Mexico's defence ministry said. Four soldiers died in the operation, and security forces killed seven armed attackers.

The ministry said the arresting force approached "using stealth and surprise" and was met with gunfire and explosives. Troops entered the house and took Meza Flores and two associates into custody: his alleged second-in-command, Germán N, alias "Serafín," and José N, described as a financial operator for the Beltrán Leyva organisation. The three were flown on an air force plane to Mexico City to face prosecutors.

Security forces detained eight suspected criminals in all, three of whom were transferred to the capital and five to prosecutors in Culiacán. Soldiers seized a fifty-calibre rifle, grenade launchers, grenades, firearms, drugs and cash, and reported no civilian casualties.

Meza Flores, 44, headed the Meza Flores transnational criminal organisation, which the defence ministry also identified as the leadership of the Beltrán Leyva organisation, an ally of the Mayos faction of the Sinaloa cartel. The FBI listed him among its ten most wanted fugitives, and the State Department offered a reward of up to five million dollars. A United States indictment from 2012 accuses his organisation of importing heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana. He had evaded capture for more than a decade.

The raid set off violence across Sinaloa overnight. Armed clashes broke out between security forces and cartel members, who set flaming roadblocks in several areas, as has followed earlier captures of cartel leaders. The military activated its anti-blockade plan and responded jointly with state authorities.

Gunfire from security-force helicopters struck the ground near Kuroda Park in Guasave during a professional baseball game between the Venados de Mazatlán and the Algodoneros de Guasave, sending players and spectators for cover. Burnt-out cars littered roads nearby. By Saturday the violence had substantially subsided, and Sinaloa authorities said the situation was under control. An unverified video posted online appeared to show the detained Meza Flores urging associates to stand down for the sake of civilians.

The capture came days after President Claudia Sheinbaum met President Donald Trump on the sidelines of a summit in Asia. Trump said Friday that the two had discussed trade and security. He repeated his assertion that the cartels "are running Mexico." The defence ministry said the operation was conducted in coordination with United States intelligence "in the framework of bilateral cooperation."

Meza Flores faces criminal charges in both countries. Officials in Sinaloa said security forces remained on alert as his arrest removed one of the longest-hunted figures in Mexico's drug war.
