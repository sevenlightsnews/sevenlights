Crystal Palace appointed Thomas Frank as manager on Monday, confirming the former Brentford and Tottenham coach on a two-and-a-half-year contract running to June 2029. He succeeds Pierre Sage, dismissed after 16 league and cup games in charge, and inherits the Premier League's bottom club, two points and a goal difference away from safety.

Frank has been out of work since Tottenham dismissed him in February, after eight months in charge. Palace approached him in the summer about succeeding Oliver Glasner; he declined while taking a break from football. He has also drawn interest from Newcastle and Fulham in recent weeks. Steve Parish, the chairman, led the renewed negotiation personally and assured Frank funds would be available in the January transfer window. "Crystal Palace is an iconic club in England and in the Premier League," Frank said. "I will do everything I possibly can to help and support the players." Parish called him "the perfect fit."

The defensive task is stark. Palace have conceded 24 goals in their first 10 league matches, the worst record in the division. Sage fell out with the club's hierarchy after a haphazard summer window in which the club signed 11 players but failed to replace the France defender Maxence Lacroix adequately. A deal with Club Brugge for the Ecuador defender Joel Ordóñez collapsed when he failed a medical owing to a fractured foot.

Frank's full backroom staff was not confirmed as of Monday. Justin Cochrane, an assistant in Thomas Tuchel's England set-up who worked with Frank at Brentford and Tottenham, is expected to join him, and talks are under way with Chelsea over Bernardo Cueva, Brentford's former set-piece coach. Paddy McCarthy and the goalkeeping coach Andy Quy will stay.

His record at Brentford, whom he kept in the Premier League for four seasons on a modest budget, marks him as a coach who organises defences and drills set pieces. Frank's first two league matches come against Coventry and Hull, either side of a Europa League meeting with Real Sociedad.
