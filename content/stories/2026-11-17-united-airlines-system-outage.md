United Airlines said Monday it had resolved a widespread technology outage that delayed departing flights for about 90 minutes. The Chicago carrier said the failure touched no flights in progress and no United Express services, and that there was no indication the problem was cybersecurity-related.

An internal memo from United's pilots' union, seen by Reuters, said a company-wide web-based outage disabled several applications used by pilots, including crew communications. United was unable to provide final aircraft weights for departing flights and advised pilots to follow airport station guidance on whether to leave their gates. Before each departure pilots use performance software, often on a cockpit tablet, to confirm an aircraft can take off safely under current weather and runway conditions, calculations that turn on the weight of fuel, baggage, cargo and passengers aboard.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said in an interview Monday night that he did not know what caused the outage, which he put at about an hour and a half. "This is not an FAA system-wide problem," he said. United apologised on X, writing: "We sincerely apologize for the inconvenience. We're aware of the widespread system outage, and our teams are working hard to get things back up and running as soon as possible." It was not immediately clear whether Monday's failure involved the Unimatic system.

The disruption followed Friday's nationwide ground stop, in which the Federal Aviation Administration halted all United States commercial flights for more than four hours after a telecommunications failure. The Trump administration said the grounding stemmed from a fibre upgrade during routine maintenance that led to data flooding and crashed a critical communication system. More than 12,000 flights were canceled that day, and carriers spent the weekend repositioning aircraft and crews. United temporarily grounded flights in August 2025 after a problem with its Unimatic load-planning system.

The airline gave no account of what caused Monday's failure and did not say how many flights were affected. United advised passengers to check flight status on its website and app as departures resumed.
