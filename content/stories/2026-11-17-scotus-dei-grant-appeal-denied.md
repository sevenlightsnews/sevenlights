The Supreme Court declined on Monday to hear the Trump administration's appeal over the cancellation of research grants awarded to University of California researchers, leaving in place a federal appeals court decision that the terminations likely violated the First Amendment.

The case traces to executive orders signed in the first days of the president's second term. They directed agencies to cancel grants tied to diversity, equity and inclusion programs, and to align federal funding with the administration's views on such questions as climate change and transgender rights. Lawyers for the university said the administration cut off more than $324 million in grants to the system's research between January and June 2025. The terminated awards came from the National Endowment for the Humanities and the National Science Foundation, and formed part of a nationwide review of grant terms at universities across the country; some of the suspended funding has since resumed.

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit ruled that the cancellations were likely unconstitutional restrictions on speech. The Justice Department had urged the Supreme Court to take the case, warning that letting the appeals court decision stand would clear the way for taxpayer dollars to flow to research projects promoting diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives.

The university's researchers contended the abrupt cuts punished them for the content of their work. With the appeal refused, the 9th Circuit's ruling governs, and the case returns to the trial court for further proceedings. No order on reinstating specific grants issued Monday, and the litigation over individual awards continues in the lower courts. The decision leaves the administration's other funding terminations, challenged in other circuits, to be resolved on their own records. The university had asked the court to leave the appeals court decision undisturbed, arguing the government had no constitutional authority to cancel awards after reviewing the viewpoints they expressed.
