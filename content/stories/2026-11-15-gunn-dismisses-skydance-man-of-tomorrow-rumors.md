James Gunn said no one from Skydance has seen "Man of Tomorrow," the Superman sequel scheduled for July 2027, dismissing reports that the studio's new owners were forcing sweeping changes on the film.

Gunn wrote in a post on Threads on Friday that "a lot of untrue rumors" were circulating, "but one of them is that Skydance has seen 'Man of Tomorrow' and that all sorts of sweeping changes are being made. No one from Skydance has even seen the movie — the only people that have are Peter Safran and a very small trusted group of friends and family. So any stories around this narrative are false."

The speculation followed announcements that Gunn and producer Peter Safran would step down as co-heads of DC Studios. DC belongs to Skydance following the Warner Bros.-Paramount merger. Reports have said Skydance Chief Executive David Ellison intends to restructure the film universe Gunn built, and Jeremy Latcham has been appointed to lead DC Studios from December.

Nexus Point News, citing unnamed sources, reported this month that "Man of Tomorrow" was being trimmed ahead of its release date, with planned cameos cut and reshoots in preparation. Gunn had confirmed that Xolo Maridueña's Blue Beetle was to return in the sequel; the report said that appearance was among those on the chopping block.

Gunn's statement did not address whether Skydance would seek changes to a film it has not viewed. Page Six reported that Directors Guild of America rules mean Gunn could not be removed from the project, and so would finish the film either way.

The sequel stars David Corenswet as Superman and Nicholas Hoult as Lex Luthor. "Superman," released in 2025, was the first installment of the studio's relaunched universe. No release-date change or production halt had been announced by Warner Bros. or DC Studios as of Saturday.

The status of the DC film slate after Latcham takes the helm in December remains the central question for the franchise, with "Man of Tomorrow" the next scheduled release.
