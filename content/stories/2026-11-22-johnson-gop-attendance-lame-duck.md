House Speaker Mike Johnson told Republicans in a letter Friday that the party needs "every Republican to be here to faithfully complete our full two-year term." Party leaders had pulled a scheduled procedural vote from the floor earlier because 14 Republicans were missing.

The absences left Johnson without the headcount needed to advance party-line legislation and forced leaders to call it quits on the week. Eleven of the 14 will not be returning in the next Congress. Several were among the more than 30 incumbent House Republicans defeated in the November elections, including Reps. Lauren Boebert of Colorado, Monica De La Cruz of Texas, Scott DesJarlais of Tennessee, Max Miller of Ohio and Jeff Van Drew of New Jersey.

"Attendance is a great frustration for me right now," Johnson told reporters at the Capitol on Thursday. "We want to run through the tape and finish strong, but we can't do that unless people are present at work," he wrote in the letter. The Twentieth Amendment ends the current Congress at noon on January 3. He closed the letter with a sports image: "Let's score every point we can and leave it all on the field!"

The House clerk's office recorded between 23 and 35 members not voting in each of the last six floor votes since November 11. On Thursday, the final session day of the week, 21 Republicans, 10 Democrats and one independent missed votes. Votes scheduled for Friday were canceled so members could return home for Thanksgiving. Members are paid until early January; the base salary for a rank-and-file representative is $174,000.

Johnson named the priorities for the final weeks of the Republican majority: a funding bill to avert a government shutdown on December 11, the annual defense authorization bill, the farm bill and permitting reform. He said he wanted to pass "every meaningful Republican bill that can garner 218 votes" and to make Democrats take politically difficult votes before the chamber changes hands in January.

The margin for error is narrow. Party leaders need near-full attendance to move bills on party-line procedural votes, and the lame-duck calendar after the Thanksgiving break runs only a few working days before the December 11 spending deadline. House leaders are expected to press the funding and defense bills first when members return.
