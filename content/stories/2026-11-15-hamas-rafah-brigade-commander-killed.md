Hamas confirmed early Sunday that Samir Abu Younis, commander of its Rafah brigade, was killed in an Israeli air strike on the al-Mawasi area of Khan Younis in southern Gaza on Saturday. The Israeli military and the Shin Bet security agency said earlier in a joint statement that they had targeted a senior Hamas military commander in a drone strike in Khan Younis.

The strike hit a tent sheltering displaced people and wounded two others, according to Hamas and medical personnel. The casualties were taken to Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis. Verified video from the vicinity of the hospital showed ambulances inside the grounds and stretchers stained with blood. Hamas said hours after the attack that the person targeted was a senior member of its military wing.

Sky News reported that Abu Younis had helped plan and oversee the cross-border raid of October 7, 2023. Israel has repeatedly struck al-Mawasi, a coastal strip crowded with displaced Palestinians, despite previously designating the area a humanitarian zone.

Another senior Hamas military commander was killed in a strike in Khan Younis a week earlier, the Israeli military said at the time.

The ceasefire in Gaza took effect on October 11, 2025, but Israeli attacks have continued. Gaza's Ministry of Health said 16 people were killed and 68 injured in the 48 hours to Saturday. The ministry said at least 1,643 Palestinians have been killed and 5,964 injured since the ceasefire began, and at least 850 bodies have been recovered from under rubble. It put the toll since the war began in October 2023 at at least 74,695 killed and 176,093 wounded.

The ministry said deaths related to cold had risen to 11 after a nine-month-old baby died of hypothermia, the first such death this winter. Much of Gaza's population remains displaced in tents and damaged buildings, and recent heavy rains have flooded shelters and damaged water systems.
