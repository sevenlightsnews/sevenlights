The fourth and final season of "Yellowjackets," Paramount+'s thriller about a high school soccer team stranded in the Canadian wilderness, opened to reviews that were largely negative, with critics saying the series buries its final answers in the structure meant to deliver them.

The season picks up where the third left off: Natalie, using a salvaged satellite phone, has called for rescue, and the stranded teens race to fix their stories before help arrives — while in the present, the survivors' middle-aged selves face a renewed public spotlight. The show's creators, Ashley Lyle and Bart Nickerson, have called it their most structurally complex season, adding a third parallel timeline: the adults in the 2020s, the teens' last days in the wilderness, and their first days back home in New Jersey. Hilary Swank joins as the adult version of Melissa, introduced after Juliette Lewis's departure from the series.

The A.V. Club graded the season C- and called it "a total and complete mess," writing that after drifting "into a soapy drama" across its middle seasons, the show "can't figure out if it wants to be a campy drama" about traumatized survivors "or a supernatural thriller" about the woods that changed them, and "dangles truths in front of us like a tempting treat, then irritatingly pulls them away." The Playlist judged the season "a messy final season [that] loses itself in its own mythmaking."

/Film, reviewing only the first two of ten episodes made available to critics, was more patient: it called the post-rescue opening a delivery on the promise the show made from its first episode, praised the introduction of the survivors' parents and the question of how they will cover their crimes, and found the wilderness timeline starved of screen time in the episodes screened.

The reviews recall the pattern of the show's middle seasons, when ensemble puzzle-box storytelling outran its mystery. The remaining episodes air weekly; whether the finale resolves the force the characters call "It" — or declines to, as critics fear — is the last question the series controls.
