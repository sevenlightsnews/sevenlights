A federal judge in Washington ruled on Wednesday against the Democratic National Committee over the government's taxpayer-funded advertisements that promoted Mr. Trump and Republican policies before the November midterm elections, declining to extend a temporary ban on the spots.

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan wrote that the DNC had not shown a likelihood of prevailing in the case. She noted that the temporary restraining order against the ads had been issued when the election was less than a week away. "But the November 2026 midterm elections have come and gone," she wrote, adding that the party now claimed it could be harmed by the spots in December congressional runoffs or because the 2028 presidential cycle had already begun. "At this juncture and on this record, the court is unpersuaded," AliKhan wrote.

The ads appeared on cable and broadcast networks in late September, funded by a $20 million Department of Homeland Security contract. Many touted Mr. Trump's or the administration's accomplishments and carried the disclosure "Paid for by the U.S. Government." The DNC sued the Trump administration in October, arguing the spots were illegal government-sponsored propaganda. The plaintiffs contended the ads differed from most past public service announcements because they did not seek to help the public make use of specific government programs.

The White House defended the spots as public service announcements of the kind produced by past administrations, arguing they were not political advertisements because they did not explicitly call on viewers to take any particular action. Mr. Trump said in early October that his super PAC, MAGA Inc., would begin paying for the ads. He called them a "positive promotion for our Great U.S.A." Before the ruling, the Justice Department argued there was no threat of harm because the government had already stopped using public funds to air the ads and the midterms had passed.

AliKhan's temporary order had barred the administration from developing, airing or distributing some of the challenged ads through Nov. 25. In a similar case in New York, a judge blocked a broader collection of the spots and required the government to pull three of them from official websites and social media accounts. That judge also declined a long-term ban.

A DNC spokesperson, Angelo Fernández Hernández, said the party was "disappointed" and "will fight on to put an end to the Trump administration's attempts to undermine our democracy." The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment.
