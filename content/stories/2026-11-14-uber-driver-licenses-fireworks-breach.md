Uber is notifying thousands of drivers that their personal information, including driver's license details, was stolen in a breach at Fireworks AI, a third-party technology provider, according to data breach filings submitted to state attorneys general this month. State notices reviewed by TechCrunch recorded 2,567 affected people in Massachusetts, 3,430 in South Carolina and 1,873 in Rhode Island, at least 7,870 across three states, with additional notifications involving other states and an Illinois filing that also identified driver's license data. Uber has not confirmed the nationwide total.

Uber said the incident came to its attention on Oct. 9, when Fireworks reported that an unauthorized party had compromised internal login credentials. Those credentials gave the attacker access to Uber's account with the provider, a path to personal records stored outside Uber's own systems. Investigators confirmed on Oct. 13 that Uber data had been accessed. The notification letter to affected drivers is dated Nov. 10.

The company said it immediately cut its data connections with the provider and opened an investigation. It had identified no misuse of the information when the letters went out and is offering free credit monitoring and fraud assistance through a TransUnion subsidiary.

Fireworks said cybercriminals breached its cloud provider to reach its development systems, then used internal credentials to obtain customer data. The company said the attackers appeared motivated by stealing and reselling tokens for large language models, the computing credits that power AI services. No known criminal group has claimed responsibility.

The same breach touched other clients. Notion confirmed that the actors obtained anonymized chat transcripts from Notion AI customers, affecting fewer than 1 percent of workspaces, and said its own systems were not breached. The disclosures show how one provider's security failure can expose records belonging to several companies.

Uber requires drivers to submit proof of a valid license, insurance and residency, along with bank statements and payment information. This is the company's second known breach this year; an extortion gang claimed responsibility for stealing data from its Uber Freight unit. Fireworks confirmed a security issue involving unauthorized access to some systems but did not answer questions about the intrusion's scale. Notifications to drivers in additional states were continuing.
