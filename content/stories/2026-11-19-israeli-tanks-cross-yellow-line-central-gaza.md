![Armored military vehicles kicking up dust in single file along a straight coastal highway at dawn, low ruined buildings and olive trees on the horizon, hazy amber light, long shadows, photojournalism, no readable text, 3:2](/images/stories/israeli-tanks-cross-yellow-line-central-gaza.webp)

Israeli tanks crossed the "Yellow Line" into Hamas-controlled central Gaza on Wednesday, the first ground operation across the ceasefire boundary since the truce took effect last October, forcing dozens of displaced Palestinians from their tents.

Tanks rolled along the Salah al-Din road, the territory's main north-south artery. An Israeli military spokesperson said troops were carrying out an operation targeting "Hamas infrastructure" west of the line. A Gaza security source described "intense Israeli artillery shelling" north of the Nuseirat refugee camp and reported military vehicles in the area amid ongoing gunfire and airstrikes from midday. Residents said they heard tank fire.

Hamas condemned the raid and the forced displacement of Palestinians as "a flagrant violation of the ceasefire agreement" and a dangerous escalation. Palestinian medics said one person was killed in an Israeli airstrike near the site of the operation. Two others died in separate incidents on Wednesday, one in Deir al-Balah and one in Gaza City. The Israeli military said it had struck a militant in Gaza City and was unaware of any incident in Deir al-Balah.

The operation came a day after the Board of Peace, the US-backed body overseeing the truce under President Donald Trump's plan to end the war, reported to the United Nations Security Council that Israel and Hamas had both failed to honour their commitments. In its second report to the Council since the ceasefire began, the body warned that "a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely."

Israel has stepped up attacks on Hamas, which it says is regrouping and rearming. Hamas accuses Israel of trying to derail the October 2025 ceasefire. Israeli strikes have continued throughout the truce, and Hamas remains armed. Last week an airstrike on Nasser hospital in southern Gaza killed six people, including a surgeon, according to the hospital director.

Gaza's health authorities say at least 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took effect; the Israeli military says five of its soldiers have died over the same period. Palestinian authorities put the death toll from the military campaign, launched after the Hamas-led attack of October 7, 2023, that killed about 1,200 people, at more than 74,000.

The raid also pressed on Israeli politics. The opposition politician Gadi Eisenkot has been seeking to form a government without the caretaker prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu. The military did not respond to Hamas's condemnation.
