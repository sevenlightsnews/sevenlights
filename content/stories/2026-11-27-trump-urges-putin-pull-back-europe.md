President Trump said on Thursday that he had asked Vladimir Putin to "pull back all military aggression" against territories throughout Europe, a shift in tone toward the Russian leader he had called his friend just a week earlier.

Posting on Truth Social, Mr. Trump said that during his conversations with Mr. Putin at the APEC summit in China last week, he "spent a lot of time talking about the fact that I would like to see President Putin pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe." He did so, he said, "in the hopes that a Deal can be made between Russia and Ukraine, and made quickly." A deal was "a strong possibility," he added: "Thousands of people are being killed each and every month. A complete bloodbath."

The statement did not specify which territories Mr. Trump meant, and did not name Ukraine, which Russia invaded in February 2022. Speaking later to reporters and US service members, Mr. Trump defended his shifting rhetoric as pragmatic negotiation, saying personal rapport was essential while Mr. Putin and President Volodymyr Zelensky remained deadlocked. "I've been very tough on Russia, but I want to make a deal," he said.

The remarks came a day after Mr. Trump hosted NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte at his Mar-a-Lago resort in Florida. In a joint statement issued Wednesday, the two said they "discussed the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the war."

The demand followed a week of alarms in NATO's east. Poland accused Russia of violating its airspace last week after two missiles and a drone crashed in its territory; three Russian cruise missiles landed in Polish territory during a massive aerial attack on western Ukraine. Russia denied the accusation. Estonia has accused Russian security services of ordering an attempted arson attack on an Estonian defense company. Moscow called the charge "unfounded." Russia in turn has accused Estonia of allowing Ukrainian drones to use its airspace to strike northwestern Russia, charges Tallinn called baseless.

Mr. Trump's call for restraint coincided with Washington's deferral of a major overhaul of its military footprint on the Continent. US Deputy Ambassador to NATO Scott Oudkirk told allied envoys in a closed-door meeting in Brussels this week that the review of the roughly 80,000 American troops stationed in Europe had been postponed. Asked whether he would reduce that force, Mr. Trump said recent allied steps — pledges to raise defense spending toward 5 percent and to buy 750 F-35 fighter jets — "made me feel a little bit differently."

President Volodymyr Zelensky warned that Russia was preparing a large-scale attack, and European officials said the president's public pressure on Moscow amounted to little without a stated consequence for ignoring it.
