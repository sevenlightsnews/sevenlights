Anthropic filed a prospectus with US regulators on Tuesday for a Nasdaq listing that would value the Claude chatbot developer at as much as $1.4 trillion and raise up to $64.4bn, a valuation set well below the $2 trillion flotation that had been expected before investors turned skeptical of the artificial intelligence trade.

The California company is selling 825 million shares at a range of $70 to $78. At the top of the range the company would be worth $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis, counting all shares in existence including employee stock options. Trading is expected to begin next week. Anthropic will raise a further $26bn through a private placement with cornerstone investors including Alphabet, Google's parent, and the chipmaker Nvidia, and a greenshoe option could add $9.7bn if demand is strong, bringing the total take to roughly $100bn.

The offering would rank second only to SpaceX's listing, the largest ever at a valuation of $1.78 trillion and a raise of $75bn. Anthropic's $100bn ceiling would exceed the amount SpaceX raised. The Financial Times reported that bankers weighing the deal considered lifting the valuation to $1.5 trillion or more, a sign of robust investor demand.

The filing disclosed revenue running at an annual rate of $81bn as of 31 October, up $10bn from the previous month. Chief Executive Dario Amodei is selling about $260m of shares in the float, as are his six fellow co-founders; at the top of the price range their stakes would be worth more than $18bn each.

The valuation's fall from the $2 trillion mark reflects a cooling in the AI trade. Victories by candidates opposed to datacenter construction in last week's midterm elections added to doubts over whether companies can generate returns on AI infrastructure. Brian Hopkins, an analyst at Forrester Research, said the elections gave investors "a chance to correct valuations that they were beginning to have concerns about," adding: "The market needed an excuse to correct itself a little bit in terms of valuations."

The Chinese spying scandal enveloping Nvidia, a cornerstone company for AI investors, threatens to overshadow the industry but has not yet affected share prices other than Nvidia's, the Guardian reported. The flotation remains subject to regulatory review, and a final price will be set before trading opens.
