Rescuers in Ukraine worked through Monday to reach residents trapped under the rubble of a block of flats that partially collapsed in a Russian strike, after attacks on the Kyiv region killed at least 15 people.

An overnight strike on Dymer, about 45 kilometres north of the capital, hit a five-storey building and killed at least 11 people there, Kyiv regional official Tymur Tkachenko said. He said rescuers recovered another body from beneath the rubble as the regional toll rose. Ukraine's State Emergency Service said the dead included a teenager and a woman in her forties. Euronews reported 15 people missing after the drone strike in the region.

The strikes formed part of a nationwide assault in recent days using ballistic missiles and drones. Ballistic missiles hit key road crossings in the capital and railway stations, and President Volodymyr Zelensky called it one of the most massive strikes of the war so far. At least nine people were killed and 28 injured in the attacks of the past week, Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty reported. Damaged bridges in Kyiv were partially closed to traffic, forcing residents to cross the Dnieper River on foot.

Moscow has intensified nightly aerial barrages as Ukraine braces for another winter and the invasion grinds through its fifth year. President Vladimir Putin vowed last week a "painful" response to a Ukrainian strike that badly damaged the Crimean Bridge, the crossing Russia built to link its mainland to the occupied peninsula.

The latest strikes coincided with a flurry of diplomacy around the war and came as the Kremlin pushed back against a US claim that Russia could be open to Western-backed security guarantees for Ukraine. Mr Putin's summit with President Donald Trump and China's Xi Jinping earlier this month had raised expectations of renewed negotiations without producing a framework.

Officials said the search at Dymer would continue while crews braced unstable structures. The death toll was expected to rise as recovery teams reached the lowest floors.
