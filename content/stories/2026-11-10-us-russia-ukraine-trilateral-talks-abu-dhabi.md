United States, Russian and Ukrainian negotiators opened trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi this week, with American officials seeking what they described as a final end to the war, now in its fifth year.

The agenda centers on security guarantees. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine and its allies have discussed arrangements similar to NATO's Article 5, under which an attack on one member is treated as an attack on all. President Trump told an interviewer on November 5 that a broad agreement on European security was in the works. "It's going to be a big agreement and it's going to involve more than Ukraine," he said, adding that it might cover all of Europe.

The positions of the warring sides remain far apart. Moscow wants Ukraine to withdraw its troops from the four regions Russia claims and to accept limits on its military. Kyiv insists an immediate ceasefire must precede a wider settlement and says only a direct meeting between Mr. Putin and Mr. Zelensky can settle territorial questions.

A dispute over Europe's role shadowed the opening. Ukraine's chief negotiator, Rustem Umerov, said his delegation would meet separately with European envoys in Abu Dhabi. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the Europeans would be needed in broader negotiations but only later. Kremlin foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov was blunter: "No one is going to invite them to the negotiating table."

The talks began as Russia stepped up air attacks on Ukraine's energy system ahead of winter. Mr. Zelensky said on November 7 that Russia sought to "plunge the entire country into a blackout." Strikes have left thousands of Ukrainians without heat or electricity, and Kyiv's mayor, Vitali Klitschko, called conditions in the capital "extremely difficult." Ukrainian authorities said transmission lines, residential buildings and key transport links, including the main bridge over the Dnipro River, were hit. Moscow has leaned on ballistic missiles and faster jet-powered drones, exploiting a shortage of American-made Patriot interceptors.

Both sides have also escalated long-range drone and missile attacks against each other. No date was announced for a follow-up round or for a leaders' meeting.

Update, Nov. 11, 2026: Ukrainian and Russian delegations met for a second day of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday. Washington called for a "new" security framework on the opening day, and President Zelensky said he was "happy" with the change in approach by the American mediators. Senior US officials told reporters they saw a "real shot" at serious progress toward ending the war, citing signs of growing willingness on both sides to reach a settlement, and negotiations were set to continue through Wednesday.

Update, Nov. 12, 2026: The two-day talks concluded with agreement on what both sides described as the largest prisoner exchange of the war, expected to involve more than 1,000 prisoners from each side and potentially rising to 1,500, but with little progress toward a broader peace. Envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff said the exchange would "reunite families, build trust and create momentum towards peace," in Kushner's words. A senior member of Ukraine's delegation, David Arakhamia, said the Russian team had arrived with "very, very strict directives," and Russia's chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, used the sessions to question Europe's security architecture. No breakthrough came on a ceasefire or the territorial disputes blocking a settlement. Kushner and Witkoff were due in Moscow on Thursday to brief President Vladimir Putin and then in Dublin for talks with President Volodymyr Zelensky.

Update, Nov. 13, 2026: Kushner and Witkoff held more than four hours of talks with Putin at the Kremlin on Thursday before flying to Dublin to debrief Zelensky. Putin indicated he would not water down demands to control parts of Ukraine that Russia claims to have annexed, and ruled out foreign peacekeeping troops, a deployment he said would be a "continuation of war" and that Moscow would treat as a "legitimate target." Zelensky and Kushner said afterward that Europe would join the next round of negotiations, though the Kremlin did not confirm agreement on the point. An EU official told Agence France-Presse that representatives of five European capitals and the European Commission had also met the US envoys, and European leaders gathering in Dublin were to decide on a special envoy and a common position for the talks. Zelensky said fresh restrictions on the Starshield satellite service were hampering Ukraine's ability to intercept Russian jet drones. Fighting continued: Russia said on Friday it had shot down nearly 1,200 Ukrainian drones overnight, and four people were killed in a Ukrainian drone attack on an industrial zone in Volgograd, regional governor Andrei Bocharov said.
