Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as president and chief executive less than a week after saying it would not, an about-face that President Trump publicly urged and that reportedly included a call by the president into a board meeting. Nvidia shares rose more than 30 per cent on Monday, adding about $950bn of market value — just under $1tn, the largest one-day gain for a single company on record.

The episode has put the 2008-era phrase "too big to fail" back in circulation, this time attached to a company rather than a bank. Nvidia is the second-largest stock on the US market. NPR's economics coverage said investors treated Mr. Huang himself as "a good chunk of the company's value," summing the market's view in three words: "Jensen Huang is Nvidia." The broader indexes rallied with the news, though they had been pointing upward before it.

The company's reach runs through the financing chains of the artificial-intelligence economy. Nvidia has a deal with CoreWeave, a heavily indebted data-center operator that buys Nvidia chips and rents computing space to customers. If CoreWeave cannot find customers, Nvidia will pay for the computing power itself — an arrangement NPR compared with an auto manufacturer leasing cars from its dealerships to keep them in business. Credit investors had priced CoreWeave with a better-than-average chance of bankruptcy over five years, MarketWatch reported in October, and CoreWeave lost hundreds of millions of dollars last quarter after interest payments. CoreWeave's stock rose 22 per cent on Mr. Huang's reinstatement.

For all the celebration, investors are not paying for aggressive growth. Nvidia stock trades at about 10 times forward earnings, a multiple NPR noted is in the same neighborhood as Kroger, a predictable grocery chain, and below what investors will pay for other chipmakers — even though Nvidia doubled its sales over the last year.

Commentators on the governance question argued that when rules bend for a top performer, accountability becomes harder for everyone else, and asked what oversight can reach a company whose founder the board, the White House and the market all declined to do without. Fortune's account of the episode, titled "The 11 Days That Brought Jensen Huang Back to Nvidia," chronicled the whiplash sequence from the board's first decision to its reversal.

Congress has not accepted the reversal as final. Three House committees said Mr. Huang will testify next week, and the Senate Banking Committee has called him for 3 December.
