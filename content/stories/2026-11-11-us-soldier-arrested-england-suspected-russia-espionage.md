A senior US Army officer was arrested in England on Tuesday on suspicion of assisting Russian intelligence, British counterterrorism police said. The man, 41 years old, was detained near the city of Gloucester in western England in a case American officials described as espionage.

The Metropolitan Police said its counterterrorism commanders worked closely with the Federal Bureau of Investigation and the US military on the arrest, which it said was "not linked to any other ongoing Counter Terrorism Policing investigations." The soldier was serving at Imjin Barracks near Gloucester, headquarters of NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, according to a US official, a senior NATO official and a British government official, all of whom spoke to the Associated Press on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military matters. The US official said the man was an officer assigned to the corps.

FBI Director Kash Patel said the arrest related to "allegations of espionage, which is of the utmost concern to the United States and our allies." Brig. Gen. Sean Stinchon, the commanding general of US Army Counterintelligence Command, said the "alleged conduct of a senior soldier, entrusted with sensitive information, is a sobering reminder that insider threats are real and persistent."

The arrest came the day after US forces removed refuelling aircraft from the Royal Air Force base at Mildenhall in eastern England. The Wall Street Journal reported the withdrawal was prompted by a threat from Iran. It was the second such apparent evacuation of aircraft from British bases in three months, following a similar move in September.

The Allied Rapid Reaction Corps is a NATO high-readiness headquarters capable of commanding multinational forces across a range of operations. Its garrison at Imjin Barracks draws personnel from allied militaries across Europe and North America.

British police said the suspect remained in custody and the investigation was continuing. No charges were announced, and police gave no date for a decision on further action.
