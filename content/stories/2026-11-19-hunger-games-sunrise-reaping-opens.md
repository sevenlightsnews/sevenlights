"The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping" opens in theaters Friday, the first new entry in the franchise since 2023. The prequel re-enters the world of Panem through the 50th Hunger Games, the Quarter Quell in which the number of tributes from each district is doubled.

Francis Lawrence, who has directed every film in the series since "Catching Fire," returns behind the camera. Joseph Zada, a newcomer, plays the 16-year-old Haymitch Abernathy of District 12, the role Woody Harrelson carried as the embittered mentor in the original trilogy. The film adapts Suzanne Collins's novel, published this decade, in which Haymitch survives the doubled Games and is enlisted by a group secretly working to undermine them.

Notices were strong. The Playlist called Zada "a revelation" in "Lawrence's devastating tale of rebellion." The A.V. Club judged the film "another honorable attempt at prequelizing" the series, praising Lawrence's handling of the material while finding the outcome never in real doubt — "we're not exactly sitting around wondering if Haymitch will survive the Games."

The franchise is among the most durable of the young-adaptation boom of the 2010s, and the prequel follows the commercial logic that has extended other literary series. Lawrence has split and extended earlier instalments; here the additional backstory has, so far, revived interest rather than diluted it. The original series starring Jennifer Lawrence concluded more than a decade ago.

Coverage of the film's campaign through the run-up came as a steady drum of studio features and premiere reporting tracked by Deadline, which has maintained a running file on the sequel plans that follow this instalment.

The film plays in standard, IMAX and premium large-format auditoriums. Whether the prequel restores the series to the top of the box office will be measured against opening-weekend figures over the Thanksgiving stretch, when franchise releases historically perform best.

Update — November 20, 2026: Advance ticket sales passed $21 million, the second-best preview total in the franchise, ahead of recent openings for "Wicked" and "The Odyssey," box-office trackers Deadline and The Hollywood Reporter reported. The A.V. Club, grading the film B as previews began, called it "another honorable attempt at prequelizing" the series, filling in the interval between "Songbirds and Snakes" and the original trilogy, with Mckenna Grace as Maysilee, Ben Wang as Wyatt, Jesse Plemons as a young Plutarch Heavensbee and Ralph Fiennes as a younger President Snow. The opening arrives in the Thanksgiving stretch.

Update — November 21: Deadline reported the film began its global weekend with a running cume of about $76 million, counting an estimated $51 million Friday that includes $21 million in previews, the second-best preview haul of 2026 behind "Spider-Man: Brand New Day's" $72 million. The picture led 66 of the 67 markets it has played to date, with 17 further territories opening this weekend, and posted the largest opening day in the franchise in several markets. The net production cost is $150 million, financed largely through foreign presales; analysts put the likely global opening near $200 million.
