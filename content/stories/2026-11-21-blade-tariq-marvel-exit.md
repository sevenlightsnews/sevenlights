Bassam Tariq, who was attached to direct Marvel's Blade vehicle for Mahershala Ali before the project collapsed, said this week that the experience settled his terms for future work. "Life's too short to make shit films, so that was my big lesson: I have to have full control over my script," he told The New Arab, in interviews discussed by Deadline and The Hollywood Reporter.

Tariq said Marvel began casting and building around the vampire-hunter story before a screenplay was finished — "funny and strange to start casting and building without a proper script" — and that revisions arriving from the studio put him in an untenable position with his company. "If you're the bearer of bad news to your actors, but you're not the one making any of these decisions — and it's actually coming from a corporate entity — that's not a great way to be a director," he said. "I learned I was losing credibility with my actors, and it wasn't a healthy way to operate as a filmmaker."

Blade, announced in 2019 with Ali as the day-walking detective Eric Brooks, never reached a camera. Tariq exited the project; Marvel has not revived it.

The wreckage yielded one artifact. Tariq said costumes built for Blade were repurposed for Sinners, Ryan Coogler's 2025 vampire film, which drew widely praised notices. "I was so happy that that's the film we got out of it, because Sinners feels so much bolder than what we could have accomplished," Tariq said, adding that the film "felt so singular for Ryan Coogler, who has been building all these IPs for other people."

Tariq and Ali did finally work together, on Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother, a personal film the two made after the Blade split. Tariq said the collaboration surprised the actor, who had not worked that way before.

The interviews land amid a broader reassessment of the franchise model in Hollywood, where several Marvel titles have underperformed and development collapses have stretched for years. Tariq's account adds a director's view of the mechanics: scripts revised late, casts announced early, and a hired filmmaker left answering for choices made above him.

He left the summary of the exit on practical ground. "If we disagree, it's between them and me," he said. "It's quite sad because we had such high hopes for what we wanted to build."
