The Toronto Tempo won the WNBA draft lottery Sunday, claiming the first pick in the 2027 draft against 33 percent odds and putting Southern California guard JuJu Watkins within reach of the expansion franchise.

The Tempo, who joined the league this past season, entered the lottery tied with the Houston Comets — the franchise formerly known as the Connecticut Sun — for the best chance at the top pick. Both clubs held 33 percent odds, and the Tempo's draw prevailed. The Comets will select second. Washington held the third-best odds and kept its own pick at No. 3, while also carrying the Chicago Sky's first-round selection through a trade. Seattle picks fourth and the Portland Fire fifth. The Los Angeles Sparks pick sixth and seventh, the latter acquired from Phoenix. New York, Dallas, Indiana and Chicago follow in the first round; the Sky also hold the 12th pick, and Dallas, Golden State and Minnesota round out the order.

Toronto's and Portland's odds were set by their inaugural-season records, the only two teams so weighted; the other five lottery clubs were ranked on records over the previous two seasons.

Ms. Watkins, the USC guard who has carried the Trojans through the college game's biggest stages, is widely viewed as the player most likely to go first if she enters. CBS Sports' mock draft projects her first and Hannah Hidalgo of Notre Dame second, to Toronto and Houston respectively. The draft pool remains unsettled after the NCAA granted players a fifth year of eligibility, a rule change that keeps several college stars in school. Players have until April 2 to declare for the draft, with those still competing in the NCAA Final Four given additional time.

The pick is a shortcut for a franchise a single season old. The 2027 draft will be Toronto's first, and the Tempo now hold the right to choose ahead of every other club.
