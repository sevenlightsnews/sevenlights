Uefa has fined the Football Association 15,000 euros and ordered a one-match restriction on selling tickets to England supporters for an away competitive fixture, over "racist and/or discriminatory" behaviour by fans at Sunday's 3-2 Nations League victory over Spain in Madrid. The restriction is suspended for a probationary period of two years.

The disciplinary body said the behaviour in question was believed to be a chant at the Metropolitano stadium combining the papacy and the IRA, a song described as a staple of a small section of England support but rarely heard in stadiums. Under Uefa's disciplinary regulations, insulting the human dignity of a group of persons on grounds including religion carries a minimum penalty of a fine and either a partial stadium closure or an away-ticket restriction. A further such offence by England fans within the two-year probation would trigger the ban for a competitive match.

The FA said it "will not tolerate this unacceptable and deeply offensive chanting," and would work with the police who attend all England home and away matches to identify the perpetrators and take the necessary action.

The match had been preceded by reminders from England manager Thomas Tuchel to his squad of anti-racism protocols, given the last England match in Madrid, in 2004, was marred by abuse aimed at the team's black players. Spain was punished in a parallel case last month: its federation was fined £15,000 and ordered to play its next friendly behind a partially closed stadium after discriminatory chants aimed at Muslims during a March match against Egypt in Barcelona.

The rulings leave both federations on notice in the same competition cycle. England's next competitive away fixture falls in the spring, inside the probationary window; the FA's response, and its cooperation with police identifications, will determine whether the suspended restriction is ever activated.
