Former Representative Mary Peltola has defeated two-term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan of Alaska, The Associated Press projected Tuesday, electing the state's first Democratic senator since 2008 and extending the party's midterm gains in a state President Trump carried by 13 points in 2024.

"As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my career to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans' support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first," Peltola wrote on X Tuesday evening.

Peltola, who campaigned on a "fish, family and freedom" message of affordability and protection for Alaska's fisheries, took the seat held by Sullivan since 2015. Sullivan, a former state attorney general and Marine Corps veteran, had staked his re-election on his record on energy development and national security and on Mr. Trump's endorsement; the president travelled to Alaska in October to rally support for him.

The race drew national scrutiny for an episode involving a second candidate named Dan Sullivan. The New York Times reported that Democratic operatives recruited a retired teacher from the town of Petersburg, listed as Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., to draw votes from the incumbent. Peltola's campaign denied involvement. Alaska's Republican governor, Mike Dunleavy, ordered a state investigation, and the Justice Department opened a criminal investigation and issued subpoenas. No one in the race has been charged.

The Trump administration sued Alaska in October, seeking to delay certification if the similar names proved decisive. A federal judge dismissed the suit on November 1, ruling the administration lacked standing and its proposed remedy unworkable; the Justice Department appealed. Sullivan committed to accepting the results.

Alaska's ranked-choice system shaped the count: voters rank candidates, and last-place contenders are eliminated in rounds until a majority emerges. Peltola's crossover appeal was visible as early as her 2022 special election, when she became the first Alaska Native elected to Congress and Republicans ranked her above former Governor Sarah Palin. She won a full term that year and lost her House seat in 2024.

Election officials will complete the remaining certification steps in coming weeks, with the Justice Department's appeal pending. Peltola's victory adds to Democratic wins in Texas, Ohio, Iowa and South Carolina that retook the Senate for the party.
