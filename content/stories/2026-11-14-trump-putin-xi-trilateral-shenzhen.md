President Donald Trump will meet President Xi Jinping of China and President Vladimir Putin of Russia on Thursday in Shenzhen, China, in the first trilateral meeting among the three leaders, the White House confirmed Friday. The session will conclude Trump's visit to China and take place on the margins of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit hosted in the technology hub.

"President Trump will conclude his visit to China with a trilateral meeting between the president of the United States, the president of the People's Republic of China and the president of the Russian Federation," White House spokesperson Olivia Wales said in a call with reporters. Wales said Trump would discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons, whose proliferation he "believes is very dangerous."

A senior U.S. official said no agreement is anticipated. The official described the session as an "entry point" and "the first conversation." The last nuclear arms pact between the United States and Russia, New START, expired in February, removing caps on the two largest arsenals for the first time in more than half a century. Trump rejected Moscow's call for a one-year extension and has called for a "new, improved and modernized" deal that includes China.

Pentagon estimates put China's arsenal at more than 600 warheads, against more than 5,000 each for the United States and Russia. Beijing has shown little interest in a nuclear pact while its stockpile remains far smaller. "We do think it's time for China to sort of step up, you know, to the adults' table," the senior U.S. official said.

The official said Trump intends to discuss the Iran war with both leaders but not the war in Ukraine, which is being handled on a separate track that China does not join. No bilateral meeting with Putin is scheduled, though the two will appear together at summit events. Elon Musk will be the only business leader in the presidential delegation, and he will not take part in official meetings. The officials said Trump is not expected to raise the espionage investigation into a suspected Chinese intelligence asset during the trip, and there are no plans for engagement with North Korea.

Trump embraced Xi after a truce ended their short-lived trade war last year and has cultivated his relationship with Putin throughout the Ukraine conflict. The Shenzhen meeting tests whether three-way talks can begin where two-way arms control has stalled. The summit session is set for Thursday.

Update, November 17, 2026: China's foreign ministry confirmed Monday that the meeting will proceed as planned. "On November 19, Chinese President Xi Jinping will hold a China-Russia-US heads of state meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin and US President Donald Trump in Shenzhen," the ministry said in a brief statement. Trump departed Monday for the ASEAN and APEC meetings that bracket the summit. Russia and Ukraine exchanged strikes on bridges across their shared border in the days before the meeting, as Moscow and Kyiv jockeyed ahead of talks in which the three powers will take part.

Update — November 19, 2026: The summit is under way. Mr. Trump reached Shenzhen on Wednesday evening, and Mr. Xi personally greeted him on the airport tarmac, reciprocating the personal welcome Mr. Trump extended to the Chinese leader at their previous meeting. Mr. Putin, who arrived earlier, was welcomed by Politburo Standing Committee member Cai Qi. At the gala dinner, Mr. Putin walked over to Mr. Trump's seat and shook his hand. The two then held a brief informal "pull-aside" in a separate room of the Kylin Villa reception compound; pool footage showed Mr. Trump, accompanied by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, approaching Mr. Putin immediately on entering the room. A US official said Mr. Trump planned a separate meeting with Mr. Putin on Thursday, in addition to the trilateral session, which the three leaders were scheduled to open later Thursday.

Update — November 20, 2026: The trilateral meeting was held Thursday at the Kylin Villa and lasted about 90 minutes, the first gathering of sitting US, Chinese and Russian leaders. The three governments issued a joint "peace declaration" listing four areas of cooperation: steady energy supply to world markets, United Nations reform, limiting the powers of the International Criminal Court, and a tentative commitment to discuss nuclear disarmament and non-proliferation. Mr. Trump said the three men were "making history." Mr. Putin said a new page in the history of diplomacy had been written and said his relationship with Mr. Trump rested on "similar civilisational values." Mr. Xi used the hosting role to present China as an equal alongside the United States and Russia. No concrete arms-control accord was announced. Assessments of the outcome diverged: The Guardian's diplomatic editor judged the summit a footnote rather than a new page in history, while the South China Morning Post reported the declaration's substance suggested more than a photo opportunity. The meeting came as Ukraine struck Russia's bridge link to Crimea and as Russia's offensive in Ukraine approached its five-year mark.
