President Donald Trump will meet President Xi Jinping of China and President Vladimir Putin of Russia on Thursday in Shenzhen, China, in the first trilateral meeting among the three leaders, the White House confirmed Friday. The session will conclude Trump's visit to China and take place on the margins of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit hosted in the technology hub.

"President Trump will conclude his visit to China with a trilateral meeting between the president of the United States, the president of the People's Republic of China and the president of the Russian Federation," White House spokesperson Olivia Wales said in a call with reporters. Wales said Trump would discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons, whose proliferation he "believes is very dangerous."

A senior U.S. official said no agreement is anticipated. The official described the session as an "entry point" and "the first conversation." The last nuclear arms pact between the United States and Russia, New START, expired in February, removing caps on the two largest arsenals for the first time in more than half a century. Trump rejected Moscow's call for a one-year extension and has called for a "new, improved and modernized" deal that includes China.

Pentagon estimates put China's arsenal at more than 600 warheads, against more than 5,000 each for the United States and Russia. Beijing has shown little interest in a nuclear pact while its stockpile remains far smaller. "We do think it's time for China to sort of step up, you know, to the adults' table," the senior U.S. official said.

The official said Trump intends to discuss the Iran war with both leaders but not the war in Ukraine, which is being handled on a separate track that China does not join. No bilateral meeting with Putin is scheduled, though the two will appear together at summit events. Elon Musk will be the only business leader in the presidential delegation, and he will not take part in official meetings. The officials said Trump is not expected to raise the espionage investigation into a suspected Chinese intelligence asset during the trip, and there are no plans for engagement with North Korea.

Trump embraced Xi after a truce ended their short-lived trade war last year and has cultivated his relationship with Putin throughout the Ukraine conflict. The Shenzhen meeting tests whether three-way talks can begin where two-way arms control has stalled. The summit session is set for Thursday.
