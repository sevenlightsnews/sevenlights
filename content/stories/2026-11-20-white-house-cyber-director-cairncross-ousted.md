Sean Cairncross was fired as the White House national cyber director on Thursday, days after a wave of technology outages disrupted United States air travel, banking and national security systems. Kirsten A. Davies, the Department of Defense chief information officer, will hold the post in an acting capacity, officials said.

"The president fully supported firing Sean Cairncross from his position," a person close to the White House told Reuters. Cairncross declined to comment. Axios, which first reported the departure, cited officials who said chief of staff Stephen Miller pushed for the change because Cairncross "lacked the strong cyber credentials to perform the job."

The role oversees cybersecurity policy and strategy across the federal government. Cairncross, a former executive at the Republican National Committee, had led the office since his confirmation in 2025.

Outages struck regularly during the past week. The Federal Aviation Administration ordered a nationwide halt to air travel, a Microsoft failure disabled email services for businesses and government users, and the National Security Agency's own network was briefly disrupted. The disruptions drew sharp criticism of the administration's response. The top United States intelligence official said Wednesday that the failures were separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes, not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.

Mr. Trump told Americans to "relax, everything's good," calling the outages "individual events."

The dismissal leaves the coordination of federal cyber policy under an acting official while questions about the causes of the outages remain unresolved. The next permanent director will require Senate confirmation, and the chamber comes under Democratic control in January, setting up a contest over the appointment.

Davies retains her Pentagon post while acting at the White House. Her tenure as acting director is expected to run at least until a successor is nominated and confirmed.
