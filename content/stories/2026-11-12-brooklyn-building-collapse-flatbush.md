A three-storey building in Brooklyn collapsed into an adjacent excavation site at about 4 p.m. Wednesday, killing at least two people and trapping others beneath the rubble, officials said. More than 250 first responders dug through the debris in the Flatbush section of the borough.

Officials said at a Wednesday evening news conference that two people died, four were taken to hospital in critical condition and twelve more were in stable condition. One firefighter was treated for minor injuries, and seven children were among the patients. More than 17 people were injured overall. Officials said they did not know how many people remained at the collapse site.

Hours after the collapse, at about 6:30 p.m., firefighters pulled a dust-covered person, alive and moving, from a hole dug into the rubble pile and rushed the survivor by stretcher to a waiting ambulance.

The building, on Flatbush Avenue and dated to 1930 in city records, had stood at the edge of a lot where a one-storey structure had been demolished for a construction project. The site was being excavated, and the collapsed building fell into the open pit, filling it with brick and wood. An adjoining building that shared a wall with the collapsed structure remained standing, but its wall was sheared off, exposing bedrooms and closets. Household goods and clothing lay among smashed concrete and twisted metal.

New York City Fire Department Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore called the rescue mission "extremely dangerous" and said people from the collapsed building and from the construction site had been trapped. Rescuers used dogs and dug by hand, tossing chunks of rubble aside as darkness fell, and worked by flashlight and floodlight.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani said first responders "arrived in under a minute and have spent hours working in dangerous and highly technical conditions to rescue New Yorkers." He asked New Yorkers to hold the affected community in their hearts as operations continued.

Authorities had not determined what caused the collapse. Messages seeking comment were left with the architect, property owner and general contractor listed in city records for the construction project. Searches were continuing through the night, and a city investigation into the cause was expected.
