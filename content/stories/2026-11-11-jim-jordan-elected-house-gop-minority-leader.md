House Republicans elected Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio as their minority leader on Tuesday, choosing a conservative who built his career antagonizing the party's own leadership to head a conference reeling from last week's midterm defeats.

Jordan, the chairman of the House Judiciary Committee and a founder of the ultra-conservative Freedom Caucus, defeated Rep. Steve Scalise of Louisiana, the outgoing majority leader, in a closed-door secret ballot of the conference. Scalise, the No. 2 House Republican since 2014, trailed Jordan by 33 votes, Axios reported. The choice makes Jordan the chamber's top Republican, succeeding Speaker Mike Johnson, who declined last week to seek the post. The Hill described the result as "an extraordinary leap for a conservative icon who made his name on Capitol Hill antagonizing party leaders" and noted that Republicans chose to lead "a diminished conference" in the next Congress.

The election settled the most consequential contest open in the conference after the 2026 elections reduced its ranks. Democrats are set to take the House majority when the 120th Congress convenes in January. The Washington Examiner said Jordan's ascent "represents a quiet admission from House Republicans that the conference needed a new playbook if they hope to rebuild." Semafor reported that with Jordan as leader, House Republicans "gear up for a fight."

Jordan first won election to the House in 2007 and helped found the Freedom Caucus, the conservative bloc that has repeatedly clashed with House leadership. He has chaired the Judiciary Committee since Republicans took the chamber in 2023. His selection signals a conference prepared to press its grievances from the minority.

Scalise, the Louisiana Republican who had served as majority leader since 2023 and had been widely expected to carry the conference through the transition, withdrew from contention only after the ballot. Party officials gave no immediate accounting of the vote margin, and the conference scheduled no further leadership ballots for Tuesday.

Jordan takes the leadership when the new Congress is sworn in in January. The conference will also elect a minority whip and policy committee officers in the coming weeks, and Democrats have yet to choose their nominee for speaker.
