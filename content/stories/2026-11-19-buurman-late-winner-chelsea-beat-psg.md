Veerle Buurman scored late at the near post to give Chelsea a 1-0 win over a resilient Paris Saint-Germain in the Women's Champions League on Wednesday, lifting Chelsea temporarily to the top of the league-phase table at AFC Wimbledon's stadium.

Chelsea did little that was pretty. They held 64 per cent possession in the first half and registered seven attempts on goal to PSG's six, but could not carve open the visitors' backline. PSG had nullified the threat of Ellie Carpenter, who had become the focal point of Chelsea's attack with the striker Melvine Malard injured. Erin Cuthbert, Maika Hamano and Lucy Bronze were introduced at the hour mark in search of the decisive blow. It came from Buurman, who scrambled the winner in.

The Dutch defender had stolen the show in Chelsea's 1-0 defeat of Manchester City on Saturday, coming on after 12 minutes to neutralise Khadija Shaw and earn player of the match honours. "She is a top, young talent and is so mature on and off the pitch," the coach Sonia Bompastor said. "She is a confident player and a really complete player."

Chelsea had been frustrated by a 0-0 draw with Häcken last week that dropped them out of the top four automatic qualification places. "Sometimes in football you need to find a way to win and the result tonight was really important for us," Bompastor said.

PSG, who had lost at home to Bayern Munich before this trip, were denied a point late and remain 10th, dangerously close to slipping out of the playoff places with one league-phase match to play. Chelsea have now won three of four Champions League meetings with PSG, having progressed past them in the 2018-19 quarter-finals and won both group meetings in 2022-23.

Elsewhere on Wednesday, Lauren Hemp and Khadija Shaw scored off the bench in Manchester City's 2-0 win at Häcken, with Signe Bruun's late double securing Real Madrid a 3-1 victory over 10-man Internazionale. Juventus joined City and Madrid on nine points after late goals beat Leuven 2-0. City and Real Madrid remain unbeaten after five league-phase games.

Chelsea's final league-phase fixture closes the group stage in December; a top-four finish would spare them the knockout playoff round.
