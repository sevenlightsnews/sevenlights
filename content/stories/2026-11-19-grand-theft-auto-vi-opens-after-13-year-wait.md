Rockstar Games released Grand Theft Auto VI on Thursday for PlayStation and Xbox consoles, the first mainline entry in the series in 13 years and a launch billed as perhaps the largest entertainment release on record. Early reviews from critics were strongly positive.

The game retails for $110, with an ultimate edition at $140, and some estimates put its development budget above $2 billion. The franchise has sold more than 470 million units since 1997. Grand Theft Auto V, released in 2013, has sold more than 230 million copies and still holds revenue records for an entertainment launch.

Employers moved to manage the release well before it arrived. Lawnkeepers Landscaping, a six-employee firm in Surrey, British Columbia, made Thursday a vacation day. Co-owner Angad Toor said he proposed the holiday months ago. His father, Bal Toor, first thought it a joke, then agreed, reasoning that November is a slower month and the crew had worked six-day weeks through recent storms. "They can bond and hang out together," he said. "Some of the guys don't know GTA, but it's just something they're going to get together, order pizza."

Companies elsewhere weighed similar steps against the "GTA flu" — release-day absences real or claimed. The game had already been nominated for game of the year awards before reaching shops.

Mia Consalvo, a game-studies professor at Concordia University, said anticipation reflected a thinner market for blockbusters as studio budgets balloon and layoffs continue. Consoles that once fell in price now rise, she noted, "and so there is this pressure for the game to really stand out."

Victor Lucas, a Vancouver-based critic and creator of the Electric Playground, said Rockstar has set the template for tentpole releases since Grand Theft Auto III, with cultural reach that draws major musicians into each installment. "It will also be a master class in how to make money," he said.

The launch has not been free of controversy. Former Rockstar workers in Britain have taken the company to court, alleging it dismissed several employees, including three in Canada, for attempting to unionize, part of a longer record of long-hours work at the studio.

The release closes a stretch of repeated delays: Rockstar pushed the game from 2025 twice before setting this November date. Analysts expect first-week sales to exceed those of any previous game.
