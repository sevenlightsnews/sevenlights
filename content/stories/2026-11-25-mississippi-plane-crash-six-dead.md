![US Coast Guard rescue boat on grey Gulf of Mexico waters at dusk, crew in orange gear scanning the surface with searchlights, low coastline on the horizon, calm choppy sea, no readable text, editorial photojournalism photograph, 3:2](/images/stories/mississippi-plane-crash-six-dead.webp)

Coast Guard divers recovered the bodies of six people, including two children, from the fuselage of a small aircraft that crashed into the Gulf of Mexico off the coast of Mississippi on Tuesday, the service said.

The Cessna 210 was flying from New Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida, according to the flight-tracking website FlightAware. Air traffic control lost contact with the plane at about 15:45 local time, while it was descending at a rate of 10,000ft a minute, the Coast Guard said. The aircraft went down in the Gulf between Pascagoula and Horn Island, part of the Gulf Islands National Seashore, officials said.

Rescuers spent about four hours searching the waters in a coordinated effort with partner agencies. They initially found three life jackets, a life raft and the plane's cabin floating on the surface, Coast Guard spokesperson Juan Ros said. Divers from the Mississippi Department of Marine Resources later located the bodies of four adults and two boys, aged 8 and 10, inside the fuselage.

"While this is not the outcome any of us had hoped for, we are grateful for the strong interagency co-ordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families," said Cmdr Trevor Blount, the Coast Guard rescue mission co-ordinator.

Joe Spraggins, the Coast Guard's executive director, called recovery of the dead "a solemn responsibility" and said the dive team was working with federal and local partners "with the utmost care, professionalism and respect."

The identities of the dead were not immediately released. The Federal Aviation Administration and the National Transportation Safety Board said they were aware of the crash; the NTSB will lead the investigation into its cause. No findings on the aircraft's condition or the weather along the route were announced Tuesday night.

The crash route, from Louisiana down the Gulf coast toward southwest Florida, is a heavily traveled corridor for general aviation. It follows a Learjet 60 crash in south Florida earlier this month that killed all nine people aboard.
