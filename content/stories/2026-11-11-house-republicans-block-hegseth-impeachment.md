![Empty defense ministry press briefing room after a news conference, rows of folding chairs facing a lectern with microphones, scattered notepads, two flagpoles at the back wall, harsh overhead light](/images/stories/house-republicans-block-hegseth-impeachment.webp)

The House voted 211 to 204 on Tuesday to refer articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, a step that effectively killed the effort led by Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky, the lone Republican to vote against the referral.

Massie, who introduced a 34-page impeachment resolution on Monday, brought eight articles. Three charged that Mr. Hegseth waged war against Iran without congressional authorization in violation of the 1973 War Powers Resolution; the conflict is in its ninth month. Others cited a strike on an Iranian school earlier this year, the Pentagon's boat strikes in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific that have killed well over 200 people, the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro, and the department's scrutiny of Sen. Mark Kelly over a video the senator joined in urging service members to refuse unlawful orders.

The vote ran along party lines. Eleven Democrats and seven Republicans failed to vote, among them former Speaker Nancy Pelosi, the Washington Examiner reported. Mr. Hegseth held a news conference at the Pentagon shortly after, calling Massie a "disgruntled congressman" and likening the effort to the two impeachments of President Trump. "Both times he came back stronger, and the American people sent him back to the White House. Now they're trying a third time. This time against a member of his Cabinet," he said. "They will fail again, and if standing with President Trump puts me in that company, I'll take that company every day of the week."

Defending the boat strikes, Mr. Hegseth added: "We sank the boats carrying poison into our communities. We put a narco dictator in handcuffs. These are not high crimes. This is the job, and it's the job well done." Vice President JD Vance said on X that the question of impeachable crimes "is absurd and the answer is: obviously not," arguing the effort punished Mr. Hegseth for reorganizing the Pentagon rather than for Iran. "This isn't about Iran, this is about the fact that Pete took on the bureaucracy at the Pentagon," Mr. Vance said.

President Trump, who gave Mr. Hegseth "an 'A' PLUS, PLUS, PLUS" on Truth Social, posted that "every single Republican, except Thomas Massie," had voted to send the articles to committee.

Massie said after the vote he would not introduce another resolution. "I wasn't surprised, but I was disappointed," he said. "My diagnosis of Congress is these people know what to do. They just can't bring themselves to do it, even when it would benefit them politically." Massie retires in January after losing his primary. Democrats, who retake both chambers this winter, have not said whether they would open a formal inquiry.
