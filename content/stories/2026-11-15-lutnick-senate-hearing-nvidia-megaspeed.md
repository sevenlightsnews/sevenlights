Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee over the Commerce Department's handling of Megaspeed, the Singapore data-center company at the center of the chip-smuggling allegations that cost Jensen Huang his job at Nvidia.

"He proactively agreed to appear voluntarily before Congress," Commerce spokeswoman Kristen Eichamer told the Daily Signal on Friday. Senator Tim Scott of South Carolina, the committee's chairman, has asked Lutnick to appear for a public hearing next month, the same window in which he has asked Huang to testify voluntarily.

The chairs of the House Select Committee on China, the Foreign Affairs Committee and the Intelligence Committee wrote jointly to request a closed-door interview with Huang on December 1. "We need to know the full extent to which our national security has been put at risk," the chairs said. If Huang had not accepted by Friday evening, the chairs said, the Foreign Affairs Committee would issue a subpoena. Senator Kevin Cramer of North Dakota said on CNBC Thursday, moments before Nvidia announced the resignation, that he would support subpoenaing Huang; a spokesperson said Friday that his position had not changed.

Scott warned that the Banking Committee too was prepared to use its subpoena power. "The American people deserve answers," he wrote on X.

Huang stepped down as chief executive and from the board on Thursday after reports of his affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed whom U.S. authorities have concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. Megaspeed, which gained access as an Nvidia cloud partner to chips restricted from export to China, is under investigation in the United States and Singapore for allegedly smuggling those chips into China. The company has denied the allegations, and China has called the espionage claims a smear.

The episode has exposed a jurisdictional fight inside the administration. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Treasury, the Defense Department, the U.S. Trade Representative and the State Department had favored placing Megaspeed on the entity list for months. "But Commerce has the pen," Bessent told CNBC. "Commerce has the pen, and you will have to ask them about their internal processes." The Daily Signal reported that Lutnick has repeatedly opposed presidential plans to crack down on companies suspected of transferring advanced technology to China.

Lawmakers also question the administration's closeness to Huang, who built Nvidia into the chipmaker at the center of the AI boom and whose resignation has not quieted the demands for testimony.

The December 1 deadline for Huang's acceptance and Lutnick's hearing date mark the next steps. Committees have scheduled no votes on subpoenas as of Saturday.
