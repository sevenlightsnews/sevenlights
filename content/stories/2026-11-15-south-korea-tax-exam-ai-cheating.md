The highest-scoring candidate in South Korea's national tax accountant examination confessed on Sunday to using generative artificial intelligence to cheat in both of this year's rounds, ending months of dispute over results that sceptics had called too good to be true.

The candidate, identified by the surname Han, ranked first in the qualification exams held in April and July. He scored 397.5 points of 400 in the first round, missing only one of 160 questions. Speaking on camera wearing a face mask on the YouTube channel of a lawyer, Han said he took photographs of the exam questions with a microcamera, sent them to an AI tool and checked the answers on a smartphone. "I cheated using electronic devices and generative AI. My passing the exam with the top score was not the result of fair effort," he said.

The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency said it received a written confession through the test taker's lawyer on Sunday morning and assigned the case to investigators. Han had earlier denied suspicions that he used smart glasses, accusations that circulated after his near-perfect marks became public.

The scandal rattled a country in which standardised tests largely determine university admission, career entry and social standing, and it landed days before Thursday's annual national college entrance exam. "The presidential office views the matter very seriously, and because the college entrance exam is next week, we have been working hard to prepare countermeasures," a senior presidential office official told reporters on Sunday. The official said reports of possible cheating in the certified public accountant examination were also concerning, and that the chief of staff for policy would convene an emergency meeting to address the issue.

Prime Minister Han Seong-sook acknowledged on Friday that public concern over AI-assisted cheating was real. Her office said the government would prepare countermeasures "thoroughly in advance" and oversee the process "until the very end" to ensure the exam is conducted fairly and stably.

Police said the investigation would examine how the microcamera passed inspection and whether others exploited the same method. The entrance exam is set to proceed on Thursday under tightened screening.
