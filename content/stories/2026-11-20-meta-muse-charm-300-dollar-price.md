Meta intends to sell the Muse Charm, a pocket gadget for talking to its Muse artificial intelligence agent, for $300, according to a report by The Information that was confirmed in tone by outlets that sought comment from the company. The device would come with up to 12 months of free 5G data service from Verizon.

Meta introduced the Charm at its Meta Connect developer conference in September, promising to ship it "in time for the holidays in December." Chief Executive Mark Zuckerberg said at the time that the gadget packed "the whole Muse experience, including the real-time voice and avatar stack into something that fits on a keychain and is always available to talk to." A fingerprint sensor verifies the owner before commands are accepted, so the user need not unlock a phone or open an app.

A Meta spokesperson, Tyler Yee, said the company was "charmed by the interest in our AI devices" and would have more to share soon. The Information also reported that Meta's work on a smartwatch has been pushed to 2027 while it brings the Charm to market.

The pricing report underlines how much weight the company has placed on the agent. Muse has drawn more than 10 million users, Meta has published open-source guides for builders to make their own Muse-compatible gadgets, and the company has shipped Muse apps for Wear OS and watchOS. Its strategy is to carry the agent beyond the smartphone, where Meta's products are subject to Apple's and Google's rules, onto hardware Meta controls. The company is also building commerce tools that would charge businesses a fee when Muse users make purchases through the agent; Mr. Zuckerberg has said he believes Muse could one day generate more revenue per user than Meta's existing apps.

The Charm's price puts it above the toy-scale gadgets it resembles and below the smartphone it is meant to complement. Whether consumers will pay for a dedicated line to an AI assistant is the question the December launch will test; Meta has opened sign-up lists for sale notifications but has not confirmed the price itself.
