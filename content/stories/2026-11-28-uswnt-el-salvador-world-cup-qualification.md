The United States women's national team beat El Salvador 8-0 Friday in the quarterfinals of the Concacaf W Championship, qualifying the Americans for the 2027 Women's World Cup in Brazil and, with it, the 2028 Olympic tournament. The win was the largest of manager Emma Hayes's tenure and kept the United States present at all ten Women's World Cups staged to date.

The scoring opened in the ninth minute from a rehearsed set piece, Kennedy Wesley prodding the ball home. Tierna Davidson headed in Swanson's free kick in the 31st minute, and Swanson herself scored a minute later from a threaded pass by Wilson. Ashley Sanchez then completed a hat trick begun in the 36th minute, sliding in Alyssa Thompson's cross and adding twice more inside six minutes and 27 seconds. Wilson scored in the 44th minute to send the hosts into the interval 7-0 before a further goal completed the rout.

The match was the first competitive fixture for the United States since the Olympic gold-medal final in August 2024, two years filled with friendlies. The suspense, as the result indicates, was brief: the United States, ranked second in the world, held an overwhelming share of possession against a 78th-ranked El Salvador side that coach Eric Acuna had set up in an open shape rather than the expected low block.

The four quarterfinal winners fill Concacaf's places for the World Cup. Jamaica qualified earlier Friday with a 3-0 win over Costa Rica; Canada and Mexico play their quarterfinals Saturday. The final standings of the continental tournament will be settled over the weekend.

Sanchez, recently named the National Women's Soccer League's most valuable player, was the match's standout. Hayes said her team had stressed finishing in the final third throughout the week's training. The United States will use the semifinals as preparation for the World Cup year, with a full international calendar ahead of the Brazil tournament next summer.
