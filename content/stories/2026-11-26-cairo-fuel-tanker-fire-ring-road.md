![Blackened wreckage of an overturned fuel tanker with dying embers on a night ring road, thick smoke column over a dense district, charred cars on wet asphalt, fire engine lights in the distance, editorial photojournalism photograph, 3:2](/images/stories/cairo-fuel-tanker-fire-ring-road.webp)

A fuel tanker overturned and ignited a huge fire on the ring road encircling Cairo on Wednesday, killing at least 31 people and injuring more than 20, Egyptian authorities said. The blaze broke out above a tunnel in the Al-Warraq district of Giza, a densely populated area of Greater Cairo, the public prosecution said.

The health ministry said 39 ambulances were dispatched and the injured were taken to nearby hospitals. Fifteen were hospitalized, four of them in critical condition, and the ministry raised the emergency alert level. The health minister, Khaled Abdel Ghaffar, said the death toll was preliminary and could rise, and traveled to the scene and to the hospitals.

State media reported that the truck, loaded with fuel, overturned on the crowded highway and burst into flames, engulfing nearby vehicles. Preliminary estimates found that 27 cars and motorcycles were charred, the prosecution said. Footage showed a large fireball and a column of black smoke above the district as motorists fled; rescue workers pulled people from burning cars.

The public prosecutor's office opened an investigation and ordered DNA samples from badly burned victims whose identities had not been established. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly ordered an inquiry into the cause of the accident, and President Abdel Fatah al-Sisi expressed condolences to the families of the victims.

Ahmed Sameer, stuck in traffic as the road was blocked, said he saw at least eight bodies removed from burned vehicles. "It was a huge fire, the cars got stuck together because of the fire," he said. "It was a shocking scene."

The health ministry said the ring road was reopened after the wreckage was cleared and traffic partially resumed. Serious highway accidents occur frequently in Egypt, where authorities have cited poor maintenance and lax enforcement of road safety rules. Fuel and gas tanker fires have repeatedly caused mass casualties on highways and in industrial districts of Cairo.

The prosecution said casualty figures and property damage were still being assessed. An official statement on the cause of the crash had not been issued, and the government said it would determine further measures after a full report.
