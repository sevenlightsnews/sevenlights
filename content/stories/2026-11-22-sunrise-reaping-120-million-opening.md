"The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping" opened to $120 million in North America and $225 million around the world, Lionsgate's largest opening in more than a decade. The prequel, the sixth film in the series, earned $105 million from 40,000 screens in 84 territories in its first weekend, according to studio estimates reported Sunday.

The result reverses the franchise's most recent showing. "The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes," the 2023 instalment, opened well below the series' earlier peaks, and industry observers had questioned whether the property could return to its former audience. The new film's worldwide opening exceeded that of "Michael," the year's earlier Lionsgate success, which opened to $218.8 million globally on its way to $372 million domestically.

The film stars Joseph Zada and Elle Fanning and returns to the world of the original trilogy a generation earlier. Critics and box office analysts attributed the opening to the franchise's built-in audience and to a release positioned in the holiday corridor, where the series has historically performed best.

The weekend's other entertainment headline belonged to a different medium. Rockstar's "Grand Theft Auto VI," released this month, generated nearly $4 billion in its launch day on pre-sales and first-day sales, by far the largest opening for any entertainment product, and dominated attention in the same period. The film industry read the Hunger Games figure as evidence that theatrical releases can still command mass audiences when a proven franchise is at stake.

Lionsgate's shares rose on the estimates. The studio follows the film with an expanded release of "I Play Rocky," which posted a rare A+ CinemaScore in its wide expansion, and the limited release "Elsinore," which drew a strong per-screen average.

The franchise's next chapter is already in development. Exhibitors expect the holiday corridor to sustain the film through December.

Update, November 23, 2026: The film also opened at No. 1 in mainland China, taking 39.3 million yuan ($5.9 million) in the Nov. 20-22 weekend, according to Artisan Gateway data reported by Variety.

Update, November 24, 2026: Weekend runners-up were Farrelly's "I Play Rocky," which grossed $9.32 million in just under 2,500 theaters on its wide expansion, and "Ebenezer," down nearly 60 percent to $7.7 million in a second weekend. "Godzilla Minus Zero" fell nearly 80 percent, to $1.867 million, dropping from third to eighth. "Sunrise on the Reaping" holds a 69 percent critical rating on Rotten Tomatoes against a 94 percent audience score, with an A- CinemaScore, indicating strong word of mouth into the holiday corridor.
