A selloff in global stocks that has accelerated since Anthropic's record initial public offering in the United States is clouding the outlook for the next wave of share sales by China's artificial intelligence companies, Bloomberg reported.

DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and StepFun are among Anthropic's direct competitors weighing or preparing public offerings. Their plans do not hinge entirely on the performance of the maker of the Claude chatbot, but the faltering global AI trade is adding complexity to highly anticipated listings, Bloomberg said. The shifting regulatory environment is also in play.

The market backdrop hardened through last week. The selloff worsened as the week wore on, and not even the largest initial public offering in history could slow it, The Wall Street Journal said. The Journal put the wipeout in artificial intelligence-linked market value on Wall Street at $4 trillion.

Anthropic's own debut illustrated the split. The company listed as a public benefit corporation whose Long-Term Benefit Trust appoints four of seven directors, with the founders effectively controlling the appointment of the remaining three. Each of the seven co-founders holds a stake worth more than $20 billion, and the company's 5,300 employees hold an estimated $232 billion in combined equity. Non-founder employees were permitted to sell up to 10 per cent of their holdings on the first trading day.

Critics say listing will test the company's pledge to put safety ahead of commercial success, NPR reported. Legal experts noted that the trust can be dissolved with the approval of all seven co-founders and a majority vote of shareholders holding voting rights, a route that could neutralize the trust's control in a dispute over a safety decision. Chief Executive Dario Amodei said this month the company will "pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe," and will not bend to business or political pressure.

For the Chinese developers, the arithmetic of listing now runs through a colder market. Anthropic's record debut was followed by a rocky first full day of trading as markets plunged on worries about artificial intelligence spending, The New York Times reported.
