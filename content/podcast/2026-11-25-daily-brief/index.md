2|Today we begin with a New York jury finding a Chinese telecommunications giant guilty of stealing trade secrets. Then: the Army refuses survivors a seat at a firing-squad execution. A small plane goes down in the Gulf of Mexico with six people aboard. And a royal family patriarch faces federal charges. In politics: a medal of honor awarded behind closed doors, an embattled chip chief summoned by Congress, and unsealed records from a search of a congresswoman's home. 3|Our first story takes us to a federal courthouse in New York. There, a jury has convicted the Chinese telecommunications giant Huawei of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and of bank fraud. The verdict came after a three-month trial. Jurors acquitted the company of a racketeering count and of obstruction of justice. The case was first brought in twenty nineteen, when the United States announced criminal charges as Huawei grew into one of the world's largest suppliers of wireless network equipment. Court records name Cisco Systems, Motorola Solutions, T-Mobile, Quintel USA and Fujitsu among the companies harmed. Prosecutors described a long pattern of thefts. They said Huawei once sold routers containing a competitor's copied source code. In one episode, a worker slipped into a rival's trade-show booth in Chicago to photograph the circuits inside another company's device. On another occasion, a Huawei employee removed a robotic arm from a competitor's laboratory so it could be photographed and measured. Huawei's attorney said the company will appeal. He called the acquittal on the racketeering count a repudiation of the government's central theory. Sentencing was not immediately set.

4|Turning now to the United States, the Army has refused three survivors of the Fort Hood shooting permission to witness the execution of Nidal Hasan. Hasan is scheduled to die by firing squad at the military prison at Fort Leavenworth on December third. On Tuesday he asked a federal court to halt it. The three survivors had argued for a broader definition of victim under the military justice code. Officials cited security and space limits in denying reconsideration. A letter from the prison's commandant said the decision cannot be appealed again. Retired Colonel Kathy Platoni, who would have been Hasan's supervisor on a planned deployment to Afghanistan, said the refusal felt like a betrayal. She said she wanted to look Hasan in the eye and tell him he did not get her. Hasan was convicted of killing thirteen people and wounding more than thirty at the Texas army post in two thousand nine. If carried out, the execution would be the first by the United States military since nineteen sixty-one, and the first by firing squad since the Second World War. A plan to broadcast the execution was withdrawn after backlash from both parties.

5|Meanwhile, off the coast of Mississippi, Coast Guard divers recovered the bodies of six people from a small plane that crashed into the Gulf of Mexico on Tuesday. They include four adults and two boys, aged eight and ten. The Cessna two ten was flying from New Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida. Air traffic control lost contact at about a quarter to four in the afternoon, while the plane was descending at a rate of ten thousand feet a minute. It went down between Pascagoula and Horn Island, part of the Gulf Islands National Seashore. Rescuers searched the waters for about four hours. They first found three life jackets, a life raft and the aircraft's cabin floating on the surface. The National Transportation Safety Board will lead the investigation into the cause. The identities of the dead were not released. The route is a heavily traveled corridor for private aviation, and this crash follows a Learjet disaster in south Florida earlier this month that killed all nine people aboard.

6|Our next story reaches into Britain's royal family by marriage. A federal grand jury in Los Angeles has indicted Thomas Markle on two counts of possessing child sexual abuse material. He is the eighty-two-year-old father of Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex. Each count carries a maximum of twenty years in prison. Court documents say the material includes at least one image involving a child under twelve. Federal agents found the images during a search of a Los Angeles storage unit that Markle allegedly rented for almost two decades. He was arrested in November at an assisted-living facility in Florida, where he is a patient. A judge has ruled him mentally competent to stand trial, and he is expected to be sent to California for arraignment. Markle denies possessing the images. Police in the Philippines say separate complaints were filed against him in Cebu province. He called those claims sick lies. The duchess has been estranged from her father since before her marriage to Prince Harry in twenty eighteen.

7|Turning now to politics, and our first political story concerns Nvidia. The chipmaker's board voted unanimously on Monday to restore Jensen Huang as chief executive, one week after saying it did not intend to. The reversal followed a public pressure campaign by President Trump. Lawmakers of both parties demanded answers on Tuesday. Senator John Cornyn of Texas asked what the finding of no national-security risk was based on. Senator John Curtis of Utah said he wanted to know what had changed. Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich called the return a national security issue of enormous proportions. Three House committees announced that Huang will testify next week. He is also scheduled to appear before the Senate Banking Committee on December third. Democratic leaders pledged investigations when they take control of the House in January. Senator Elizabeth Warren called the decision reckless. Huang told employees he was thankful for a second chance. Nvidia shares rose more than thirty per cent on Monday, the largest one-day gain for a single company on record.

8|In other political news, President Trump awarded Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom at a White House ceremony on Tuesday. The nation's highest civilian honor went to his former personal lawyer. The event was listed as closed to the press, and no reporters were admitted. Before the ceremony, the White House posted a video saying Giuliani had left an undeniable mark on American history. Giuliani said afterward he was very humbled and very honored to receive the award from the president. His son Andrew called the moment incredibly special. Giuliani served as mayor of New York during the attacks of September eleventh, two thousand one, and his leadership in the aftermath made him a global figure. His standing later eroded. He lost his license to practice law after promoting false claims of election fraud. He also settled a defamation suit brought by Georgia election workers he had falsely accused of cheating. He filed for bankruptcy in twenty twenty-three. In twenty twenty-five he settled a billion-dollar lawsuit by the voting-machine maker Dominion. State charges in Georgia and Arizona were eventually dismissed.

9|Also in politics, a federal judge in Washington has released more than one hundred and sixty pages of records. The documents detail how the Justice Department and the courts debated a search warrant for the home of Representative Ilhan Omar of Minnesota. Chief Judge James Boasberg ordered portions of the sealed file made public after news organizations sought access. The records show a magistrate judge repeatedly pressed the Justice Department for briefings explaining its theory of probable cause. The department said the process included several rounds of affidavit revisions. Central to the dispute was the speech or debate clause, the constitutional shield for legislative acts. The list of items agents actually took from Omar's home is entirely redacted. The warrant itself remains sealed. About half of the released pages are mostly blacked out. The underlying investigation continues.

10|Staying with Nvidia, our business story asks what the company's comeback means for the wider economy. The board's about-face added roughly nine hundred and fifty billion dollars of market value in a single day. That episode has revived the phrase too big to fail, this time attached to a company rather than a bank. Nvidia is the second-largest stock on the American market, and investors treat Huang himself as a large part of that value. The company's reach runs through the financing chains of the artificial-intelligence economy. Nvidia has a deal with CoreWeave, a heavily indebted data-center operator. If CoreWeave cannot find customers, Nvidia will pay for the computing power itself. One analyst compared that to a car manufacturer leasing cars back from its own dealers. Even so, the stock trades at only about ten times forward earnings, a multiple close to that of a supermarket chain. And Congress has not accepted the reversal as final.

11|Turning to technology, the European Union's executive arm has confirmed a data theft. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier said the stolen information includes the names and email addresses of platform users, and the full scale of the theft is still under investigation. The platform is used by people outside the Commission to take courses on European Union policies. It has been taken offline. Regnier said no ransom demand had been received, and that attribution must wait for the investigation to conclude. The news outlet Euronews reported that the Commission suspects a China-backed group. That judgment rests partly on the hacking framework used, which has Chinese roots. The attack also disrupted an internal human resources system. Users whose data may have leaked have not been contacted individually.

15|The third sports story is a rules case from English women's football. An independent tribunal deducted three points from Crystal Palace in the Women's Super League. The tribunal found the breach inadvertent, and concluded Palace sought no unfair advantage. No party may appeal. Crystal Palace objected that the player involved was on the pitch for only forty-six seconds while the ball was in play, and called the penalty disproportionate. The move runs against the industry's recent direction, with American Idol heading to Georgia and The Masked Singer to New Jersey. The first season employed about one hundred and ten local cast and crew, and contributed an estimated five million dollars to the Australian economy. Season two will feature eight aspiring male dancers competing for a place on the parent show. Robert Irwin returns as host.
