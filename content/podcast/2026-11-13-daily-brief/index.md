Our first story takes us to Khan Younis in southern Gaza, where an Israeli missile strike killed six people shortly after midnight on Thursday. The missile hit the al-Yasin surgical building at Nasser hospital, the largest medical facility in southern Gaza. Among the dead was Doctor Rizq Abu Shaira, a fifty-two-year-old general surgeon. He was killed alongside a patient he had just operated on. Gaza's Health Ministry said about thirty people were wounded, and that medical staff and patients were among the dead. The hospital's director, Doctor Ahmed al-Fara, said Israeli forces fired two missiles and only one exploded. He said the strike forced the suspension of four departments, including radiology and the operating rooms. Israel's military said it targeted a Hamas commander who used the hospital as a headquarters, and said it took measures to limit civilian casualties. Doctor al-Fara said the hospital received no warning. Hamas denied the site was used for military purposes. Nasser hospital has been struck seven times during the war. The World Health Organization says it has documented more than one thousand attacks on health care in Gaza over three years.

In the United States capital, a physician living above a fitness studio shot and killed the building's manager on Thursday morning. The shooting happened shortly before eight in the Logan Circle neighborhood. Police said the gunman, fifty-five-year-old Matthew Lambert, also wounded five other people. He then killed himself during a standoff with negotiators. The interim police chief, Jeffrey Carroll, named the dead man as forty-six-year-old James Stuart. Stuart was the building's manager and the apparent target. Carroll said Lambert had been locked in a long-running conflict with the building's homeowners association. Four of the wounded were shot after Lambert retreated to his seventh-floor apartment and fired from its balcony. Those victims included three members of a construction crew working across the street. Investigators recovered a shotgun, a rifle and a handgun, and believe Lambert used all three. A fire broke out inside the building during the incident. Carroll said Lambert had broken a gas line in his fireplace, and that gasoline and flammable materials were found in the unit. The Federal Bureau of Investigation assisted the police department.

Turning to Washington politics, President Trump wrote on Truth Social on Thursday that the United States does not need a deal with Iran. He ordered the Vice-President to give his complete and total attention to corruption and crime in Democratic-run cities and states, naming Minnesota, California and Illinois. The directive pulled the Vice-President away from the Iran file he had personally managed. He had been working through intermediaries in Iran and Qatar. He was pursuing a ceasefire and a resumption of nuclear talks, after the war that began in February with a joint United States and Israeli strike on Iran. Trump created the Vice-President's fraud task force by executive order, and his post framed that mandate as his new center of gravity. It was not immediately clear whether the Iran portfolio would pass to another official or lapse. The war has strained the administration and the economy. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz after the February strikes, and fuel prices surged. A United States blockade of Iranian ports remains in effect, though the strait has largely reopened. Oil prices jumped and stocks fell after Thursday's post, as traders read it as a signal that a negotiated settlement had moved further away.

Staying with election news, state senator Cindy Holscher has won the race for governor of Kansas. The Associated Press projected her victory on Thursday evening, two days after the vote. Holscher defeated Ty Masterson, the president of the State Senate, by fifty point three percent to forty-nine point seven percent. She campaigned on halting the construction of new data centers in the state, a pledge that resonated in farming communities wary of industrial development. Masterson built his campaign on property tax cuts, and Republicans outraised and outspent her throughout the race. The result keeps the executive mansion in Democratic hands. Holscher will succeed term-limited Democratic governor Laura Kelly. Kansas voted for President Trump by sixteen points in twenty twenty-four, and Republicans had expected to hold the office. In the state's other marquee contest, Republican Adam Hamilton won a United States Senate seat last carried by a Kansas Democrat in nineteen thirty-two. Democrats had already captured a majority of American governorships for the first time in two decades before the Kansas race was called.

Also on Capitol Hill, Maryland senator Chris Van Hollen announced Thursday that he will challenge Chuck Schumer. The contest is for the post of Senate majority leader, when Democrats take the chamber in January. Van Hollen claims the support of more than ten senators. He argued that this year's Democratic sweep, which gave the party both chambers of Congress, was a mandate to change how the party is led. Schumer, party leader since twenty seventeen, says he has the votes to stay. He has the announced backing of nine colleagues, including independent senator Angus King of Maine and senator-elect Sherrod Brown of Ohio. Six senators-elect set to join the caucus in January have opposed him. Senator Bernie Sanders withdrew his support for Schumer on November eleventh. Van Hollen acknowledged he is the underdog. The leadership vote is by secret ballot of the Democratic caucus and is expected in December, before the new Congress convenes.

In business news, Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of Nvidia and stepped down from its board early Thursday. The board asked chief financial officer Colette Kress to serve as interim chief executive while it searches for a successor. Shares fell more than sixteen percent in New York on Thursday, wiping out more than six hundred billion dollars in market value. That was the chip maker's worst day since the pandemic-era lows of twenty twenty. The stock slid a further four percent in premarket trading Friday, while competitors Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom gained five percent. Huang co-founded the company in nineteen ninety-three and built it into the most valuable corporation in the world, worth near four point three trillion dollars before the fall. A shareholder lawsuit was filed in California on November tenth, alleging the company misled investors about risks tied to his personal conduct. President Trump said this month that the Federal Bureau of Investigation was examining Huang's ties to a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. That suspect runs a Singapore firm that moved restricted Nvidia chips toward China. Trump, who had attacked Huang daily, floated Apple chief Tim Cook as a possible successor. He said the departing chief executive was going through hell. The board said the search for a permanent successor will be conducted with an external firm.

In other news, a major technology outage hit Microsoft services around the world. The fault began with Teams in North America at about ten forty in the morning Eastern time on Thursday. It spread to Exchange Online mailboxes in the Americas by early afternoon, and later affected users in every region. Microsoft said a large number of its servers became unexpectedly unavailable, and that the resulting load overwhelmed a process that establishes secure connections. The company rolled back a recent network configuration change to relieve the strain, but said some restored servers became unresponsive again. Outlook on the web, on the desktop and on mobile devices was affected. Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive and the Copilot assistant also degraded. Microsoft said it was not aware of malicious activity, and had not identified the root cause by Friday. Security firms warned that phishing campaigns exploiting the outage had begun circulating. The last major failure came in September, when a fault in a core authentication configuration left mail and collaboration services down for days.

From Microsoft to Google, the search giant released Gemini four Hydrogen on Thursday, a model tuned for search. It becomes the default in Google's artificial-intelligence search mode, and answers the most complex queries in the overview answers at the top of results. The model parses multi-part questions, tolerates typos and vague phrasing, and fans out into a larger number of subqueries, producing more source links than its predecessors. Answers carry more images, tables, graphs and maps. Google said the model runs two point five times faster than Gemini three point eight Flash. On an independent artificial-intelligence benchmark, it beat Gemini four Argon, a larger model, at the default thinking setting. On a separate benchmark of multi-step tasks, the model ranked fifth. Koray Kavukcuoglu, chief technology officer of Google DeepMind, said the model was built for agentic execution, designed to reason across complex goals and complete tasks end to end.

Also from Google, the company said its first smart glasses running the Android extended-reality platform go on sale December tenth, and preorders are open. The frames were developed with Samsung, Warby Parker and Gentle Monster. Warby Parker frames start at three hundred ninety-five dollars, and Gentle Monster frames at four hundred forty-nine dollars. The eyewear carries no display. It is built around the Gemini assistant. The frames carry speakers, four microphones and a camera on the left temple. A small light signals when the camera is recording. The frames weigh about forty grams. Google is pitching privacy as the headline feature, an answer to the objections that ended Google Glass in twenty fifteen. Camera imagery is not saved to users' Gemini history, and the glasses detect tampering and block capture if the recording light is covered. Before imagery is sent to Gemini, the faces and body parts of bystanders are obscured. Privacy advocates were not persuaded. The company has opened showrooms in New York and other cities.

That was his ninety-second international goal, in a record one hundred twenty-sixth appearance for his country. England's manager Thomas Tuchel told British television that the team took a while to grow into the game but settled after a quarter of an hour. He called it a perfect evening for Kane. Spain beat the Czech Republic two-nothing on Thursday and lead the group three points above England. Spain need only a draw in Sunday's meeting to finish first. A win would take England to the top of the group on goal difference.

Meanwhile, in California tennis, Jessica Pegula beat Linda Noskova in three sets on Thursday. The score was seven-six, four-six and seven-five. The match came at the Women's Tennis Association's season-ending finals in Indian Wells. Pegula saved a match point in the deciding set of a contest that lasted two hours and forty-five minutes. Noskova had gone a break up in the third set and held match point on Pegula's serve at five-four. Pegula held, broke back and closed out one of the matches of the year. She served a career-high sixteen aces. Noskova, the Wimbledon champion, committed a career-high fifteen double faults. The win sent Pegula into the semifinals and eliminated Coco Gauff, who had beaten Marta Kostyuk earlier on Thursday. Pegula plays French Open champion Mirra Andreeva in Saturday's semifinals, their first meeting.

In entertainment, the Writers Guild of America posted official credits for Avengers Doomsday on Thursday. The guild named Stephen McFeely and Michael Waldron as the film's writers, and credited ten others with additional literary material. Among those credited are Ryan Reynolds and Hugh Jackman. For Jackman it is his first writing credit from the guild. The listing of the two actors' names lent weight to industry speculation that their characters, Deadpool and Wolverine, will appear in the film. Reynolds appeared in character at San Diego Comic-Con in July, and Jackman at Disney's fan event in August. Marvel has never confirmed the duo's involvement. The Playlist judged the actors' contributions, likely drawn largely from improvisation, sufficient to earn guild credit under the union's arbitration process. The film introduces Robert Downey Junior as Doctor Doom. Marvel has not commented on the credits. Avengers Doomsday is scheduled for release on December eighteenth.

And to wrap up today's show, critics published reviews on Thursday for Ebenezer, Paramount Pictures' adaptation of Charles Dickens's eighteen forty-three novella A Christmas Carol. The film was directed by Ti West, with Johnny Depp as Ebenezer Scrooge. The prevailing verdict was that the film is faithful to a fault. The Playlist called it a punishingly dull and wholly unnecessary Dickens adaptation. IndieWire's review said Depp was absolutely insufferable in a dull and dreary retelling, while conceding the film was solid and skillfully crafted. SlashFilm found a passionless retelling in which Depp neither helps nor hurts. The film keeps the novella's structure, dividing itself into five staves and retaining much of Dickens's dialogue. It adds horror-flavored touches around the hauntings, and Ian McKellen appears as Jacob Marley. Paramount's marketing promised a Scrooge unlike previous screen versions. The reviews suggest the central journey from miser to penitent follows the familiar path.
