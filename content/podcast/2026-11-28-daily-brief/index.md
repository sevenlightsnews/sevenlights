2|Our first story takes us to Ottawa, where the Supreme Court of Canada has largely upheld Quebec's ban on conspicuous religious symbols for public-sector workers in positions of authority. The ruling came by six judges to one. It confirms the province's use of the notwithstanding clause to shield the law, known as Bill twenty-one, from charter challenges. The law was passed in twenty nineteen. It bars teachers, police officers, lawyers and judges from wearing religious items at work. Those items include the hijab, the turban, the kippah and a conspicuous cross. The court struck down one narrow provision covering legislators whose faith requires a face covering. Critics reacted sharply. The head of the National Council of Canadian Muslims called the decision an unrestricted licence for governments to take away fundamental freedoms. Supporters in Quebec saw vindication of the province's secular tradition. The case was among the most heavily litigated in the court's one hundred fifty-one year history, with fifty-one interveners taking part. The judgment may also complicate Prime Minister Mark Carney's effort to show a united front as Canada faces a trade war with the United States.

3|Also in world news, an authorized thirty-minute documentary titled Heal airs on the British Broadcasting Corporation on Sunday. It gives the most detailed public account yet of the Princess of Wales's cancer fight. The film follows the illness she announced in March twenty twenty-four, after major abdominal surgery. The princess, forty-four, says the film is meant to reframe how illness is understood, presenting recovery as a process rather than a single outcome. It intercuts her interview with visits to hospitals, patient support groups, and art-therapy sessions. The longest sequence follows the Three Peaks challenge she undertook in June, an attempt to climb the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within twenty-four hours. The climb raised funds for the Royal Marsden hospital, where she was treated. The film also addresses the effect of the illness on her three children, and includes recorded voice messages from them. She announced in January twenty twenty-five that she was in remission, and has since returned to a full schedule of royal engagements. The broadcaster says the programme will air without commercial interruption.

Her office directed inquiries to the family's wishes for privacy.

8|In business news, the budget airline Norse Atlantic Airways filed for bankruptcy in a Norwegian court Friday, ending its five-year attempt to build a low-cost transatlantic network.
