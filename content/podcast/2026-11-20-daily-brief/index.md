2|Our first story takes us to The Hague, where the International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar's security forces committed genocide against the Rohingya minority. The twenty seventeen campaign killed about ten thousand people and drove more than seven hundred thousand across the border into Bangladesh. It is the first time the United Nations' top court has held a state responsible for genocide.
The case was brought in twenty nineteen by The Gambia under the Genocide Convention, on behalf of the stateless minority. There were tears in the courtroom. In the refugee camps of Cox's Bazar, the verdict was translated into the Rohingya language for crowds gathered before specially installed screens. Many held signs reading "Thank You Gambia."
The court ordered Myanmar to allow the safe and dignified return of displaced Rohingya, and to pay compensation, with the terms set later. The orders are legally binding, but the court has no power to enforce them.
Myanmar argued its campaign in Rakhine state was a legitimate response to guerrilla attacks. Survivors described massacres, widespread rape, arson and looting. The ruling arrives amid continued violence after the twenty twenty-one military coup. Australia, which has pledged more than one billion dollars in aid since twenty seventeen, welcomed the decision as long-overdue recognition.

3|Turning now to a story out of Ohio that touched a global audience. Fiona, the nine-year-old Nile hippopotamus whose premature birth at the Cincinnati Zoo in twenty seventeen made her famous, died Thursday after swallowing a knitted hat. The zoo announced that veterinarians could not remove it.
A child at the zoo threw the cap into the air. It struck a ceiling fan and fell through an opening into her enclosure. Fiona ate it and showed no symptoms at first. Days later her appetite failed. She spent about eleven days under round-the-clock care.

The team borrowed a vibration plate from a horse farm, hoping the shaking would push the object along. Surgery was ruled out because she weighed in the region of twenty-five hundred pounds.
Fiona was born six weeks premature, weighing twenty-nine pounds, then the lowest recorded birth weight for her species. She was the first Nile hippopotamus born at the zoo in seventy-five years. Too weak to stand, she was raised by hand. Daily photographs and videos won her an international audience and a children's book series. Her name came from the hippopotamus in "Shrek."
Visitors left flowers at her statue within an hour of the announcement. Cincinnati's mayor called her a symbol of perseverance and joy.
